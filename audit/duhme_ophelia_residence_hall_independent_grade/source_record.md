# Independent source review: Duhme (Ophelia) Residence Hall

Review start date: 2026-10-26. Scope: independently confirm building identity, plan footprint and coordinates, supported vertical/architectural facts, and identify limits before grading the builder's saved candidate. The candidate has not yet been evaluated; the source basis below is independent of the builder's audit folder.

## Primary Purdue sources

1. **Purdue Libraries Maps & GIS, Purdue/campus MapServer, layer 0 (`PuGeoDB.DBO.CampusBuilding`), OBJECTID 229, buildingidlong 351, ABBR DUHM.** Query: `https://mapsweb.lib.purdue.edu/arcgis/rest/services/Purdue/campus/MapServer/0/query?where=OBJECTID%3D229&outFields=%2A&returnGeometry=true&outSR=4326&f=pjson`. Retrieved 2026-10-26. Layer's native spatial reference is EPSG:3857; query output is EPSG:4326. The saved raw response is `purdue_historic_campus_building_229.json`. The service's temporal extent ends in 2015, so the plan outline may predate later renovations. The exact feature identifies Ophelia Duhme Residence Hall at 209 N. Russell Street.
2. **Purdue Campus Facilities and Buildings Historic Database**, record 351: `https://collections.lib.purdue.edu/campus/buildings/351`. The record describes a four-story brick, stone and reinforced concrete structure, a central portion stated as 42 x 32 ft and two wings each 42 x 109 ft, with a fireproof roof. This same historical text is embedded in the MapServer feature description; treat those as a shared underlying historical record, not two independent confirmations. These dimensions describe documented portions but do not by themselves settle which side of each segment is 42 vs 32/109 ft or the finished roof envelope.
3. **Purdue Office of Emergency Preparedness, Duhme building plan**, `https://www.purdue.edu/operations/emergency-preparedness/duhm/`. Current page lists address 209 N Russell St, four stories, a ground floor and partial basement, and 84 student rooms. It is a current facilities source for identity and story/occupancy context, but provides no measured exterior height, elevations, or plan. Its room count differs from the older historic record's 73-room description, so neither count is used as a geometry measurement.
4. **Purdue University Libraries, Karnes Archives and Special Collections, Purdue Buildings and Campus Scenes, Duhme Hall Exterior**, `https://earchives.lib.purdue.edu/digital/collection/pbuildings/id/1107/` (digital image URL: `https://earchives.lib.purdue.edu/digital/api/singleitem/image/pbuildings/1107/default.jpg`). The image record identifies Purdue image PPBUC01766, original capture date 2004-06-24, Nikon D1X, University News Service credit. This is the most useful attributable exterior reference for facade/roof/entry form. Its single viewpoint does not establish all elevations, exact dimensions, or present-day changes.
5. **Purdue official campus map PDF**, `https://www.purdue.edu/campus_map/graphics/campusmap.pdf`, identifies DUHM as Duhme (Ophelia) Residence Hall in grid E7. This corroborates campus identity and broad location, not footprint geometry.

## Independent coordinate and outline measurement

The Purdue layer query returned one closed outer ring (53 coordinates) with no holes. Output was requested in WGS84/EPSG:4326. I converted every vertex to the project's local campus XY with the rule in `modular/AGENTS.md` (origin latitude 40.4274, longitude -86.9137): X = (longitude + 86.9137) * 111412.84 * cos(radians(40.4274)); Y = (latitude - 40.4274) * 111040. Result: X [-628.8512, -561.5163] m; Y [-219.1116, -184.0809] m; outer-ring shoelace area 1,051.62 m²; shoelace centroid X -595.0409, Y -202.4389 m. Detailed calculations and source attributes are in `independent_gis_measurements.json`. This is a planimetric archival/GIS footprint, not a survey or guaranteed current wall line; roof-edge digitization and historic edits may explain small differences.

## What can and cannot be graded from these sources

Identity, approximate placement/orientation, the major wing-and-center plan, four-story massing, brick/stone/concrete palette, and pitched/fireproof roof character have source support. Treat exact eave/ridge height, floor-to-floor height, precise facade bay spacing, rear elevations, interior floor plans and room layout as unverified unless the candidate package or a separate source supplies evidence. The rendering can demonstrate visual resemblance; it cannot upgrade estimated dimensions to measured facts.

## Direct image inspection

I opened the Purdue e-Archives image directly on 2026-10-26. The view shows the characteristic V-plan wings behind a prominent central gable; a tall projecting multi-floor bay window with pale stone bands above a recessed arched entrance; dark reddish-brown brick; steep dark roof planes with masonry chimneys; narrow, vertically proportioned windows; and restrained stone trim. Trees partly obscure the lower facade. These are visual observations from the documented 2004 image, not measured geometry.
