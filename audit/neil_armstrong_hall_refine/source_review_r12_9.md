# Neil Armstrong Hall — R12.9 design evidence and limits

## Roof form

The 2023 Indiana orthophoto remains the source for the canopy/eaves plan only. It does not verify the structural wall footprint, the atrium boundary, or the wall-to-eaves offset. The 2016 Purdue Engineering/Wikimedia photographs show a localized, folded triangular wing silhouette above the large glazed entrance. R12.9 shifts the photo-based maximum to the north-west traced corner and tapers it along the two adjacent roof edges. This is a photo-derived shape hypothesis; camera headings/roll and roof elevations are unknown. The 21.5 m peak remains below the coarse 21.6 m LiDAR ceiling, which is not a measured roof height.

## Curtain wall and sunshades

The 2016 images show a large multi-story glazed atrium zone with repeated aluminum framing and projecting horizontal members. Kawneer's [Neil Armstrong Hall project page](https://www.kawneer.ca/projects/education/neil-armstrong-hall-of-engineering-purdue-university/) identifies the 1600UT curtain-wall system and Versoleil sunshade/outrigger system. The page provides product/system context and project photography, not a measured facade schedule. FRP's [project description](https://frpinc.com/projects/neil-armstrong-hall-of-engineering/) supports the cantilevered steel roof truss and bridges across the atrium at two levels; it does not give their dimensions or local positions.

R12.9 expands the existing candidate upper-wing window field from z=15.15–18.85 m to z=12.35–18.85 m, adds vertical mullions and a middle transom, and adds 86 aluminum outriggers aligned to the modeled curtain-wall bays/transom levels. The curtain-wall direction and cadence are qualitative photo interpretations. They are not presented as an as-built facade schedule.
