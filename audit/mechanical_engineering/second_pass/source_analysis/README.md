# Mechanical Engineering: roof-height evidence support, November 3, 2026

The retained April 20, 2018 Indiana LAZ crops were transformed from their parsed source CRS to WGS84 and then into the repository approximate geographic frame. Asset-local coordinates subtract the existing placement (66.30000114440918, 86.5 m). Neither the placement nor the asset was changed by this analysis.

`analyze_surface.py` produces a one-metre candidate upper-surface grid from 64,766 unclassified class-1 points. The pooled class-2 ground median is 622.56 US survey feet NAVD88. Heights use 0.30480060960121924 m per US survey foot. Source paths, hashes, capture date, units, method and limitations are in `provenance.json`. The CSV preserves p10/p50/p75/p90/p99 and counts; the PNG shows p75. `class1_asset_local_xyz.npy` retains transformed points for follow-up plane analysis.

`zone_statistics.py` uses the modeler's approximate manually registered wing polygons, saved in `zone_statistics.json`. All-return height p50/p75/p90 are approximately 17.75/18.86/18.91 m for the 1932 bar, 12.88/13.95/14.58 m for the 1929 zone, 7.72/7.85/8.33 m for the 1942 zone, and 17.61/20.02/21.64 m for the broad Gatewood bounding region. Gatewood includes several roof levels and the bounding region includes non-roof areas. These statistics support substantial wing height differences and contradict treating the entire complex as a uniform slab.

These are distributions of candidate surfaces, not surveyed eave, wall, ridge or floor heights. Class 1 includes roofs, equipment, vegetation and other objects. The manual regions can cross wing boundaries; percentile tails can represent adjoining walls, vegetation or equipment. A threshold above 8 m is included only as a diagnostic and is inappropriate for estimating the low 1942 roof as a whole. Use spatially inset roof regions and fitted planes with aerial/photo cross-checks before selecting detailed heights. The 2018 acquisition predates the 2023–2025 renovation, and pooled crop ground is not a datum survey for each wing. No vertical field in the GIS was treated as a measurement.
