## Round-12 change and score

The top-down main-floor render now shows a more continuous center aisle between the curved seat banks, and the matching stair flight is wider. This addresses one of the round-11 interior findings. It is still an estimated width: the supplied notes say the indexed R101 text identifies a pit lift but does not expose legible aisle dimensions. The aisle should remain labeled estimated, not measured or accessibility-compliant.

The pit-lift platform and perimeter seam are new. Their source note correctly says only that R101 names a pit lift; exact position, size, and operating state were not verified. In the handoff the modeled perimeter measures 2.55 m by 18.6 m, which reads as a broad stage-front strip. The new images do not establish that footprint or make the mechanism legible as a lift. This is a weakly supported, potentially oversized feature; reduce it to a small clearly schematic marker/platform or defer its geometry until a readable plan can establish the size and location.

I raised the score by 0.1 for the more legible aisle and successful save/reopen. Those local improvements do not resolve the larger omissions.

## Remaining architectural and material gaps

### Exterior

The round-11 oblique render remains the latest exterior evidence. It shows the broad footprint and roof mass, but the long wall areas are still mostly brick fields crossed by repeated pale horizontal and vertical strips. The fly tower reads as a plain box with a flat cap. The supplied exterior view does not show convincing, source-matched facade openings, entry surrounds, side-exit stair walls, or roof and fly-tower detailing. SAH Archipedia describes red brick with limestone banding, pilasters and ornament, a curving entry canopy, secondary entrance surrounds, and stepped limestone side-exit walls. These feature families are therefore actionable, but dimensions and window cadence need inspectable photos or drawings rather than invented grids.

Brick, pale trim, roof, and glazing have distinct base colors, but their broad, repetitive treatment still reads as a schematic massing model. The seat upholstery is source-informed gray; round 12 does not change material quality. Additional surface texture alone would not fix missing facade geometry and detail.

### Interior

The center aisle is improved, and the current Purdue seat map supports three levels and curved seating blocks. However, the plan view still shows highly repetitive seat arrays, a simple blank stage, and little room-level construction. Cross-aisles, balcony transitions, backstage/stage relationships, and exit routes remain indistinct or unsupported. The pit-lift slab is not enough to establish a detailed stage interior. The current Purdue directions page describes gray cushioned seats, a wide central aisle, and a high ceiling; the round-12 aisle is closer to that description, but the view still lacks the detailed room character needed for 9/10.

## Required follow-up before 9/10

1. Replace the wide, unverified pit-lift strip with geometry supported by a readable plan, or keep a small, clearly labeled schematic callout that does not imply an established size or exact location.
2. Add evidence-led facade openings and the documented entry/exit, pilaster, fly-tower, apsidal-end, and roof details. Keep dimensions and window cadence explicitly estimated where sources do not establish them.
3. Improve the auditorium's cross-aisles, stairs, balcony transitions, stage/backstage organization, and exit/circulation legibility using readable floor-plan evidence; keep unsupported layout details identified as schematic.
4. Rework exterior material and trim depth so masonry, limestone, roof tile, metal, and glass read as distinct built surfaces rather than a repeated strip pattern.
5. Only claim a continuous Elliott–Hovde route after both sides and the connector are independently verified in a wider context view.
