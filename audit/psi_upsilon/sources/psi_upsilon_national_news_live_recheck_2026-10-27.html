Announcing the 2026 Summer Leadership Series

In an effort to help prepare undergraduate leaders as they return to campus this fall we’ve renewed our online Summer Leadership Series Program. Chapter Services staff identified 8 programs to focus on – each session will have specific officers and chairs invited and encourage to attend, so as to not overwhelm leaders, and offering advice specific to their roles. Outside of the Summer Leadership Series we’re also offering a Thesauristes Academy for Chapter Treasurers and an overview of our chapter management tool, Asana. These sessions are focused around several educational topics identified and asked for by our chapter leaders and advisors: Recruitment and Marketing, Alumni Engagement, New Member Education, Ritual and more. The topics and times can be found below, you can click on a session for a better description as well as the intended audience and Zoom information, all times are Eastern:

Tuesday, August 18th @ 5pm – Welcoming Alumni Back

Tuesday, August 18th @ 6pm – Psi U Cares

Wednesday August 19th @ 5pm – Recruitment 101

Wednesday August 19th @ 6pm – Planning a Successful Philanthropy or Service Event

Wednesday August 19th @ 7pm – Officers Academy: Getting your chapter from GOOD to GREAT

Thursday August 20th @ 5pm – Your Chapter’s Brand

Thursday August 20th @ 6pm – A Mighty Friendship: New Member Education Best Practices

Thursday August 20th @ 7pm – Ritual and Traditions

In recent years we’ve substantially redesigned our Chapter Leadership Program, driven both by necessity and by what we’ve learned about supporting chapters more effectively with modern resources. The result is a program that reaches more members, more often, than ever before — built around consistent, year-round contact rather than travel alone.

Our online education has grown considerably. We moved to the Tightrope Risk Management Program, introduced the Inclusive Practices Cultural Competency Program for New Members, adopted the Phired Up Recruitment Certification Program, and added Aboveboard for Chapter Officers, which covers running meetings, committee work, and goal setting. We also launched the Psi U Cares Program to encourage chapter members to look out for one another’s mental wellness, and created the Thesauristes Academy for Chapter Treasurers to complement the Archons Academy.

Just as important is how we stay in contact. In-person visits now work alongside regular virtual programming: monthly Town Hall meetings that connect Chapter Archons, Alumni Presidents, and Advisors to one another; Spring Roundtables devoted to the chair positions our traditional programming sometimes overlooked; and the Summer Leadership Series. We’ve also brought the project management tool Asana to our chapters, helping officers delegate and track their work, and we continue to build out those projects with resources like Summer Leadership Series recordings and Officer Introductions so chapter leaders can train on their own schedules.

That work continues this summer, as we redevelop and refresh our educational materials and manuals from the ground up and are exploring new ways to engage with these materials. Our goal is simple: every chapter officer, advisor, and alumni volunteer should have the training and support they need, when they need it — and we’ll keep building until that’s true.

182nd Convention Awards

Awarded at the 182nd Convention in Ann Arbor, MI

For a full list of criteria, past recipients, and award histories, visit our full awards page.


Garnet and Gold Award for Academic Excellence

Awarded to any chapter, provisional chapter, or owl club that has achieved a chapter GPA in excess of the all-men’s average (or all-campus average, as appropriate) for their campus across multiple semesters.

Delta, New York University

Reported GPAs of 3.841 and 3.762 — the highest reported of any chapter.

Eta, Lehigh University

Exceeded the all-men’s average in both evaluated semesters on university-reported data, by 2.4% in Fall 2025 and 3.0% in Spring 2025, when the chapter ranked 1st of 9 fraternities on campus.

Omega, University of Chicago

Reported GPAs of 3.765 and 3.696 across the evaluation period.

Phi, University of Michigan

University-reported GPA above the all-men’s average in both semesters on file, with self-reported chapter GPAs running roughly 3% above the campus benchmark and exceeding it in every semester on record.

Phi Beta, The College of William & Mary

Above the all-men’s average both semesters per university-reported data, by 3.0% and 3.4%.

Psi, Hamilton College

Reported GPAs of 3.701 and 3.415 across the evaluation period.

Theta Theta, University of Washington

Exceeded the campus all-men’s average in both semesters of the evaluation period, by about 2%.

Upsilon, University of Rochester

Above the all-men’s average both semesters, including a margin of 13.6% in Spring 2025 — the largest single-semester gap of any chapter.

Xi, Wesleyan University

Consistent GPAs of 3.683 and 3.684 across the evaluation period.

Zeta, Dartmouth College

Roughly 1% above the all-men’s average in both reported semesters on university-reported data.

Owl Award for Exceptional Academic Performance

The Owl Award for Exceptional Academic Performance recognizes chapters demonstrating truly significant academic achievement, judged on campus rank, the margin by which the chapter GPA exceeds the all-men’s average, GPA improvement, the GPA itself, and other significant factors.

Eta, Lehigh University

Ranked 1st among all fraternities at Lehigh in Spring 2025 — the top rank among chapters with verifiable data — while sustaining margins of 2.4% to 3.0% above the all-men’s average at a chapter of 64 members.

Phi Beta, The College of William & Mary

Climbed from 13th of 15 to 4th of 18 on campus with a 6.1% year-over-year GPA gain — the most dramatic rank improvement and the largest confirmed gain of any chapter.

Clasped Hand Award for Outstanding Philanthropy & Service

The Clasped Hand Award for Outstanding Philanthropy and Service is presented to chapters that have devoted significant time and effort to philanthropic efforts and community service, going above and beyond in their communities. During the 2025-26 Academic Year Psi Upsilon chapters performed more than 9,400 hours of community service and raised over $121,000 for charitable causes.


Alpha Omicron, New Jersey Institute of Technology

Contributed 487 service hours, or 24.3 hours per brother — the second-highest per-brother figure reported — with a sustained focus on the American Foundation for Suicide Prevention from a chapter of just 20 members.

Eta, Lehigh University

Contributed 2,387 service hours — the most of any chapter and an average of 37.3 hours per brother — across more than 35 distinct events.

Lambda Sigma, Pepperdine University

Raised $35,000 through its Psi U Think You Can Dance event for Conquering Paralysis — $777.78 per brother, the highest per-brother fundraising figure reported.

Phi Nu, Christopher Newport University

Contributed 501 service hours (17.3 per brother) led by American Foundation for Suicide Prevention programming — a notable achievement in the chapter’s first spring back following reactivation.

Pi, Syracuse University

Strong on both dimensions with 996 service hours and $15,398 raised; its Movember campaign alone raised $10,000 and contributed to one of the largest Movember campaigns in the world, which is led by a group of Pi alumni.

Tau Epsilon, Clemson University

Raised $41,934 — the most of any chapter — anchored by its Safe Harbor event for domestic violence awareness.

Award of Distinction

The Award of Distinction was established by the 142nd Convention in 1985 to recognize Psi Upsilon chapters when they perform in an extraordinary manner. The award may be given annually to one or more chapters for any project, service, program, activity, or accomplishment performed during the year, which merits special recognition.


Psi, Hamilton College

For its annual Greek Society Speaker Series on mental health and addiction — a clinically facilitated, alumni-funded program that extended Psi Upsilon’s wellness values to the broader Hamilton Greek community.

Xi, Wesleyan University

For a multi-year, multi-million-dollar capital campaign to restore and preserve its historic chapter house, undertaken alongside a digitization of the chapter’s archives in partnership with university archivists.

Diamond Award for Exceptional Chapters

This award recognizes chapters that distinguish themselves by exceeding the Psi Upsilon Fraternity chapter standards and is the top award a chapter can receive.


Eta, Lehigh University

The Eta Chapter was the most comprehensively excellent chapter of this cycle, excelling simultaneously across academics, service, recruitment, brotherhood, alumni engagement, and member wellness — and doing so at scale with 64 active brothers. Eta finished 1st among all fraternities at Lehigh academically while exceeding the all-men’s average in every evaluated semester, and contributed 2,387 hours of community service, more than any other chapter of Psi Upsilon, across more than 35 distinct events. For sustaining that breadth of achievement without sacrificing depth in any area of chapter life, the 182nd Convention proudly awards Eta Chapter the Diamond Award for Exceptional Chapters.

2025 – 26 Year in Review

The 2025–26 academic year was a strong one for Psi Upsilon. Across our chapters, brothers lived out the Fraternity’s core values — Lifelong Friendship, Moral Leadership, Intellectual Engagement, Responsible Social Conduct, and Service to Society — through scholarship, brotherhood, alumni relations, and service to their campuses and communities, while several chapters rebuilt, grew, or returned to campus after periods of dormancy. The summaries that follow highlight notable accomplishments from each chapter that submitted a report this year, drawn from the full chapter reports collected for 2025–26.

This roundup includes only those chapters that submitted a News and Service Report by the July 1 deadline. Chapters that did not submit a report by that date are not represented here, and their accomplishments this year may go unrecognized in this summary as a result.

Collectively, Psi Upsilon chapters performed 9,458 hours of community service and raised over $121,000 for charitable causes this year, while initiating 454 new members into the Fraternity. The year also marked the return of two chapters to active campus life: Beta Beta at Trinity College moved back into its chapter house after two years of inactivity, and Phi Nu at Christopher Newport University completed its first full year back on campus as well, welcoming its first new member class since returning.

DELTA, New York University (1837)

Several brothers earned Dean’s List honors this year as the Delta Chapter leaned into intellectual engagement, reserving study space at Bobst Library daily. Recruitment brought in nine new pledges in the fall and five in the spring through events like Dinner with Brothers, and brothers deepened their lifelong friendships with alumni at their retreat, Yule Log and Delta Day events.

Service stayed a priority despite tightening restrictions on fundraising at NYU: brothers logged over 200 hours per semester through the Upperdeck mini-golf event, Circle K bag-decorating and dog-toy drives, and the Tzu-Chi food pantry. Individually, Ian Lin was accepted to dental school after volunteering with a mobile dental clinic, and Erica Zhang competed with the NYU volleyball team.

PSI, Hamilton College (1843)

A visit from therapist Stuart Warren headlined the Psi Chapter’s annual Greek Societies Speaker Series this fall, an event sponsored by their alumni and open to all students at Hamilton College. They also saw 12 new brothers initiated in the Fall. The chapter counted 21 varsity athletes and three team captains, and John Young was twice named NESCAC Defensive Player of the Week for football.

Beyond athletics, the chapter put service into practice through a weekly commitment to the Harvest food rescue initiative, moving over 50 pounds of food to local charities each week, and hosted its annual Walk Out of the Darkness event for mental health awareness.

XI, Wesleyan University (1843)

From a rush pool of over 200 applicants, the Xi Chapter welcomed 13 new brothers this Spring — including a Freeman Scholar, two QuestBridge Scholars, and a varsity athlete — even while managing a full renovation of the chapter house’s fire system that required a rotating 24/7 fire watch for two months. In a mark of intellectual engagement, the chapter also piloted a new Librarian position to begin digitizing its 18th-century archive, and Wesleyan’s president attended a Homecoming event at the house.

Brothers put service into action with a weekly presence at St. Vincent de Paul’s soup kitchen and warming shelter through a record-cold winter. On the athletic and academic side, Zach Idinopolus captained the varsity squash team, Tori Rideau-Winds earned a second All-NESCAC selection, and Juliette Sullivan qualified for the New England D3 Indoor Championship in track.

UPSILON, University of Rochester (1858)

Wilmot Warrior Weekend was the Upsilon Chapter’s signature act of service, raising nearly $2,000 in the fall and again in the spring for cancer research at the Wilmot Cancer Institute, with brothers running, biking, and staffing the event. President Yuriy Sandmeier was inducted into the Order of Omega in recognition of his moral leadership, while also helping his club tennis team reach USTA sectionals.

Swimmer Ben Friedberg had a standout year, breaking the school’s nine-year-old 50-yard freestyle record and anchoring a program-record 200 medley relay that ranked among the top 70 in NCAA Division III.

PHI, University of Michigan (1865)

Soccer and flag football teams both reached the fraternity league championship game this fall for the Phi Chapter, which also posted a 3.543 GPA — above both the IFC and all-men’s averages — brought in an eight-man pledge class, and seated a new executive board.

Its annual Pie Upsilon philanthropy event raised $690 toward Winterfest, benefiting the Autism Alliance of Michigan, the Fisher House Foundation, and the Women’s Center for Southeastern Michigan.

OMEGA, University of Chicago (1869)

Archon Sammy Pichler closed out a term defined by moral leadership for the Omega Chapter, whose eleven prospective new members were immediately active in service — helping organize a memorial event, held jointly with Alpha Omicron Pi, that raised thousands of dollars for PAWS Chicago in honor of a UChicago student who had passed away. Brothers also supported a Hyde Park dance studio’s year-end performance and a campus food drive.

Will Foley and Max Rivera strengthened alumni outreach over the course of the year and began fundraising for a new back-lot project, laying groundwork for the chapter’s long-term growth.

PI, Syracuse University (1875)

Brother Trent Jamison completed treatment for lymphoma this spring and is now cancer-free — a moment the whole Pi Chapter rallied around, and a reminder of the lifelong friendship at the heart of the Fraternity. It capped an exceptional year: 25 brothers made the Dean’s List in the fall and 18 more in the spring, the chapter welcomed 10 new brothers in the fall and 14 in the spring, and philanthropy events raised over $13,000 combined for Meals on Wheels and the Movember Foundation, including a car-smash fundraiser with sister sorority Delta Gamma.

The spring semester brought a 3.35 cumulative GPA, a half-marathon completed by six brothers together, and a service Saturday at an animal shelter that logged 224 hours in a single day. Remy Guillot was elected New Member Education Chair for the campus Interfraternity Council.

BETA BETA, Trinity College (1880)

After more than a year off suspension with the chapter house vacant, brothers of the Beta Beta Chapter moved back in, welcomed longtime chefs Dan “D2” Bergeron and Danny “D3” Rosa back to the kitchen, and held officer elections that renewed the chapter’s moral leadership. A November fundraiser for men’s mental health awareness — an act of service — raised thousands of dollars, and brothers handed out candy at Halloween on Vernon Street and donated to the Stuff-a-Cruiser Christmas Toy Drive.

On the field, Nick Behrens, Patrick McCaffrey, and Ethan Krauss were named Co-NESCAC Champions with the Trinity football team, and the chapter reached the finals in both intramural basketball and flag football — a strong return to form after a difficult stretch away from the house.

ETA, Lehigh University (1884)

Mandatory study hours and a new academic big-brother program paid off for the Eta Chapter, which posted the highest new-member GPA on the Hill and saw an 18% jump in brothers named to the Dean’s List. By spring, 32% of the chapter had made the Dean’s List with a 3.6 GPA or higher, with no members on academic probation. President Cullen Mangan conducted DNA-repair research at Laverty Lab, and the chapter raised roughly $900 for Active Minds and mental-health causes in the fall.

Spring brought sustained service — 434 combined hours at the Lehigh Valley Humane Society, a Polar Plunge that raised $500 for Special Olympics, and a dance-competition win with Kappa Delta that raised $650 for Active Minds. Senior Jake Feuerstein presented his research at the American Public Health Association’s annual meeting, and Connor McDowell received the James J. Duane III Student Life Leadership Award.

TAU, University of Pennsylvania (1891)

Philanthropy chair Samuel Pañeda helped organize a 5K for the CASA Foundation ($1,000) and a coffeehouse fundraiser with the Friars Senior Honor Society that raised over $2,300 this fall, part of a year that also saw the Tau Chapter post a 3.70 GPA and win the intramural soccer championship. In the spring, the chapter’s Walk 27L Fashion Show was projected to raise over $2,600, and brothers Axel Mattson, Mikey Himes, Beto Valenzuela, and Daniel Bauer completed the IRONMAN 70.3 Texas.

The chapter also named its first Health and Wellness chair to promote responsible social conduct and regular check-ins among brothers. Several brothers received Psi Upsilon Foundation Scholarships, Mikey Himes was named a Psi Upsilon Outstanding Junior, and Alexander Dartnell was named MVP of the Ivy League squash tournament.

THETA THETA, University of Washington (1916)

The Theta Theta Chapter took part in four philanthropy events this quarter, kept a 3.53 average GPA, and brought in a six-man winter pledge class — well above current Greek recruitment trends. Brother Samuel Hartin Pasco raised over $1,000 for Alpha Gamma Delta’s Mr. Greek philanthropy, and the chapter logged required volunteer hours with Seattle Rotary. The chapter’s Health and Wellness chair, Colin Xie, held two required check-in meetings per quarter with every brother, and more for those who needed them.

ZETA ZETA, University of British Columbia (1935)

Despite a first-semester social ban, the Zeta Zeta Chapter posted one of its strongest years yet, welcoming 22 fall pledges and 9 spring pledges and initiating 20 of the fall class along with all nine in the spring — a testament to the strength of its brotherhood. The chapter’s “Psi U Think You Can Score?” charity soccer tournament raised over $700 for Breast Cancer Research Canada, and Formal 2026 — a two-day trip to Victoria — was the largest formal in the village.

Brothers logged over 220 service hours across the year and introduced new internal traditions, including an 8-week gym transformation challenge and a “Brother of the Semester” award, to rebuild social momentum after the ban.

EPSILON NU, Michigan State University (1943)

A LUG Hockey team started by brothers became one of the league’s top teams this year, part of a standout run for the Epsilon Nu Chapter that also included a national Greek Basketball Association ranking and one of the largest rushes on campus — over 350 potential members and 40 bids extended — while holding a 3.2 chapter GPA. The chapter also raised about $3,500 combined through a Landshark philanthropy event and a cancer-research fundraiser.

Spring rush drew 119 potential members and yielded 17 new brothers, and philanthropy stayed strong: bar nights raised $2,300 for Lansing Youth Sports Network, Winterfest raised $750 for the Brendan Davis Memorial Foundation, and a charity lacrosse game with Sigma Alpha Epsilon raised $600 for brain cancer research.

GAMMA TAU, Georgia Institute of Technology (1970)

Keshav Parthasarathy’s senior capstone, “Bike Buddy,” won Best Electrical and Computer Engineering Capstone Project this year, one of several marks of intellectual engagement in the Gamma Tau Chapter, and Asher Gannon co-published a paper through an ACM student design competition. A live-music fundraiser at the chapter house also raised $405 for Mosaic Georgia, and individual brothers logged extensive volunteer hours, from feeding patients at the Shepherd Center to removing invasive species and cleaning up the Atlanta Beltline.

Twelve brothers graduated this spring, several with faculty honors, and the chapter’s semiannual Philanthropy Show — featuring a band with over 43,000 monthly Spotify listeners — raised $711 for Refuge Coffee, which supports Atlanta’s immigrant and refugee community.

EPSILON IOTA, Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute (1982)

A new Casino Night became an instant hit for the Epsilon Iota Chapter this year, part of a rush season that drew 30 unique attendees — more than triple the chapter’s size — and welcomed two new brothers. Brothers also reworked the chapter house basement and hosted a well-attended alumni game show, strengthening lifelong friendships across the house.

Individually, Brother Li crocheted more than 25 hats, scarves, and blankets for a local organization supporting children with autism, a quiet act of service that the organization then sold to fund its work. Two brothers also took on leadership roles in RPI’s newly founded Student Transgender Association, providing moral leadership in support of a safe campus space.

PHI BETA, William & Mary (1984)

A record new-member class of nine brothers joined the Phi Beta Chapter in the fall, when twelve brothers also made the Dean’s List and every brother volunteered at least once at the Community Growers farm for a combined 57 hours of service. The chapter closed the spring 4th of 16 fraternities academically with a 3.557 GPA, and Landen Wright secured a first-ever homecoming partnership with music sorority Nu Kappa Epsilon along with funding for an English Honors Thesis.

Jack Nystrom won the chapter’s Outstanding Junior Award in recognition of his moral leadership, James McCormack received the intramural department’s Supervisor of the Year Award, and Joshua Pomeroy placed 3rd at the Commonwealth Cyber Fusion cybersecurity competition. Brothers also raised money for Habitat for Humanity through a Pie-a-Brother event and bake sale, and volunteered at Newport News repair blitzes and the local Humane Society.

PHI DELTA, University of Mary Washington (1996)

No brother finished either semester below a 3.0 as the Phi Delta Chapter made intellectual engagement its centerpiece, with 56% on the fall Dean’s List and 55% in the spring at a 3.5 chapter average. Career milestones followed suit — three brothers landed internships, one was accepted to Marine Officer Candidate School, and another was offered a full-time Foreign Service Officer position with the State Department.

After acknowledging a gap in service during the fall, the chapter rebounded in the spring with an average of 10.5 service hours per member, grew alumni support enough to help cover initiation fees and revive scholarships, and kept traditions of lifelong friendship alive, like the alumni-vs-actives Keg Bowl flag football game.

LAMBDA SIGMA, Pepperdine University (1998)

In Malibu, the Lambda Sigma Chapter put service at the center of its year — a beach cleanup with Heal the Bay, volunteering at a Suicide Prevention Walk, installing a 9/11 memorial flag display, and supporting the Dream Center Los Angeles’s outreach to Skid Row.

The chapter’s biggest night of the year, Psi U Think You Can Dance, raised $35,000 for the Conquering Paralysis initiative of the United Spinal Association — a major contribution toward spinal cord injury research and one of the chapter’s strongest expressions of service.

ALPHA OMICRON, New Jersey Institute of Technology (1999)

Nico Giovannetti stood out for his intellectual engagement this spring — earning an American Heart Association research grant, joining NJIT’s Grace Hopper AI & Biomedicine Summer Research Program, and co-authoring a published preprint on biosensor design — capping a steady year for the Alpha Omicron Chapter. Eighteen active brothers carried the chapter through the fall, welcoming two new members and hosting its annual Phoenix Banquet for members and alumni, while several brothers made the Dean’s List.

Stephen Junger was named a Psi Upsilon Outstanding Junior in recognition of his moral leadership while serving as an RA and interning with the NYC Department of Design and Construction. The chapter’s Valentine’s Flower Fundraiser for the American Foundation for Suicide Prevention became its central act of service for the year.

SIGMA PHI, Saint Francis University (2007)

After years without one, the Sigma Phi Chapter’s alumni chair finally re-founded the chapter’s alumni association, renewing lifelong friendship across generations of brothers. The association took on the chapter’s debt, freeing it to hold events like a spring formal. The chapter also raised money through a Car Show ($750 net) and a self-built haunted mini-golf course ($200 net), and contributed to a Patch Together benefit concert that raised over $21,000 overall for families affected by tragedy.

The chapter’s spring momentum was capped by being named campus-wide “Fraternity of the Year,” winning the “Excellence in Community Involvement” award for its service, and ranking first in overall chapter assessment. Andrew Stewart was named Fraternity Man of the Year for his moral leadership, and Brandon Dick earned multiple biology honors along with acceptance to graduate school at the Virginia Institute of Marine Science.

PHI NU, Christopher Newport University (2010)

In its first fall back on campus, the Phi Nu Chapter ran a door-to-door food and supply drive — an act of service — that collected 441 pounds of goods for Thrive Peninsula, while holding a 3.2 chapter GPA and placing three brothers on the Dean’s List. Brothers also practiced responsible social conduct in everyday campus life, making a point to greet fellow students, hold doors, and carry themselves as gentlemen during the chapter’s rebuilding year. Its first spring rush since returning drew 15 accepted bids out of 15 offered.

The chapter’s American Foundation for Suicide Prevention fundraising was its biggest philanthropic effort, raising $1,455, and three brothers — Henry Gagnier, Connor Robbins, and Cristian Person — were inducted into the Order of Omega leadership honor society in recognition of their moral leadership.

2026 Outstanding Juniors Announced

The Psi Upsilon Outstanding Junior is a long standing tradition in the fraternity recognizing rising seniors who best exemplify the values of Psi Upsilon Fraternity: Lifelong Friendship, Moral Leadership, Intellectual Engagement, Responsible Social Conduct, and Service to Society.

Each chapter votes on who should receive this award and reports it to the International Office.

Many chapters began recognizing members in the Junior Class dating back to the early 1900s with this form of recognition, and would award them with “Alumni Keys”, a special version of the Psi Upsilon Badge that had a key to wind a pocket watch. In the 1980s the award was formalized by the Psi Upsilon Executive Council and Foundation and now Outstanding Juniors are recognized by the International Fraternity and receive the Alumni Key in the Fall or at the Awards Banquet as a part of the Psi Upsilon Convention.
