# Felix Haas Hall — independent review round 5

**Asset reviewed:** `modular/buildings/felix_haas_hall.blend`, reopened in Blender 5.2.3 for this review. The reopened file contains `Asset_felix_haas_hall` with 1,056 direct export objects. I inspected the round-five overall exterior, entrance close view, all four orthographic elevations, interior plan render, continuity/reopen records, and the saved Purdue Events exterior reference and Purdue CS first-floor plan.

**Overall: 6.8/10. It does not meet 9/10.** Round five improves the roof color, fills some previously bare side elevations, and makes the interior's evidence limits clearer. The reference's distinctive masonry composition is still only loosely represented, visible facade details remain surface overlays, and the interior is a schematic based on a nondimensioned plan.

## Ranked fixes

1. **Rebuild the visible entry and corner masonry pattern from the photo.** The current white bands and piers project like a frame over a plain brick box. Match the source's pale stone field, red-brick inset panels, corner treatments, and arch surrounds, and show the treatment clearly at full-building scale.
2. **Make the glazing and openings read as part of the walls.** The current overlays leave the GIS wall behind the window areas. Cut openings in the detailed facade shell or provide a convincing recessed reveal with visible jambs and interior depth; check this in an oblique view.
3. **Refine the long and end elevations from corroborated views.** Round five removes the most obvious blank planes, but the elevation renders still show large unarticulated brick zones and an estimated east window schedule. Find clearer source views or mark unresolved areas as schematic without presenting guessed window locations as established.
4. **Improve the interior's visual credibility.** Keep the Purdue plan's room names and adjacency, but reduce repeated desk placeholders, improve door/circulation readability, and soften the hard directional shadows. Continue to identify dimensions and furniture as estimates.
5. **Recheck material response in neutral daylight.** The deeper roof red is a useful correction. Tune the limestone from cool white toward the warm pale stone in the reference and check brick/stone contrast in a less dramatic lighting setup.

## Evidence and limits

- The saved Purdue Events photograph, `audit/batch3_haas/exterior_reference.jpg`, supports qualitative brick/limestone contrast, the central entry, arched lower openings, and broad roof form. Perspective does not establish metric elevations or side-window positions.
- Purdue CS's [HAAS first-floor plan](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/resources/docs/HAAS_first.pdf) supports room names and broad adjacency, not scale or exact room dimensions.
- Purdue's [historical timeline](https://collections.lib.purdue.edu/timelines/puhistorical) supports the 17-step count, not tread dimensions.
- The round-five evidence and continuity records report 1,056 export objects, unchanged GIS bounds/transforms, 17 treads, estimated added side windows, and overlay glazing. These checks support asset continuity and bookkeeping, not measured facade accuracy.

**9/10 decision:** No. The asset is more complete and better documented than round four, but the visible facade's defining masonry pattern and real wall/window depth remain substantially simplified, and interior accuracy is limited by the available nondimensioned plan.
