## Architectural features to carry into future modeling

**Northeast main entrance and street face.** The official close photo and registered 2025 panorama show three distinct paired glazed-door portals in pale rectangular stone surrounds, each with a dark rectangular transom. Separate raised family-name lettering sits above the door group, while the much larger incised **Electrical Engineering** inscription remains in the upper pale stone band. Do not substitute one for the other or place the two inscriptions at the same height.

The three central upper windows are set **lower** than the upper windows immediately to either side: brick panels occupy the space above them below the inscription band. This is a defining break in the opening rhythm, not a uniform grid. Dark frames divide the central tall windows into two columns and three rows. The shorter top-row windows have two columns and two rows in the official close photo. Main side bays differ in height and arrangement, and some lower windows contain visible louver/air-conditioning inserts. Count and register each facade separately before building; the close photograph is cropped and cannot establish the entire building's bay count.

Brick piers project slightly between the taller openings, with small pale caps/cornice blocks beneath the upper stone band. A lower horizontal stone belt separates low ground openings from the main wall above. Red/brown brick, thin mortar, dark window recesses, stone sills and deep eave shadows are all visually significant. The roof edge projects beyond the brick and has repeated brackets/rafter-like supports visible clearly in the MSKTD exterior photograph; model their depth rather than painting a flat stripe.

**Southeast return and connector.** The 2025 southeast image and the MSKTD project photo both expose three rounded lower arches in a recessed connection, tall divided glazing above, and a lower roof than the main bar. Their relationship to grade and the blank brick areas around them is distinct from the three rectangular front portals. Register this connection against the plan before assigning every visible wall to the same wing. Corner windows turn onto the return but their row heights and individual pane/louver arrangements require a separate schedule. Retain downpipes, dark recesses and the roof overhang where supported; don't infer hidden openings behind the tree.

**Northwest bridge and neighboring boundary.** The 2025 corner camera shows a glazed enclosed elevated bridge toward Mechanical Engineering, with its own roof and structure beneath. It is not an extra floor across the whole facade. Mechanical Engineering, MSEE and Potter are separate assets; resolve bridge/connector ownership and attachment points before future edits. The raw image includes neighboring walls that must not be duplicated into this building.

**Duncan/service side.** The southern 2017 reference shows large relatively blank brick fields separated by tall light stone pilasters/bands, a shallow red roof edge, service doors, a glazed raised connection and a recessed lower passage/entry. This composition differs strongly from the street window grid. The 2014 study proposes new curtain walls and an accessible connector; those proposals and the later architect photo are leads for resolving changes. Do not copy the study rendering as a real photo, preserve a 2017 blank wall as verified current condition, or assume every proposed window was built.

**Roof and massing.** The overhead shows multiple connected bars, central lower flat-roof masses/recesses, gray roof areas, reddish sloping tile edges, small penetrations and neighboring roof connections. Main wings do not all share a single uniform roof height or a simple solid rectangular roof. Cross-check actual roof polygons and courtyard voids before extracting heights. The GIS outer primary footprint and equipment feature do not separately resolve every internal lightwell, covered space or roof face. Exclude adjacent ME, MSEE and Potter roofs from the building's silhouette and point selection.

## Material/color matching

Compare the official 2025 front, the 2025 Street View set and the architect exterior under their differing lighting. Common observed materials are warm red/brown brick with pale joints, light gray/cream stone bands and portals, near-black window frames and grilles, muted red roof tile edges and dark exposed eave/soffit elements. Glazing shifts from blue reflections to almost black in shade; it is not a uniformly opaque blue material. The warm sunlit MSKTD corner is more orange than the shaded 2025 avenue facade, so one photograph's saturation is not a calibrated base color. No physical color samples or reliable absolute sRGB values are available.

Model stone/brick boundaries and projecting trim as geometry where they affect depth and shadows. Keep the carved upper inscription, raised lower lettering, door frames, transoms, arches and window/louver variations distinct. Interior banners and historic grayscale images must not drive exterior color matching.
