## Identity, period, footprint and boundaries

The queue match is Forney Hall of Chemical Engineering, FRNY, 480 Stadium Mall Drive. Purdue's School history gives the original Chemical Engineering Building a 1939 construction date. The campus GIS historical feature 485 describes its completion in 1939, while the current whole-building feature 12 records the 2002–04 addition and a 2018 change field. Purdue facilities describes the original as a 1940s structure; Purdue's 2011–12 annual report calls the older part 1940. Retain the 1939 record as the most precise dated claim, but note the 1939/1940 discrepancy rather than asserting a precise original completion day.

A new five-story addition was dedicated in October 2004 and occupancy began January 2005. Purdue Engineering facilities lists 44,687 assignable square feet for the addition, while Purdue Chemical Engineering newsletters and reports use roughly 100,000 square feet for the addition. These are source-reported program-area figures with different definitions; neither is a footprint measurement. An official 2013–14 report says the East Wing was the last original wing not yet renovated and scheduled renovation completion in fall 2016. A 2016 Purdue facilities shutdown notice associates work with the East Wing renovation, but no final exterior/as-built record was found. Do not interpret renovation timing as proof that the outer masonry changed.

The existing Purdue GIS snapshot identifies feature 12 as the current whole-building outline and feature 485 as the 1938–39 historical outline. The historic polygon is mostly inside the larger current outline, consistent with later additions but not sufficient to assign every current mass to a date. Its 85-vertex current ring, after the existing 0.4 m local RDP approximation, reduces to 22 outline points. The local approximate ring area is 3,621.9 m², while the GIS `Shape.STArea()` attribute reports 6,261.1 m². OSM way 117146497 yields about 3,718.0 m² locally and overlaps 98.8% of the GIS local polygon area; this cross-check supports a broadly similar plan outline but is not a survey and does not resolve the GIS area discrepancy. The GIS layer also reports six levels, while the visible photo impression is four main upper window rows over a lower/base zone and the official source calls the addition five-story. Level definitions and phase boundaries need a plan/elevation source.

Boundary for this research pass: the FRNY principal building only. Do not merge nearby Chemical Engineering Annex/other engineering buildings or fountain and landscape objects into this mass. The image foreground's stone fountain balustrade, carved caps, paths, lamps and plantings are independent site elements, not part of the building shell.

## Signature details, roof, massing and materials

- **Massing:** irregular connected multi-wing footprint per current Purdue GIS and corroborating OSM outline; one historical 1938–39 outline is smaller than the current whole-building boundary. The official history establishes an old building, 2004 addition, and later East Wing renovation. Available photos do not spatially separate all those phases, so no precise original/addition seam is assigned.
- **Entrance:** the visible entry has a pale stone frame with a shallow arched/peaked upper profile and a roundel above in F01. F02 resolves the broad entry position and short step/landing, but tree shadows and distance hide its profile. No measured projection, jamb/capital/arch cross-section, inscription or door-leaf count is supported.
- **Windows:** repeated tall rectangular dark glazed openings with lighter sills/heads on the photographed planes. The broad photo shows horizontal story bands and a darker lower/base opening line. No sash subdivisions can be counted reliably at this distance; no other facade family's windows are observed.
- **Roof:** F01 and F02 show only a narrow oblique roof edge, with green-colored coping/edge flashing and occasional small rooftop exhaust/vent elements visible in F01. The roof surface, pitch, ridges, internal courts, step-backs, equipment count and attachment profiles are not visible. This green edge is a photographic color observation, not a verified copper material specification. No aerial or LiDAR/roof-height dataset was retained in this pass.
- **Materials:** both photographs show red/brown brick and pale horizontal masonry bands/entry trim with dark glazing. F01's brighter winter daylight and F02's spring foliage produce different exposure/occlusion; color is qualitative only. Brick bond, stone type, joint depth, metal frame coating and roof material are unverified. Do not copy procedural material colors in the model audit as source truth.
- **Dimensions:** the GIS and OSM areas and local conversion above constrain the plan only, with a material GIS area conflict. No current surveyed dimensions, measured floor-to-floor heights, eaves, roof or parapet elevations were found. The asset's modeled wall-top and height values are not building measurements.

## Existing audit discrepancy checklist

This is a source-to-audit comparison, not a visual grade or a use of model geometry as evidence.

- The existing build notes use a 13.9 m wall top and mention an estimated 14.02 m legacy 46 ft GIS height. Neither is independently established by the inspected facade photos; keep as estimates only.
- The build notes describe six levels, but the visible photo sequence and official five-story addition description do not define a consistent whole-building level count. Resolve with dated plans/elevations.
- The build notes use a schematic roof and HVAC arrangement. The two photos show only a slim roof edge and a few distant vents; they do not support roof slope, ridges, equipment count, or placement.
- Existing entry/windows use source-informed brick/stone vocabulary, but this pass finds no reliable full-face schedule, close profile photo, or all-side comparison. Do not treat modeled repetitions or mirrored faces as confirmed.
- GIS outline/model footprint comparison in prior audit is historical/current footprint context and has an area conflict; it does not validate current elevation or every wing boundary.

## Evidence-led exterior brief

For any later model pass, retain an irregular multi-wing campus building with at least two visibly distinct construction phases: the original 1939 Chemical Engineering building and the 2002–04 five-story addition, plus the subsequently renovated East Wing. The only directly observed current-era facade family is red-brown brick with pale horizontal masonry accents, repeated tall dark rectangular windows, and an inset stone-framed entrance. A dark lower/base opening band appears on the photographed face. The roof edge is a shallow green-toned line with occasional small vents in the distance; its roof planes are unknown. The entrance and repeated openings have real depth and visible stone framing, but no profile measurements. Use the two inspected view IDs above for only their visible regions. Preserve all unknown faces, unmeasured levels, roof arrangement, phase seams and dimensions as unknown instead of filling by symmetry. The prior asset has already moved beyond the evidence in several of those areas, as the discrepancy checklist states.
