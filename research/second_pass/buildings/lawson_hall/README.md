# Richard & Patricia Lawson Computer Science Building — exterior research

**Second-pass queue rank:** 26 · **stable asset ID:** `lawson_hall` · **assigned owner:** Rossen

**Official footprint:** Purdue GIS BuildingShapesZip4, FID 59 (`LWSN`)

## 1. Identity, date and footprint

The current Purdue GIS feature names the building “Richard & Patricia Lawson Computer Science Building,” gives address 305 N University Street, owner PURDUE, and reports `BUILT=2004-09-01` and `OCCUPIED=2006-07-04` after converting its Unix-millisecond values to UTC dates. Purdue Computer Science’s March 17, 2006 construction update says the exterior was substantially complete except for trim, forecasts final completion for June 25, 2006, move-in during summer and fall classes in Lawson; Purdue’s dedication record places the ceremony on September 15, 2006. The differing date fields describe different project milestones and are not treated as contradictory completion dates. The GIS `BUILT` date is not independently defined as groundbreaking, construction start or completion.

FID 59 was queried live on 2026-10-30 and saved in `sources/live_feature_59_2026-10-30.json`. Its 54-vertex ring reprojects from Web Mercator to NAD83 / UTM zone 16N (EPSG:26916) as a ground plan polygon of approximately **32.49 m east–west by 84.77 m north–south**, **2,388.68 m²** area and **234.07 m** perimeter. Those are calculations from the current GIS ring, rounded to centimetres; they describe the plan footprint, not gross floor area or surveyed wall face. A comparison with the cached project snapshot in `sources/purdue_footprint.json` found effectively identical boundaries (IoU 0.9999999997; maximum boundary difference about 9 nanometres after reprojection), so the old location crosswalk is geometrically consistent. Raw GIS `Shape__Area=4129.9765625` and `Shape__Length=308.0528632` are Web Mercator planar aggregates and do not equal the ground-area/perimeter calculations; use the ring with a ground CRS for layout.

The north-up footprint and all three plans establish a long north–south bar with a broad curved eastern Commons frontage, a stepped/angled southern entrance zone, and a western/northwestern service and classroom/office wing. The principal public entrance occupies the southeast/south end; plan labels identify the eastern curved interior as Commons and show the south/southeast vestibule. The 2023 oblique and 2024 entrance photograph are compatible with this orientation, but their exact camera locations and bearings were not published. The aerial’s viewing/CRS extent is saved in `sources/aerial_export_wide.json`; its export is north-up. Adjacent roads and neighboring campus buildings fall outside FID 59. A footprint-neighbour query found no other saved Purdue footprint within 20 m; this says nothing about above-grade canopy, shared campus paving, utilities or undocumented connectors.

## 4. Facade families, entrances and material observations

**Long brick wing.** `W23-COMMONS`, `W06-CONSTRUCTION` and the completed-building view show red to brown masonry across the long wing. The wing has many repeated smaller punched openings, plus at least one high-level projecting multi-panel glazed bay visible in the 2023 corner view. Some vertical glazing at the corner is taller than the punched-window groups. The sources do not establish brick bond, exact brick/module dimensions, lintel/sill composition, opening cadence for the hidden elevations, or whether all high-level glazing is continuous between floors. Treat brick as a visual family, not a verified product/specification.

**Curved Commons and entrance corner.** A broad light tan/pale gray curved fascia or canopy carries raised “Lawson Computer Science Building” lettering in `W23-COMMONS`; the 2024 Purdue entrance photograph gives a closer view of the same projecting band and entry. Beneath is a glazed entrance/storefront zone with dark metal-looking frames. The images show paired entry doors, a large transom/storefront area, broad stairs, metal handrails and a side accessible ramp. The curved east Commons volume has extensive glazing in the 2023 view, though vegetation masks the lower facade and reflections mask interior/window divisions. Geometry needing depth includes the projecting curved fascia/canopy, rounded/angled vestibule, changes between brick and glazed corner, stepped approach and rail/ramp. Neither photo is a measured detail.

**Visible color/material map (photographic).** The long wing reads as red-brown brick; the entry band reads as pale tan/light stone or precast; frames read dark gray/black; glazing appears blue-gray with sky/tree reflections; railings appear metallic silver. These are appearance observations under differing daylight, not calibrated color values or confirmed material specifications. It is unresolved whether the entry band is limestone, precast concrete, architectural cast stone or a combination. Roof appears predominantly dark flat roofing in the aerial, with a lighter raised/service strip. Aerial color is not sufficient to identify membrane, metal, ballast or equipment material.

No source reviewed establishes installed window product, tint, frit, mullion dimensions, masonry bond/joint color, stone/precast specification, roof membrane, repair history, or current facade condition. The public sources support a family-level description only.

## 5. Roof, levels and dimensional constraints

The 2023-02-11 aerial (`A23-OVERHEAD`) shows a north–south main roof plate with a broad dark roof, a lighter central raised/covered strip and several stepped regions, including the southern entrance/terrace edge. The L3 plan places Tolonga Terrace at the southeast. The imagery does not reveal parapet height, slope, roof membrane, mechanical units concealed by the light strip, drainage, or exact roof-to-facade setbacks. The saved aerial crop is north-up and georeferenced by the export metadata; it is a resampled visualization, not a roof survey.

The Indiana Statewide 3DEP LiDAR tile `in2018_29951885_12` was acquired 2018-04-20. The source tile is COPC/LAZ, horizontal CRS NAD83(HARN) / Indiana West (US survey foot), vertical NAVD88 height using Geoid12B (US survey foot). Source metadata and acquisition-index record are retained. A 82,657-byte clip contains 12,465 returns from the live FID 59 bounding rectangle; 10,897 lie within the footprint polygon. It was made with a COPC bounding-box query followed by exact-ring point-in-polygon filtering for statistics. After eroding the ring by 1 m, 10,256 unclassified Class 1 returns remain. Relative to nearby Class 2 ground returns, Class 1 height-difference percentiles are p05 5.32 m, p25 14.77 m, p50 14.83 m, p75 18.43 m and p95 18.58 m. The broad, separated upper clusters indicate multiple roof/top-return levels, but **Class 1 includes façade, trees and other unclassified returns** and the low-density scan, occlusion, footprint erosion and ground baseline affect those values. These are screening indicators, not surveyed roof/eave/penthouse elevations. They do not validate the existing asset’s approximately 19 m maximum Z.

GIS `NUM_LEVELS=5`, `CountOfFLO=5`, `Elevation=621`, `Extrution=48`, `F01=32`, `F02=47.3`, `M="15.3"` and `R=62.6` are retained verbatim in the live source. Layer metadata provides field names and data types but no definitions, units, vertical datum, or calculation method for these fields. Do not interpret `Extrution=48` as feet/metres or `Elevation=621` as a roof/eave height. The L1/L2/L3 plans are north-up and graphically scaled 0–32 ft; their prior audit registration used matched corners and reports ~0.3 m raster-pick uncertainty. L2/L3 outer-line comparisons against GIS have local maxima ~0.725/~0.670 m, respectively. This constrains approximate plan placement only. No floor-to-floor heights, facade elevations, roof section, verified penthouse dimensions, mechanical schedule, or Level 4/5/basement sheets were located.

## 6. Construction brief for a future research/model phase

Preserve the current FID 59 plan footprint and its north–south orientation. Organize the primary mass as a long, mostly rectilinear brick office/teaching wing joined to a glazed, curved east-side Commons and a southeast entry/vestibule composition. Keep the south end’s angled/stepped plan, projected round vestibule, Tolonga Terrace zone at Level 3, and the north/south plan irregularities; do not simplify the building into a symmetric bar. Visually distinct treatment includes repeated small punched windows in brick, tall glazed corner/Commons zones, a projecting multi-panel high-level bay, and the light curved entrance name fascia over the doors. Steps, rails and accessible ramp are supported visually but their profiles and dimensions are unknown.

For a future builder, use the official plans only for approximate plan organization at Levels 1–3; use the 2023 overhead for roof plan and mass setbacks; use the licensed 2023 photo for the southeast corner and the official 2024 image for the entrance appearance. Treat both 2006 photographs as phase/context records. Do not copy the existing model’s heights, window cadence, roof equipment, or material parameters as evidence. Any geometry beyond the footprint, the three published plans, and the observed southeast corner needs additional registered current imagery or measured drawings.

### Existing asset/audit discrepancy checklist

- Prior independent review R8 reports the asset perimeter closely aligned to FID 59, but that is an XY fit only and does not validate any above-grade wall, facade opening, height or roof surface.
- Asset maximum Z is reported as 19.0 m; LiDAR’s high return cluster is compatible in rough scale but is not a roof survey and cannot confirm that value.
- GIS five-level fields coexist with only three available department plan sheets. Level count and floor naming/elevation remain unresolved.
- R8’s curved Commons, windows, south entry, fascia, doors, stairs and rails were acknowledged as schematic/visual approximations. This research confirms presence and approximate placement of those features, not model dimensions.
- The R8 roof notch aligns approximately with the plan-labeled third-floor terrace, but no guard, parapet, elevation or terrace-floor detail was measured.
- GIS ring edge fit, old shape area/length fields, plan raster envelope offsets, upper-level heights and roof equipment remain distinct evidence questions.
