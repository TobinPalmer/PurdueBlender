## Identity, chronology, location, and source limits

Purdue BuildingShapes feature FID 796 identifies SOFDH / Phi Delta Theta at **503 State St**, owner category GREEK (G1). Purdue’s 2017 chapter article identifies the house at 503 W. State Street and captions its photograph as the exterior during State Street work (V1). The chapter’s current honor-roll page covers the 2025–26 giving year, supporting continuing chapter activity at this address (I1); it does not document the building’s present physical condition.

The March 1910 *Scroll* article, “Indiana Theta’s New House,” documents a newly constructed house begun in July 1909 and names Indianapolis architect Elmer E. Dunlap (Illinois ’97). It describes brick walls, a slate roof, a deliberately high basement, and servants’ quarters on the fourth floor. The accompanying original basement, first-floor and second/third-floor plans are retained as full-page PNG derivatives of the original issue (H1–H3). They show the historic 1909 plan organization, including a front portico symbolized by four columns, but contain no exterior elevations, compass arrow or scale that can be tied to the current envelope. These are firm historic-core constraints, not proof that the whole 2017 house is unchanged from 1909.

A checked 1981 *Scroll* result mentioning extensive exterior renovation, reroofing and painting belongs to **Iowa Beta**, not Indiana Theta at Purdue. It is explicitly excluded as a Phi Delta Theta lifecycle source (X1). A Purdue Archives finding-aid result for “Buildings—Fraternity Houses—Phi Delta Theta” (object 5985) could not be opened; it is a retrieval lead, not visual evidence. A 2019 chapter-house update page was captured, but its linked photographs returned server errors and therefore add no inspected view.

## Massing, signature details, materials, and vertical limits

The visible house reads as a multi-part red-brick residence: a prominent image-left gabled block, centered classical entry/portico, and a lower/darker image-right wing under changing roof heights. The portico, triangular lettered pediment, circular louver, front balcony rails, pale upper gable siding and vertical Greek lettering are the clearest recognition elements. The 1909 article’s high basement, repeated second/third-floor plan and fourth-floor servants’ area explain the original vertical program, but do not map it conclusively to the current facade.

For the 1909 construction, brick walls and slate roof are explicit in the article. In 2017, brick, pale horizontal siding, light trim, dark balcony rails and a dark roof appearance are visible; the roof material, brick bond, mortar, trim substrate, window product and any post-1909 replacement history remain unknown. The round pediment feature appears louvered. No measured elevation, section, roof plan, survey or datum-controlled LiDAR analysis was recovered. Asset dimensions and audit-derived estimates are not physical building measurements.
