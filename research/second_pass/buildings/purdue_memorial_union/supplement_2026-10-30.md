## Opening/profile schedule and material comparison

- **Main south tower:** N02 and 2024 institutional photo show four pale door leaves with individual dark upper panes, a broad arched fanlight with rising/curving tracery, five stacked middle window lights, pale recessed spandrels below, and a separate upper five-light arched composition. Existing clock face is unsupported by these frontal photographs and must be removed from this face. Fine lead bars differ from load-bearing pale mullions. Relief figures flank the middle tier rather than floating outside the tower. Broad corner surfaces remain brick with narrow stone horizontal courses; the tower is not white at its top.
- **Terrace-facing main wing:** N01 resolves five three-light shallow-arched groups in its visible run, each with straight jambs, curved/segmental head, a central rising decorative mullion and fine rectangular lead divisions. Five upper rectangular triple groups align above. Pale sills/heads have projecting edges; brick piers have occasional pale quoin blocks and carved upper pendants. This is not the old model's six narrow semicircular windows across both flanks. A complete opposing-wing count is still unresolved; do not mirror five as a verified whole-building total.
- **Gabled terminal bays:** N03/N06 show large three-light curved-head upper windows, smaller grouped openings below, steep triangular brick gable with pale sloping coping, and shallow pilaster/corbels. Their locations/depth must be registered to inventory steps rather than repeated at every arbitrary corner.
- **2022 ground-level frontage:** N01/N03/N06 reveal tall pale door/side-light assemblies with horizontal glazing subdivisions, recessed lower-level dining entrances and pale vestibule caps, distinct from the older narrow basement windows. New terraces expose them below surrounding upper walks. The 1924 grade photograph cannot supply present stairs/ramp layouts.
- **Roof:** gray/charcoal small slate-like courses in N01/N03/N06, several small gabled dormers and brick chimneys. N09 shows much of the center is flat/light membrane. The existing vast blue low-gable U roof is a material and massing discrepancy. No unsupported roof equipment count is inferred.
- **Materials:** Compare flatter N01/N02 with sunlit N03/N06. Brick is varied warm red/orange-brown with pale mortar; limestone/trim is warm cream/tan, glazing dark desaturated blue-gray with sky reflections, pale painted sash, charcoal slate and dark rail/metal. Existing chalk-white trim and blue slate need revision. These are uncalibrated photographic estimates, not reflectance/RGB specifications. Fine mortar/lead divisions need building-scale coordinates rather than oversized procedural texture.

## Discrepancy checklist before modeling

1. Preserve stable collection/origin and separate hotel ownership. The old 18-wall historical shell is an approximation, not an immutable current survey: compare it with FID644 and log deliberate shape corrections.
2. Replace front clock/generic high turrets with source-supported brick parapet/corner articulation; separate four entry leaves, five-light middle and upper tiers, curved tracery and paired relief panels.
3. Replace unsupported narrow semicircular facade repetition with the observed broad triple-light/upper rectangular families. Retain explicit gaps in opposing/end/service schedules.
4. Replace blue, broad U-shaped roof interpretation with evidence-led slate perimeter/flat-membrane masses and distinct south tower; preserve uncertainty in exact vertical dimensions, roof voids and junctions.
5. Add source-supported lower entrances, gabled end articulation and facade depth. Site-wide landscaping/furniture remains separate; attached vestibules and entry steps are within exterior work.
6. Fresh-open and inspect exact saved file, portable dependencies, matched baseline/final elevations, obliques, roof and details; show the new model before the independent review. Missing north/service and detailed carving evidence limits the honest score and must not be silently accepted.
