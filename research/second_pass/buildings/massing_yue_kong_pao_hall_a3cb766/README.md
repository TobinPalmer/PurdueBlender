## Identity, construction phases and boundary

Purdue’s official theatre visit page gives the current address and locates the building between Agriculture Mall/Marsteller Street and Hawkins Residence Hall/Sheetz Street (P1). Purdue GIS FID 72 labels the footprint PAO, Yue-Kong Pao Hall of Visual and Performing Arts, 552 W Wood Street (P5). Purdue Archives describes a two-phase building: phase one opened in 2003 and phase two began in 2004 (P7). Purdue’s institutional timeline says construction was completed in 2004 (P8). The independent structural engineer, Fink Roberts & Petrie (FRP), also reports completion in 2004 and describes a three-story facility with basement and mechanical penthouse; concrete shearwalls surround the proscenium fly tower, and constrained theater areas use hanging levels (P6). These records identify a stepped, performance-driven mass, but no source inspected here includes as-built exterior elevations or a complete phase boundary.

The official facade image gives the building name in fixed facade letters. The Pao Hall Plaza is a separate public-space project: landscape architect REA describes Wood Street as a pedestrian mall/events terrace forming an entrance setting, with brick paving, limestone seat walls, planted walkways and a parking-lot rain garden (P9). Keep plaza walls, benches, sculptures, bollards and plantings out of the building shell. Purdue reported in August 2026 that Albert Paley’s steel sculpture *Sylvan* was installed in the sculpture corridor outside Pao Hall; that is a current landscape change, not a change to the facade (P10).

## Footprint, orientation and dimensional constraints

The prior second-pass queue used a manifest location proxy. The live-matched Purdue building polygon FID 72 resolves the identity and replaces the proxy for exterior reasoning. Its 145-vertex ring is retained in `sources/purdue_gis_fid72_raw.json`; layer metadata and the prior asset-vs-GIS screening calculation are retained beside it (P5). The source geometry is EPSG:3857 / Web Mercator metres. I inverse-projected each vertex to WGS84 and applied the repository’s local approximate transform, `x=(lon+86.9137)×84810.6041654 m`, `y=(lat−40.4274)×111040 m`. The derived plan envelope is about 94.78 m east-west by 61.05 m north-south; local-tangent polygon area is about 4,571 m². These are GIS-outline calculations, not survey dimensions. The raw shape fields are `Shape__Area=7901.0039` and `Shape__Length=446.2239`; the area is in distorted Web Mercator map-plane units and is not a ground-area estimate. The projected ring perimeter is about 339.46 m. GIS accuracy, wall/roof offset and exact data capture date are not documented.

North-up Esri World Imagery context and the long south/plaza frontage together establish the broad orientation: the bowed public facade faces the plaza to the south; a taller, largely opaque performance/backstage mass sits to its north and west. P1–U3 are all outside views from the plaza/front zone, not a walkaround. The oblique P2 panorama is particularly useful for the south-to-west corner transition and fly-tower silhouette. The aerial is a datedness-unknown map export, not survey control. Do not use the existing massing asset or prior render as real-building evidence.

GIS reports raw `Level_=4`, `NUM_LEVELS=6`, `CountOfFLO=6`, `Extrution=60`, `Elevation=615.4`; the layer dictionary does not define height units, vertical datum or whether these mean occupied floors, level identifiers or extrusion values. This differs from FRP’s three-story plus basement and mechanical-penthouse description. FRP’s structural sections and roof-suspended levels explain why simple exterior floor counting may disagree with an occupancy count, but exact reconciliation needs sections/elevations. Do not interpret 60 or 615.4 as metres. The facade images do not support absolute eave, parapet, fly-tower or floor-to-floor heights.

## Roof, levels, materials and dimensions

The sources support three distinct mass families: a lower curved brick-and-glass plaza frontage; a taller, set-back brick theater/performance volume with sparse high apertures; and a still taller ribbed light-colored fly-tower/mechanical volume, with a glazed vertical junction visible at one corner. The GIS polygon is irregular and bowed along the frontage, while the current massing asset’s own audit describes its raised tower/penthouse and roof slabs as estimates. No roof feature from a render is accepted as true-building evidence. P5 aerial indicates multiple adjoining flat/low-slope roof planes and steps, but surface boundaries and mechanical equipment are not sufficiently legible for an equipment schedule.

At P1/P2, the plaza wing appears predominantly red-brown brick with a grid of recessed control joints, pale metal window frames, reflective blue-gray glass, and a narrow light-colored coping/ledge. P3 shows a lighter tan cast in morning sun and the white/light-gray ribbed upper cladding; this exposure difference is not evidence of a different brick. P1, P2 and P4 show varying glazing colors from sky/trees and camera exposure. The material identity of trim/coping, base strips, window frames and fly-tower cladding is not specified. Do not label them limestone, aluminum, zinc or a particular brick product without plans/specifications.

The strongest dimensional constraints are plan-based GIS envelope and the source-visible relative height steps. GIS-derived approximately 94.78×61.05 m outline, 4,571 m² local area and 145-vertex bowed boundary are approximate. Source attributes conflict semantically with FRP’s three-story/basement/mechanical-penthouse description. A prior audit asset reports lower south roof at 11.5 m, upper wing at 16 m and total extent to 18.87 m; those are model estimates, not measured exterior heights, and must not be promoted into this research brief. FRP lists 166,000 gross square feet; an older Purdue facilities brochure’s indexed text gives 176,213 square feet. The brochure URL now returns 404, so its plans/section have not been visually re-inspected or retained in this dossier. The different reported gross areas have not been reconciled and do not define footprint or wall/roof heights.

The eight count is the visible run in P1, not proof of the hidden/cropped full-elevation total.

- The previous asset source attribution, visible bay schedule and southern facade concept broadly match P1–U4. These are different comparisons and both are screening metrics, not a surveyed wall-line test. - Plaza elements in P1–P4—sculptural heads, current 2026 *Sylvan* steel work, other art, benches, low seatwalls, pavement, lamps and landscape—belong to the site/public art context and not the building shell.
