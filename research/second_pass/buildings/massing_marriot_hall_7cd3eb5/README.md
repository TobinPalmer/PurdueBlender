## Identity, location, scope, and reference condition

Purdue’s building feature identifies FID 862 as MRRT / Marriott Hall at 900 Mitch Daniels Blvd (G1). Purdue’s 2007 announcement places the planned building at the northwest corner of State and University streets (H1); the 2022 Purdue Stories image and current Purdue Events venue listing confirm the present building identity and address (V3–V4). The project architect describes a more open, transparent façade on State Street and a dark-red-brick/tile-roof reference to nearby academic halls (C1). No source inspected here georeferences the individual camera views to the footprint. Therefore “State Street facade” is an architect-attributed design description, not a cardinal assignment for every photographed wall run.

The 2007 announcement predates construction and describes a projected schedule of construction in 2009 and completion in 2012 (H1). Purdue Archives’ indexed record dates the building to 2010 / existence 2010–, while the dedication release is dated April 18, 2012 (H2–H3). These dates can refer to construction, first existence/occupancy and formal dedication; no inspected source gives an as-built exterior phase or a later facade renovation. The present photos show a consistent red-brick, pale-banded and glazed design, but the capture dates of the CSO photographs are unstated. Treat them as architect-hosted design/project photographs rather than proven 2026 field condition.

The 2007 source describes the site before construction; its time-capsule photograph V5 shows people at the ceremony, not the completed building. The pale adjacent low building/pavilion visible left of Marriott in V2 is context and is not included in the FID 862 exterior. Trees, planting beds, benches, bike racks, planters, road, curbs and movable seating in V1–V4/A1 are site context, not shell geometry. The 2022 Purdue page confirms a patio and small trees outside the café (C2), but gives no building wall/terrace boundary drawing.

## Roof, massing, and signature profiles

V1/V2 show a broad, low-pitched hipped roof with a strong red-orange perimeter edge; V2 makes a hip/corner transition visible. The roof projects well beyond the brick piers, with a deep, pale soffit subdivided by repeated beams/rafters. CSO calls the intended roof reference “tile roof” (C1), but no specification or close material image verifies tile versus a metal system; the aerial’s color and fine seams cannot identify the product. V2 also shows red rain leaders aligned with outer supports. No dormers, chimneys, roof signs or roof-level mechanical screens are legible in the obliques.

A1/A2 reveal a substantial pale, apparently flat roof plane immediately behind/adjacent to the red hipped roof zone, with rooftop equipment or penetrations, plus a set-back extension. The aerial supports a compound roof rather than one uninterrupted gable plane. Its unknown capture date and limited detail prevent confident equipment counting, ridge/valley tracing or deciding whether some pale surface is membrane, a roof deck, glare or a different roof phase. Keep the perimeter hip and the separate rear flat-looking zone as separate evidence-backed constraints, and leave their wall/roof junction unmeasured pending roof plan/section.

The visual massing is a low institutional building with a prominent two-band public volume: a highly transparent entrance/corner zone, an upper band of large glazing divided by brick piers, and a lower brick wall with narrow vertical punched windows. The brick roof supports/piers continue above the glass to a deep canopy-like eave; a more solid set-back block extends behind/to one end. The photo suite cannot determine floor-to-floor heights or the exact relationship between the projected roof perimeter and the pale rear roof zone. GIS has a 64.9 m north–south envelope, but neither the aerial nor perspective images alone establish floor heights or finished-grade changes.

- **Brick walls/piers:** CSO identifies the design intent as dark red brick (C1); V1–V4 show a red to reddish-brown running-bond appearance, with pale horizontal bands and pale larger blocks above the piers. V1’s dusk/interior lighting, V2/V3’s direct sun and shadows, and V4’s exposure all change apparent color. Brick product, mortar color, bond dimensions and whether pale pier tops/bands are precast concrete, stone, coated metal or another material remain unknown.
- **Roof:** Architect states tile roof; photographs show a uniform red-orange pitched roof with fine regular lines and a projecting fascia. A1 shows the color/material changing across hip and rear flat-looking zones. Do not select tile, metal, membrane or calibrated color without specification/close evidence.
- **Glazing and frames:** Reflective gray-blue clear glazing occurs at the upper bay band and full-height entrance/corner. Thin pale gray frames/mullions and horizontal transoms are visible; lower narrow openings appear more opaque/curtained. Reflections prevent a calibrated glass tint. Frame alloy, coating and exact subdivision are unspecified.
- **Bands, base, canopy and soffit:** Continuous pale horizontal strips, light base/plinth, rectangular pier caps, flat canopy and deep soffit are photographically established. Their exact material, joints, thickness and profile are not. The white appearance in sun is not proof of limestone.
- **Attached details:** Name letters and rain leaders are visibly fixed features. Freestanding planters, seating, trees, pedestrian rails, flowers, bike racks, signs and adjacent patio furniture are excluded from the shell.

## Existing asset/audit discrepancy checklist and evidence-led construction brief

The existing asset’s audit record is a description of modeling decisions, not real-building truth (audit evidence is not used to resolve unknown facade features). It states that the model uses a north–south gabled ridge, approximate eave 9.6 m and ridge 12.6 m, a four-level reading, brick bands/base and a generalized entry. The source set above changes or qualifies those claims:

- **Roof form:** Exterior V1/V2 show a low broad hip, and A1 suggests a separate pale flat-looking rear roof. The current asset/audit’s single north–south gable and continuous-roof interpretation is unsupported and potentially inconsistent. Recheck against an as-built roof plan and registered aerial before modeling; no roof planes or heights are changed in this research phase.
- **Height/story reading:** Four is a GIS level attribute, while public views show two principal facade bands. The 9.6/12.6 m model estimates are not measured dimensions and remain unresolved. Do not promote them to source constraints.
- **Base and bands:** A pale base and horizontal pale bands are visible, but “limestone” in the existing model is not verified by any inspected material schedule or close view.
- **Openings:** Existing audit describes generalized window treatments. Current photos support upper multi-pane glazing plus lower vertical punched windows on visible runs, but not all-face counts or complete mullion schedules. No hidden face may be populated by symmetry.
- **Footprint:** The model was built to the official 33-vertex GIS ring; the ring itself is retained and rechecked against A1. GIS remains an approximate building outline, not measured wall/overhang geometry.

**Builder constraints from this pass:** retain the 33-vertex source footprint only with its mapping uncertainty; preserve the visibly transparent entry/corner zone and its wrapped flat canopy; separate the large upper glazing run from the lower narrow punched windows and the brick sign band; maintain the regular brick piers, pale cross-bands and deep roof eave; represent the red low-pitched hip only where supported and keep the pale rear roof as a distinct unresolved roof zone.
