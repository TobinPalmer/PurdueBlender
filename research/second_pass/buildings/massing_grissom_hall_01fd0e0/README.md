## 1. Identity, phases, footprint and orientation

The live Purdue GIS BuildingShapesZip4 record queried on 2026-10-30 identifies FID 236 as `GRIS`, “Grissom Hall,” 315 N Grant Street, owner PURDUE. It has 22 unique perimeter vertices (23 including closure). The live ring exactly matches the cached official Purdue footprint snapshot (IoU 1.0; Hausdorff distance 0 m after both were reprojected to NAD83 / UTM zone 16N, EPSG:26916). This verifies the planar source match and its continued currency against that cache, not the source survey accuracy.

The current ring measures approximately **43.12 m east–west by 63.77 m north–south**, **1,925.71 m²** ground area and **250.64 m** perimeter in EPSG:26916. These are polygon calculations rounded from the live EPSG:4326 ring, not surveyed wall-face or gross-floor-area values. The raw feature reports `Shape__Area=3329.3828` and `Shape__Length=329.5716`; those aggregates are consistent with measurement in the service's Web Mercator geometry and are not ground-area/perimeter values. See `sources/geometry_summary.json` for method, source comparison and nearby-feature query. The nearest other saved Purdue primary footprint is Brown Laboratory of Chemistry, about 16.53 m away; the next are Purdue Memorial Union Club (~27.49 m) and Dudley Hall (~32.86 m). The primary polygon set shows no shared wall or mapped connector. Campus paving and the separately documented de Bedout Piazza/water feature are not included in FID 236.

North-up aerial and the GIS outline show a generally north–south building with an irregular, multi-wing perimeter. The overhead view also records an open forecourt/piazza at the southeast side. The visible exterior photo is not georeferenced or cardinally oriented, so its columned elevation cannot yet be assigned to the north, south, east or west face with confidence. The prior audit labels the modeled portico “west”; that cardinal placement remains an asset assumption, not established photo evidence.

**Phase record and conflicts:** Purdue and the Archives identify the Civil Engineering Building as built/opened in 1906. Archives describes two main floors plus an attic story, a 1928 addition, a 1967 renovation and the Grissom name from 1968. Purdue Engineering’s 2013 account describes a three-story 1906 building and says a larger Grant Street-facing addition was completed in 1927. A College of Engineering campus-plan PDF labels a wing addition “1920.” These are not reconciled by a measured drawing; preserve the 1920/1927/1928 addition-date conflict. Purdue IE says the 2014–2015 reinvention gutted the 1906 building to its brick walls, rebuilt the interior, and added windows for more natural light. The 2017 Purdue account locates the de Bedout Piazza water feature at the southeast corner. The current façade should therefore follow the post-2015 exterior condition; older sources are historical context, not current opening schedules.

GIS `BUILT=-1309737600000` and `OCCUPIED=-2035670400000` decode as 1928-07-01 and 1905-06-30 UTC. The layer schema gives no field definitions, and those values conflict with the institution's 1906 building history. They are retained raw in `sources/live_feature_236_2026-10-30.json` and excluded from the phase chronology. GIS also reports `NUM_LEVELS=5` and `CountOfFLO=5`, while Purdue IE’s post-renovation account and the College of Engineering master-plan page describe three transformed floors. The meaning of the GIS count is unresolved; it is not treated as five verified above-grade stories.

## 4. Facade families, signature details and material map

**Brick wall field.** In `G15-PURDUE-G1`, the main visible wall reads as red to reddish-brown brick with lighter brick/pale masonry details at selected piers and openings. Purdue IE says the 2014–2015 work retained the brick walls, so this material family appears continuous across the reinvention, although current brick condition and repairs are not documented. The image suggests horizontal brick courses and repeated pier/window groupings. Bond, joint color, brick dimensions and whether all faces share this treatment are unknown.

**Pale horizontal trim and surrounds.** The photo shows broad cream/pale beige bands at floor/roof transitions, light surrounds or lintels at selected windows and a pronounced entablature/cornice above the columned portion. The color is a daylight photographic impression. Limestone, cast stone, precast concrete and finish system are not distinguished by a source specification; do not call the material identity verified. Band depths, moldings, joints, projection and return conditions are not measured.

**Columned feature and doorway.** At least two substantial round pale shafts with scroll/volute-like capitals and bases are visible in the oblique; the source does not show the full run or prove the total. A wide horizontal entablature/cornice spans this feature. A glazed doorway in a pale surround is visible near the left edge, but the photo cannot prove that the door is centered under, or directly behind, the colonnade. The previous model's four-column west portico is therefore a plausible interpretation that remains unverified in cardinal placement, count, spacing, height and section. Do not add an unsupported triangular pediment; no inspected current source supports one.

**Windows and frames.** Grouped windows vary: some appear as tall multi-light assemblies; others as smaller repeated units with pale surrounds/sills. Dark glass and tree/sky reflections obscure the sash. Purdue IE confirms windows were part of the reinvention to increase natural light, but gives no product or schedule. Exact mullion grids, operating sash, frame finish, reveal depth, lintels and story-by-story repetition remain unknown.

**Entrance/site edge.** The G1 image shows a glazed entry and pale frame; the 2017 Purdue IE article records the de Bedout Piazza at the southeast corner and a sculptural “Balance” water feature installed there in August 2017. The sculpture is four balanced rocks on a stone base, by Toru Oba. This is a site feature, not part of the building shell; exact paving, approach steps, accessible routes, entry orientation and sculpture dimensions are not covered by the imagery reviewed.

**Color statement.** Red-brown brick, warm pale trim/columns, dark window frames and reflective blue/green glazing are visual observations from one foliage-obscured daytime photograph. They are not color-calibrated values. The aerial is unsuitable for finish color or roof product identification.

## 5. Roof form, levels and dimensional constraints

The north-up 2023 aerial shows multiple connected roof areas over the irregular footprint. The main roof field is dark, with lighter sloping/hip-like zones and apparent ridge/valley transitions toward the southern and western portions. This is an imagery-based indication of a multi-plane roof and conflicts with the previous asset's flat parapet description. Trees/shadows and the 0.244 m/pixel resampled export obscure eaves, small slopes, gutters, equipment and exact wing junctions; no pitch, ridge height or eave line is measured. Do not flatten the roof based solely on the previous asset, and do not infer a particular roof covering from aerial color.

The live GIS exposes `Elevation=615.5`, `Extrution=48`, `F02=18.5`, `F03=35`, `R=47.2`, `NUM_LEVELS=5` and `CountOfFLO=5`. The copied layer schema lists field types but provides no units or descriptions for these vertical-looking values. The existing audit's feet conversion is an inference (48 ft → 14.6304 m), not documented GIS semantics. Keep the raw values, but do not use them as verified floor, roof or absolute elevation constraints.

Indiana Statewide 3DEP tile `in2018_30001885_12` was acquired 2018-04-20. Its compound CRS is NAD83(HARN) / Indiana West horizontal coordinates in US survey feet plus NAVD88 height using Geoid12B in US survey feet. The tile metadata and delivery-index feature are saved. A 46,345-byte LAZ clip contains 6,126 queried returns in the FID 236 footprint bounding rectangle; 3,893 lie inside its exact ring. Classes are 4,765 Class 1 and 1,361 Class 2 returns. Outside-ring Class 2 ground median is 615.86 ft. For Class 1 inside a 1 m-eroded ring, height deltas relative to that ground median have p05/p25/p50/p75/p95 of **4.15 / 15.50 / 16.75 / 18.09 / 19.35 m**. Class 1 is unclassified and may include facade, tree and other returns. Point occlusion, scan density and the nearby-ground baseline affect these values. These broad top-return clusters are not surveyed eave/ridge/portico heights and do not validate the model's 14.6304 m maximum Z.

## 6. Evidence-led construction brief and asset discrepancies

Preserve the verified FID 236 plan outline and north–south long axis. The building is an irregular multi-wing brick mass with a major pale, columned/corniced facade feature and a glazed doorway; a south/east-side piazza is separately documented. The direct photo supports grouped window families, light horizontal masonry trim, pale classical columns and a strong entablature. The 2023 overhead indicates distinct roof planes across connected wings; the precise roof profile is open research. The 2014–2015 project retained exterior brick while changing the interior and windows, so old opening conditions may not represent the current façade. The southeast water sculpture belongs to landscape/site context.

Use the current photo only as a partial treatment reference for its pictured facade. Use the 2023 aerial for footprint orientation and roof-plan investigation. Use Purdue's historical and project pages for phase boundaries, not dimensions. Keep the following discrepancies visible during any later modeling work:

- Previous independent R2 audit reports an exact XY match to the official FID 236 outline. That is not evidence for height, roof form, floor count or above-grade facade layout.
- The previous asset interprets undocumented GIS vertical fields as feet and tops the shell at 14.6304 m; neither the layer schema nor a surveyed section verifies that conversion. LiDAR top-return indicators extend higher but are not roof measurements.
- Prior audit describes a flat parapet; north-up 2023 imagery appears multi-plane/pitched. Resolve with roof-level images or drawings before choosing the roof silhouette.
- Prior asset places a four-column portico on the west face. Direct photo supports a columned feature but does not expose the full count or give a reliable cardinal camera bearing. Its location/orientation must be rechecked against a current georeferenced street view.
- Prior asset repeats schematic windows; this pass cannot supply complete counts for any face. The photo gives no defensible full bay schedule.
- GIS says five levels; Purdue IE and the College of Engineering plan describe three post-renovation floors; Archives describes two main floors plus attic for the original building. These terms may refer to different phases or definitions and remain unresolved.
- The prior independent grade noted that the shared modular manifest inventory and bounds were stale relative to the saved 27-object asset. That is a project-index discrepancy, not evidence about the actual building. This research pass did not change the asset or manifest.
