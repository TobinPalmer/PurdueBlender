## Finding and boundary

Purdue GIS FID 798 names Delta Sigma Phi and gives 341 Northwestern Avenue. The City of West Lafayette's 2020 Downtown Plan independently calls that address the Delta Sigma Phi House and lists it among structures encouraged for preservation and reuse. A Purdue-hosted Delta Delta chapter page dated January 17, 2012 also gives 341 Northwestern as its address. Those records support the named target and historic/current association, not a claim about the chapter's 2026 operating status. The March 2026 Street View has a temporary wooden fence marked “Delta Sig”; that is a removable site object and is not evidence of a building feature or organizational status.

FID 798's WGS84 outer ring has 22 distinct corners (23 coordinate pairs including closure). It is the footprint assigned to the target asset by the prior audit. A second nearby massing candidate, `massing_house_860826640_baa087e`, is explicitly outside the FID 798 identity and must remain excluded. The 2026 near-front photograph shows a historic-looking variegated brick volume adjoining a large light-painted, flatter-roofed volume. The two street views from the Grant-side parking/access lane show the brick volume and the pale mass in the same built-up area, but foliage, parking and perspective do not reveal their exact wall junction or whether every visible portion lies inside the FID 798 ring. Keep the brick and pale volumes distinct in any later interpretation pending a survey; do not fold the neighboring buildings into the asset.

The source polygon and visual evidence support a complex composite outline, but the date and connection of the pale wing are unresolved. Its presence in the June 2009 Street View indicates that it predates that image if the prior audit's capture-date reading is correct; the same wing is visible in March 2026. No alteration permit, dated construction drawing or reliable phase photograph was located in this pass. The 2020 plan is a planning recommendation, not a landmark designation or condition assessment.

## Historical shell and current condition

The primary historic source is “Indiana Delta's New Home” in *The Shield of Phi Kappa Psi*, volume 47, no. 1–5 (1926–27), printed pages 342–345. The article says Indiana Delta moved into its new home when Purdue opened for fall term on September 6, 1926. It locates the house on the large triangular lot at the intersection of Northwestern Avenue and Grant Street; describes an L-shaped, three-story house over a basement; and says the main entrance faced Northwestern, while the bottom of the L ran east–west at the south end of the lot and an end faced the point of the lot. A cement rear drive entered from the Grant side. These plan/orientation statements are valuable constraints but use the article's prose directions; no surveyed historic plan was found to reconcile them to modern bearings.

The same article calls the style “genuine old Italian architecture,” and describes dark varicolored red brick, a concrete foundation, medium-dark green tile roofing, limestone window/door/entrance borders, and a continuous belt course between the second and third floors. It describes an east-facing uncovered cement plaza with an iron grille and a Northwestern-facing entrance with a carved limestone fraternity crest. The original page 342 photograph is a contemporary black-and-white oblique of the 1926 house. It shows a substantial brick, multi-mass building with steep gabled roof forms, tall arched/round-headed openings and a strong horizontal masonry band. Print scale, trees and tonal loss prevent reliable opening counts, exact roof junctions or stone profiles. These material and stylistic statements apply to the 1926 Phi Kappa Psi Indiana Delta house, not automatically to every visible 2026 surface.

The historic occupant and current named occupant must not be conflated: the 1926 article documents Phi Kappa Psi at Purdue, while the municipal plan, Purdue GIS and chapter page identify the present address with Delta Sigma Phi. The footprint, lot description, and correspondence between the old photo and current brick mass make survival or substantial reuse of the 1926 shell plausible, but this pass found no deed, construction permit or elevation sequence proving how much of that structure remains. The large pale wing cannot be dated from the inspected evidence.

The prior audit's June 2009 and March 2026 Street View views show the red/buff brick and light-colored mass already co-present by 2009. The March 2026 front view resolves more current detail: steep, dark-roofed gable composition on the brick volume, dormer(s), stone-toned trim, large arched ground-level openings, upper rectangular windows and the pale flat/low-roof volume to its image-right. It is a perspective oblique from near 339 Northwestern Avenue, not a measured elevation. A Grant-side station in March 2026 supplies a different direction: the brick volume has a recessed arcade/terrace-like run, pale piers/arches, and a large tree and parked cars masking the lower wall. Those views show both sides of the apparent historic/current comparison, but not a complete walkaround.

## Massing, materials and dimensions

The current visual record supports at least two distinguishable masses: (1) a steep-roofed, multi-level brick house with gable projections/dormer(s), arched ground openings and masonry band/trim; and (2) a broad pale adjoining volume with a much lower roof edge, regular rectangular upper openings, arched lower openings and small high vents. A recessed terrace/arcade is visible from the Grant-side lane. The historically reported L plan, main entrance toward Northwestern, east-facing terrace and Grant-side drive are useful organizing constraints. The specific connection, height step, footprint contribution and date of the pale mass remain uncertain.

Visual materials in V1–V3: mottled red, ochre and brown brick on the historic-looking mass; pale/white painted or light masonry on the adjoining mass and arcade piers; dark roof covering; pale stone-like bands/surrounds; dark window glazing/frames and dark metal grille/guarding. These are appearance descriptions only. The article supports dark varicolored red brick, concrete foundation, limestone trim/belt course and medium-dark green roof tile for the **1926 house**. It does not prove the current brick, trim, roof or wing surfaces retain those specifications. Daylight, shade, distance, white balance and Google image processing change apparent color. No calibrated RGB, reflectance, product or mortar specification is available.

The polygon's 22 distinct corners constrain the approximate plan but its GIS attributes conflict: the October 30 response reports `Shape__Area=861.02734375` and `Shape__Length=156.9035775477` (service units stated as metres), whereas the prior audit's documented project transform applied to the returned ring yields about 498.110 m² and 119.335 m. The ring is unchanged from October 26. Do not substitute the attribute area/length for calculated ring geometry or turn the undocumented 12 m `Extrution` value into an eave or ridge height. GIS `NUM_LEVELS=1` and `CountOfFLO=1` also conflict with both historic article's three stories over basement and the present multi-level elevations. The GIS field values are retained as conflicting data, not interpreted measurements.

The previous audit estimates wall height at 7.55 m and ridge at 11.95 m with roughly ±2 m uncertainty from its street-view/model comparison. These are rough visual estimates, not measurements or source-validated elevations. No LiDAR/point cloud, verified building section, measured facade, current roof plan or datum-tied height was obtained. Do not use the historic story count as proof of current level heights.
