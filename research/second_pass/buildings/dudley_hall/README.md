## Identity, boundary, reference date and dimensional basis

The asset is Purdue's **Dudley Hall** feature FID 432 / code DUDL. Its connected neighbor is **Lambertus Hall**, a separate building and queue asset, FID 433 / LMBS. The name similarity to Lambert Fieldhouse is irrelevant; Lambertus is its own hall. The completed exterior condition is the 2022–23 Gateway complex as it stood at student opening in spring 2023, with a current-context check from Google Street View dated August 2025. Construction-period views and architect renderings are retained as such.

The official GIS rings have been compared in the adjacent [Lambertus boundary analysis](../lambertus_hall/boundary_analysis.json) and its [north-up plan](../lambertus_hall/boundary_plan.png). The analysis transforms the cached FID 432 and FID 433 polygons from WGS 84 to EPSG:26916. It finds Dudley as the **eastward arm on the south side** of an L-shaped complex and Lambertus as the north–south bar at the west end. Their official inventory polygons touch but have zero overlap. The Dudley ring area is approximately **2,501 m²**; the minimum rotated rectangle is approximately **97.18 m east–west by 32.17 m north–south** in the official campus-map snapshot. These are GIS-derived plan constraints, not facade-wall, as-built, or survey dimensions. The GIS outline has many small steps along the south edge; they register the inventory boundary but do not identify every real-world projection or facade joint.

Purdue Engineering's [2019 naming announcement](https://engineering.purdue.edu/Engr/AboutUs/News/Spotlights/2019/first-gateway-complex-building-named-for-bill-and-marty-dudley) calls Dudley four stories plus a basement and estimates 145,000 square feet. That is an individual-building estimate, not a measured plan. BSA's [project story PDF](https://www.bsadesign.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/03590127.00A-Dudley-Hall-and-Lambertus-Hall-Project-Story.pdf) describes the combined facility as five stories and 251,191 square feet; Ennead lists 250,000 GSF, and Purdue Polytechnic later reports 255,000 GSF for the two connected halls. Keep those three combined-complex area figures together as a small source discrepancy; do not transfer any of them to Dudley alone. The [April 2023 Nuclear Engineering notice](https://engineering.purdue.edu/NE/aboutus/barn%20pdf/202305%20barn.14.pdf) places a fifth-floor north wing in Lambertus and two nuclear labs in Dudley's basement. This supports keeping the complex's fifth level distinct from the four-story Dudley description.

The current modeled shell matches the FID 432 horizontal ring within 0.0001 m according to the prior audit. That is an asset-to-GIS match, not evidence that the GIS line is the building's measured exterior wall. The prior model's 16 m height and facade-level layout remain estimates; none of the sources inspected here gives a surveyed Dudley elevation.

## Signature detail, massing, roof and materials

- **Projected hinge volume:** The actual completed photo E1 shows a large pale, panel-clad rectangular box projecting in front of dark curtainwall. Staggered horizontal panel joints, a crisp projecting top edge, visible side depth, a deep recessed underside/soffit and multi-story vertical glazing change the silhouette. Lower glazing and people obscure exact door leaves. It sits at the shared approach; exact allocation to Dudley or Lambertus is not established.
- **South screen:** The screen is a deep assembly, not a flat color/stripe. Closely spaced reddish horizontal slats stand ahead of dark curtainwall, are carried by dark vertical members, and sit between broad pale vertical piers. C1 and E1 show distinct shadow gaps and corner returns. Ennead describes a terracotta-shaded curtainwall, while BSA calls the contemporary facade glass, metal and stone; available photos do not determine whether each red blade is fired terracotta or coated metal. Record material as **reddish screen, physical specification unresolved**.
- **Brick fields:** Real completed images show warm red/orange Purdue brick with light mortar beside the screen/glass composition. E1 explicitly labels the left brick volume Lambertus, while FID geometry and the camera orientation assign the long screen arm to Dudley. Do not transfer the entire brick mass or its opening counts to Dudley. BSA calls traditional Purdue brick the foundational material, not a surveyed brick blend or mortar specification.
- **Glazing and cladding:** Dark charcoal mullions divide reflective gray-blue curtainwall; reflections and interior lights change appearance with view/time. The pale horizontal-jointed panel frame appears metal/stone-like in photographs and matches the BSA material vocabulary; exact product, finish, color, glass construction and calibrated reflectance remain unknown. Use separate materials for opaque spandrels, clear/reflective glazing, frames, cladding, brick, mortar and blades.
- **Roof and heights:** C1 provides one real elevated roof view. It shows a mostly flat, parapeted composition with pale roof/fascia zones, at least two tall rectangular mechanical enclosures and several cylindrical rooftop units across the combined complex. Their exact Dudley/Lambertus ownership, type, heights and current configuration are not established. The building's four-story-plus-basement description and the complex's separate Lambertus fifth-floor north wing constrain interpretation, but floor-to-floor heights, parapets, roof steps, basement exposure and roof datum are unmeasured. The 2018 regional LiDAR tile predates the 2022–23 building, so it cannot supply as-built roof or eave heights.
- **Campus/site features:** P3 shows brick gateway piers and a paved approach in front of the building. Keep freestanding piers, paving, planters, trees, lights, bike racks and people out of the building shell. BSA's page 5 landscape/site plan distinguishes planting, promenades, bike parking, entry plaza and service areas; it is a 2022 design plan, not verified as-built hardscape.

## Construction brief and existing-asset discrepancy check

Preserve the official FID 432 footprint for horizontal placement while treating every vertical and facade dimension as unresolved. The supported Dudley mass is the eastward south arm of the L-shaped pair, with a four-story-plus-basement source description. Preserve the long south-facing curtainwall/screen family, the modular deep screen and pale vertical piers, the distinct upper glazed ribbon, and the east Grant Street lobby location. At the west end, coordinate the shared projecting glass entry and Lambertus boundary before assigning the hinge wall, roof or screen to either asset. Keep the Lambertus north–south brick bar separate and do not copy its name or window schedule into Dudley.

The 2023 completed images confirm that the connected Gateway facade is richer than the prior broad four-level schematic treatment: they show an actual deep screen bank, a projecting pale-clad/glazed hinge volume, a continuous glazed band and visible rooftop plant. The base shell's GIS XY match remains useful for alignment but is not a building-truth check; the existing estimated 16 m height, facade levels, projected volume and exact division of the shared hinge need primary elevation/as-built evidence before they are treated as dimensions. No geometry, assets or grades were changed during this research pass.

## Pass conclusion

**Prepared with major gaps; bounded pass complete; not demonstrated model-ready.** The pass registers Dudley against its adjacent Lambertus footprint, assigns the completed long south screen elevation to the Dudley arm, identifies a shared south entrance, confirms a current east-side lobby location, and documents the real projected glazing, cladding, brick and roof context. The north/service elevation, complete east entrance, shared hinge ownership, opposite-face openings, measured height and current roof plan remain unresolved. No model changes or grade changes were made.
