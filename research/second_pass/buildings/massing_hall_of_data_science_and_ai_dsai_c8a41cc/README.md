## Identity, history and boundary

This asset is the former **Helen B. Schleman Hall of Student Services**, now named **Hall of Data Science and AI (DSAI)**. The official Purdue GIS snapshot (`audit/dsai/purdue_arcgis_FID196.json`) names the building and address and supplies a closed boundary ring. The service response has 39 coordinate entries including closure (38 unique points). Converting the ring with the project’s approximate local-campus transform gives bounds **X −129.86 to −65.90 m, Y 139.66 to 210.94 m** and an approximate projected polygon area of **1,976 m²**. The shape field `Shape__Area=3417.07` has no documented units and does not equal this projected square-metre calculation; keep the raw GIS field and projected ring area distinct. The project asset directly uses this GIS ring, and the independent saved-asset audit reports a maximum nearest shell-vertex discrepancy of about **0.000008 m**. This establishes asset-to-source copying, not surveyed wall accuracy or independent registration.

The historical identity matters because two separate projects are easily confused. Purdue first approved a **new Data Science Building** in 2019; the August 2021 Board packet rescinded that authorization and approved renovation of the existing Schleman Hall instead. It describes the Schleman renovation as 101,000 gross square feet, construction completion May 2024 and occupancy August 2024. Purdue states that the completed 2024 renovation converted the former Schleman Hall to DSAI. The 2019 new-building site proposal north of the Armory is **not** this asset. MSKTD describes the existing Schleman building as phased construction from the 1920s and 1980s. Purdue archive history also identifies an earlier pharmacy/student-services/geosciences building and a large addition. Exact phase boundaries in the present outer shell have not been mapped from record drawings.

The boundary is an irregular multi-wing footprint, not a rectangle. The 2021 Board packet’s basement/first/second/third-floor plans likewise show offsets, several interlocking wings, a central circulation/core zone and different floor outlines. The plan sheets include a north arrow, but no surveyed control or scale dimensions were identified, so they have not been registered to the GIS perimeter. Do not assume the older plans establish the post-2024 wall line at fine resolution.

## Massing, levels, roofs and dimensional constraints

MSKTD states the existing building was built in phases during the 1920s and 1980s. In the 2024–25 facade views, the mass reads as several joined wings around setbacks/courtyard spaces, with stepped roof intersections rather than one continuous rectangular bar. The public photos show red clay/tile hipped roofs, deep dark eaves, parapet/band changes at wing junctions and a central corner volume whose pale-stone vertical treatment stands out. The 2019 geotagged images directly confirm a planted second-floor terrace/green-roof area with brick parapet and metal top rail. Purdue NRES reports the green-roof installation dates to 2000; no evidence found here confirms which of the 2023 proposed planting/paver designs, if any, was installed later.

The 2021 drawings identify four named plan levels **0–3**, with Level 0 called basement and Levels 1–3 called first through third floors. The GIS also reports four levels. The exterior photos show multiple horizontal glazing bands and a tall upper window tier, but do not resolve whether GIS `NUM_LEVELS=4` counts the basement, all visible storeys, or another campus convention. Do not infer four above-grade floors from that field or from the model. Likewise, raw GIS fields `Extrution=25`, `Elevation=624.5`, and the F02/F03/R values have undocumented units/semantics. Existing audit records describe estimated shell eaves around 10.65 m and a roof high point around 13.19 m; those are model measurements/assumptions, not observed building heights.

The 2021 Level 2 and Level 3 plan sheets show a large out-of-contract region in addition to areas scheduled for complete renovation. This is a useful construction-phase distinction, not a reliable indication of later visible facade differences. The later Purdue Sustainable Operations page confirms the overall project completed in 2024. No elevation set or post-renovation as-built roof plan was found. The 2019 green roof proves a terrace condition at that date, but the exact outer footprint, parapet runs, roof-to-wing joints, tile ridge/hip geometry, skylight/plant/equipment positions and current planting condition remain unmeasured.

## Materials and signature details

- **Masonry:** deep red/red-brown brick across current architect, Purdue and 2019 terrace photographs. The 2019 image supports a regular running-bond-like horizontal mortar pattern at the photographed walls; course size, bond changes and mortar color are not measured. Photo color is not calibrated.
- **Stone:** pale limestone-colored bands form strong horizontal floor-level courses, wrap prominent corners and frame selected windows/entrance zones. Exact stone versus precast identity, sections, joint spacing, projection and repeated locations require specifications or close straight-on detail photos.
- **Windows:** dark metal-looking frames with several distinct families: taller, close-spaced upper-tier lights; lower separated punched or paired vertical openings; and a lower band of small square/transom-like openings. The 2019 terrace wall includes one wide grouped opening with narrower side lights. Frame material, mullion sections, glass type, sash operability and exact sizes are unknown.
- **Roof/eaves:** red clay/terracotta-looking tiles on hipped roof forms; deep dark soffit/eave with a visible fascia/gutter-like line. Exact tile profile, ridge/hip cap, gutters, downspouts, roof pitches and connections are not dimensioned.
- **Entry:** at least one older-looking projecting tile canopy on brick piers with pale stone bases is shown in the undated former-Schleman image; a 2024 view shows a recessed glazed entrance under a broad light-colored portal/band. Determine whether these are separate entrances or renovation changes before modeling either as the principal entry.
- **Terrace:** 2019 geotagged view shows planting trays/beds, modular square paving, fixed benches, brick parapet and red metal guardrail. These are documented for the historic accessible terrace; current plant and furniture arrangement is not verified.
