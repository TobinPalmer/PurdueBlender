## Identity, extent, and reference date

Purdue BuildingShapes feature FID 218 identifies **Helen R. Johnson Hall of Nursing**, code JNSN, at 502 N University Street. The campus historic record identifies Scholer Corporation as architect, lists brick among materials, dates construction to 1975–1977, and says the building was dedicated in 1990. Purdue's timeline says Johnson Hall was complete in 1977; a School of Nursing handbook reports that the Nursing and Allied Health building was ready for occupancy in 1977 and renamed for Helen R. Johnson in 1991. These records concern the legacy JNSN, not the new Darr-Chaney Hall of Pharmacy and Dauch Hall of Nursing (A1, H1–H2, P1–P3).

The source-backed wall-plan reference is Purdue's single closed FID 218 ring. The twelve unique vertices give a valid but stepped, roughly square footprint with project-local bounds of about **42.40 × 42.72 m** and shoelace area **984.01 m²**. OpenStreetMap way 117146488 independently identifies “Johnson Hall of Nursing (JNSN),” address number 502, and gives 1005.86 m²; its boundary is within about **0.876 m Hausdorff distance** of the Purdue outline. This supports using the Purdue vertices as a plan constraint, not as a surveyed wall line. The service's `Shape__Area=1701.02` conflicts with the area calculated from its ring and is not used for scaling (G1–G2; prior calculations in `../../../../audit/massing_johnson_hall_of_nursing_jnsn_823fca7/sources/source_metrics.json`).

The retained asset must be read as the **legacy** building shell at FID 218. A 2023 Indiana Commission authorization proposed a new 186,000-square-foot facility and named Johnson Hall among the buildings for demolition; Purdue's 2024 project schedule forecast make-ready demolition/utilities beginning September 2024, construction start October 2024, completion October 2026, and occupancy October–December 2026. The newer official project site, reviewed October 30, 2026, instead schedules completion for January 2027; Purdue's October 7, 2026 Administrative Operations page forecasts December 2026 occupancy. October 30 Purdue webcams show the new facility substantially enclosed and in finish/site work (P1–P4). These are replacement-project records and do not provide a dated demolition-completion confirmation for the exact old JNSN footprint. The current direct visual status of that legacy footprint remains unverified; the replacement's live views should not be used as JNSN exterior references.

## Dimensional and massing constraints

Purdue GIS reports three levels for FID 218. This is consistent with the general scale implied by the pano but is not an elevation drawing. The raw `Extrution=24` field has no documented units and is not a usable height. The roof point distribution supplies a defensible central height statistic of about **8.14 m above locally sampled ground** for the 2018 surface, with the 95th percentile at 8.18 m. It supports a low, broadly level main roof plane across the eroded polygon interior. It does not establish the edge/eave height, floor-to-floor spacing, basement exposure, absolute NAVD88 elevation of a façade datum, or the identity of upper-tail returns. The original lidar tile, class selection, 3 m interior erosion, 5 m ground buffer, unit conversions, counts, and quantile method are recorded in the audit analysis and `jnsn_lidar_height_measurement.json` (L1).

The 2018 overhead image independently supports a broad flat-looking roof and small raised objects; neither source establishes that every edge is level. The roof object pixels and lidar upper tail require georeferenced point-level review before any penthouse, parapet, vent, or equipment feature could be assigned. The project-local plan bounds and point-derived roof statistic are the only usable quantitative exterior constraints in this pass. No dimensional claims are made from the 2007 Street View perspective.

## Evidence-led brief and asset discrepancies

Evidence supports identifying the legacy asset as a three-level, irregular-footprint campus building, constructed 1975–1977, with brick listed in the institutional materials record, a broad mostly flat-looking roof in 2018 imagery, and a visible upper-level window ribbon on a single tree-obscured long-wall view from September 2007. This is enough to reject the former generic box as an adequate source-backed shape: the footprint should follow the Purdue ring, and the main-roof cluster should be tied to the lidar method above. It is **not** enough to reconstruct a credible exterior across all faces.

The prior candidate used one approximate upper ribbon on the visible long wall and omitted an unsupported entrance, canopy, limestone trim and unobserved openings. Preserve that restraint. The front preview's apparent brick material is compatible with the historic record at broad level, but the record does not specify which walls are brick, and the low-resolution panorama cannot define a precise facade. Its eight rendered panes were explicitly marked as approximate, not a measured count. The saved preview and earlier validation are not real-building sources and must not be used to fill gaps (see `../../../../audit/massing_johnson_hall_of_nursing_jnsn_823fca7/README.md` and `source_record.json`).

The 2018 footprint and LiDAR describe the pre-replacement legacy shell. The October 2026 webcams describe a different, new facility. Keep those data separated. Before any later modeling task, resolve whether the intended reference is an archival JNSN shell or a current site condition; the new Darr-Chaney/Dauch structure is a separate project and not this FID 218 asset.
