## Identity, date and footprint

The subject is the named Stewart Center complex at 128 Memorial Mall. Use the current Purdue campus map's FID 262 as the ranking and location reference. The historic Purdue campus buildings service's OBJECTID 615 also identifies STEW, Stewart Center, and has the same general footprint. These are separate GIS layers and are not surveys.

I projected both saved EPSG:4326 rings to EPSG:26916 (UTM zone 16N; metres) using pyproj, built planar polygons, then compared intersection, union and Hausdorff distance. FID 262 has 49 ring vertices, a 138.52 × 92.01 m bounding envelope and 10,150.20 m² polygon area. OBJECTID 615 has 54 vertices, almost the same envelope, and 10,195.76 m² area. Their intersection is 10,098.42 m²; intersection-over-union is 0.98545; symmetric difference is 149.12 m²; maximum boundary separation is 3.11 m. This strongly supports that both records describe the same mapped complex outline with small digitizing/version differences. Use the current FID 262 ring for this research identity; the existing R4 asset audit traces its shell to historic OBJECTID 615. Neither outline establishes surveyed wall offsets, facade heights, wings, roof shape or entrance position.

The GIS inventory fields NUM_LEVELS=8, CountOfFLO=8, and Extrution=84 are not validated against a vertical datum or units. Purdue Archives' building history calls out three floors in the central part, rather than giving a whole-complex floor schedule. Do not turn those GIS fields into an eight-storey elevation or 84-unit height. The old service also has a 484,279-square-foot inventory value; its scope and measurement method are not stated in the record inspected here. No building-wide measured height, eave, parapet, ridge, roof plan or dimensioned elevation was found in this pass.

Reference condition is the documented occupied complex around 2024–2026. The exterior photos are contemporary but undated at capture; the Purdue Agriculture page was published in November 2024. Street View provides a dated August 2019 west-side record. Construction history and the mural's 1961 dedication are historical context, not proof that every original finish is unchanged today.

## Current massing and connections

Treat the footprint as a large, irregular complex containing the historic central three-floor zone and adjoining wings, rather than as one simple rectangular hall. Purdue Archives says the 1954–1958 Memorial Center addition incorporated the existing University Library and reconstructed Fowler Hall; the building was renamed Stewart Center in 1972. The 2024 Road School maps identify Fowler Hall, Loeb Theater, HSSE Library, Ringel Gallery, Welcome Center, main entrance and a loading dock in the complex, with a route toward Purdue Memorial Union. Those maps are schematic and unscaled, so they confirm uses and connections but do not locate those rooms or doorways precisely on the GIS ring. The evidence does not establish which visible courtyard walls, stairs, ramps, terraces, loading structures or possible shared edges belong to independent adjacent assets.

The mural, The Spirit of the Land Grant College, is documented above the HSSE Library entrance and dedicated in 1961. Purdue's 2025 tour calls it a feature of the West Foyer Lobby. Place it as an interior feature unless an exterior view specifically demonstrates its outside face; available records do not geolocate an exterior mural panel.

### Opening and detail brief for the observed west elevation

Start at the visible southern end only after a future registered image establishes that end; W1 does not show a trustworthy endpoint, so its reproducible schedule is frame-local rather than a full north-to-south count. In the W1 crop, record three visible tall portal/window groups as a minimum. Each has a strong limestone outer surround/reveal and tall rectangular divided glazing; lower zones transition to door-height glazing at the seen entry bay. Brick spans between surrounds. Smaller low-level window openings occur to the right of a tall group in the cropped scene, but foliage and image edge hide their total and exact sequence. Visible stairs meet a raised landing; straight metal rails follow the stair edges. There is not enough resolution to count every mullion consistently or characterize sash operation, fasteners, coping joints or masonry bond at construction-detail scale.

Across the inspected photos, the reliable material palette is red-brown brick, pale warm gray limestone or cast-stone surrounds/coping, clear reflective glazing with light-colored mullions, stone or concrete stairs, and painted metal rails. W1 is sunlit and W3 is overcast; their differing exposures confirm contrast between brick and pale trim, but neither is color-calibrated. Stone identity, brick bond, mortar color, glazing coatings, frame alloy and surface weathering are not analytically verified. No reliable roof color or roof covering is visible.

The portico in P1 has six round shafts in one frontal row, capitals and a broad horizontal entablature beneath a triangular pediment. Entry glazing is behind/between the columns; steps lead to its landing. These are silhouette-critical depth features. Dimensions, shaft fluting, exact spacing, capital profile, pediment pitch, handrail arrangement and relationship to the irregular shell are not measurable from this unscaled photo. Landscape hedges, retaining walls and paving are site elements and must be separated from the building envelope.
