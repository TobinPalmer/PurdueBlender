# Twin Pines Cooperative — exterior research pass (rank 60)

**Status: Prepared with substantial current-side, rear, roof, dimension and detail gaps; provisional. Research only.** The building under review is the active Twin Pines house at 322 Waldron Street, Purdue GIS FID766 / abbreviation SOTP. The current external view set includes a March/April 2026 Google panorama at one curb station and an undated current porch image. No Blender asset, builder, placement, or master scene was edited.

## Identity, reference date, boundary, and history

The fresh Purdue BuildingShapes layer-0 feature FID766 is named “Twin Pines,” has abbreviation SOTP, address 322 Waldron St, owner COOP, and one closed exterior ring. The active cooperative’s official contact page lists Twin Pines at 322 Waldron Street; the Purdue 2023 story quotes alumna Joyce Miles saying Twin Pines is in the same house and same Waldron spot as when she lived there. These independent name/address references resolve the queue’s earlier manifest-proxy identity uncertainty for location ranking. The exact current physical condition is referenced to the April 2026 street imagery and the currently published cooperative porch image; the latter has no verified capture date.

The Purdue News Service finding aid lists a photograph titled “Twin Pines Co-Op House at 322 Waldon” dated 1954-11-30 (MSP19, item 19.004.006, one negative). The item image is not online in the finding aid and was not inspected. The 2011 HHS Ambassadors article also publishes a Twin Pines exterior image at the 322 address, with the house sign visible. The cooperative site labels the organization “Est. 1952”; this is not an independently verified construction year. Do not infer that the present shell is unchanged from 1954 from the alumna’s “same house” statement.

There is a separate historical name trap: Purdue Archives says Shoemaker Cooperative was founded as “Twin Pines” in 1940 and renamed after Carolyn Shoemaker. That is organization history, not evidence that the present 322 Waldron structure was built in 1940. The 1954 photo catalog title and the current postal address identify a house at 322 Waldron by 1954.

### Footprint comparison and georeferencing

The repository’s existing audit and asset use the named OpenStreetMap way 301934836 as a six-distinct-node outline. A fresh Purdue GIS query now provides a second, official named/addressed footprint, FID766. The two projected rings are not coincident: in the repository’s documented approximate campus conversion (WGS84 to local X east/Y north using origin 40.4274, -86.9137 and the local equirectangular factors in `modular/AGENTS.md`), the Purdue ring is about 25.08 × 11.70 m and 243.6 m²; the OSM ring is about 24.61 × 13.51 m and 291.0 m². Their polygon intersection is 227.0 m², union 307.6 m², and intersection-over-union about 0.738. The official ring has 14 distinct vertices; OSM has 6. These are source-outline measurements in an approximate local frame, not surveyed wall measurements. The disagreement may include a porch/overhang/generalization difference, but the photographs do not settle that explanation.

For project ranking, use the geometric centroid of FID766’s ring transformed with the repository local conversion: approximately `[-328.982780, 72.931730]` m (`-86.91757903, 40.42805681` WGS84). It differs only slightly from the previous manifest proxy, so the score changes slightly and rank 60 remains. This centroid selection does not make the FID outline a verified wall line.

### Signature details visible in the saved evidence

- The porch roof is a shallow, projecting cover with a lined underside. TP-WIX shows one small central ceiling light; the photo does not establish wiring/conduit or roof build-up.
- The posts are round and painted light, with scroll-like capitals and square brick piers. TP-2011 shows four supports across the visible porch; the close current crop supports masonry bases and decorative heads, but neither image provides a measured shaft/capital profile.
- The 2011 image shows a low masonry/stone-looking foundation band at grade, a white porch rail, and a freestanding Twin Pines sign in the yard. The yard sign is not part of the building.
- The visible roof is steeply pitched with intersecting gable forms and dark gray shingles; the attic gable has a small window. No roof plan, ridge direction, dormer schedule, chimney attribution or current rear-roof evidence is available. No exposed service vent/downspout count can be reliably made.

## Massing, levels, roof, materials and dimensions

The clearest exterior evidence supports a compact detached house with two full occupied-looking stories beneath a steep pitched roof and attic/gable level, a lower covered porch across a major wall, and at least one projecting or returning wall plane at the right of the 2011 view. The 2011 image suggests an intersecting-gable roof; the panorama is too obstructed to map valleys, ridge ends or rear additions. No verified basement is visible. No aerial, roof plan, measured elevation, LiDAR point set or section was obtained.

The official GIS attributes report `NUM_LEVELS=1`, `CountOfFLO=1`, `Elevation=625`, `R=0`, and `Extrution=12`, with no published field units or vertical datum. The one-level count conflicts with the two-storey plus attic appearance in TP-2011 and the distinct upper/lower window rows in SV-1. `R=0` does not support a flat roof; its field semantics are undocumented. These attributes are not usable floor-to-floor, eave, ridge or total-height measurements. The existing audit asset estimates a 9.05 m gable ridge, but that is a prior modeling assumption, not measured building evidence.

Material and color map from the actual image regions: the large wall fields have horizontal lap siding, appearing white in the 2011 image and muted gray-green in the later current-page porch image; trim, columns and rail read as white/light; shutters read dark blue/black in the 2011 photo; porch supports sit on red/brown brick piers; the foundation reads dark masonry. The roof looks dark gray shingle. These are photographic color/material observations without calibrated color or proof that every face shares the same finish. The current crop confirms the porch columns/brick bases remain legible, but people and shade affect apparent color. Window sash material, glazing, siding product/profile, brick bond, mortar, roof product, flashing and any alteration between the images are unknown.

## Boundary, first-pass asset and discrepancy checklist

- The existing asset/audit uses OSM way 301934836 as the footprint source. A fresh Purdue GIS record directly identifies FID766 as Twin Pines, address 322 Waldron, owner COOP. The two footprints overlap but differ by about 16% of their combined plan area; a six-vertex OSM shell is not an independent survey and should not be silently treated as the official outline.
- FID766 has 14 distinct ring vertices and local-conversion bounds 25.08 × 11.70 m; the OSM outline has 6 and 24.61 × 13.51 m. The OSM outline is slightly wider north–south and offset at corners. The photos do not resolve which line is wall, covered porch, foundation, roof projection or generalization. Future placement can use the current official GIS centroid; an accurate shell needs a mapped wall-to-ring comparison.
- Existing first-pass evidence describes the mesh as two storeys plus attic with a gable, porch, siding, shutters, columns and brick bases. TP-2011 broadly supports those families, while TP-WIX shows a more current porch image. The asset’s window counts, roof dimensions, facade bearing and 9.05 m ridge remain assumptions unless independently mapped/measured.
- The official GIS floor count of one conflicts with photo evidence of two storeys. Preserve this conflict rather than adding a guessed floor-height schedule.
- The apparent front elevation and entry can be placed only photo-relatively. Do not assign a compass direction from the model, queue proxy or imagery perspective.

## Evidence-led future-builder brief

Preserve a small two-full-storey residential mass with a steep intersecting-gable roof, attic opening at the visible gable, projecting covered porch on the photo-visible front wall, light horizontal siding, dark shutters, and a porch carried by round light-colored columns on brick bases. Keep the front/porch wall distinct from the end gable and side return: their openings are not shown to be symmetric. The porch rail, steps, lower foundation and possible rear addition require current corner views before geometry. Use official FID766 for identity and current queue centroid; treat its outline, one-level count and roof fields as unresolved GIS descriptions. The OSM-versus-GIS footprint and official level-versus-photo conflicts are explicit decision points for later research, not choices to hide in a model.
