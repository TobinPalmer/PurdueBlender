## Identity, chronology, orientation, and property boundary

The chapter’s current About page identifies the house at **103 N University Street**, says its original construction was in **1963**, an addition followed in **1968**, major renovations occurred in the **1990s**, and significant exterior/interior updates had taken place by 2026 (T1–T2). A first-party Alumni Organization campaign says a **2023 Summer Refresh** was intended to include painting, flooring, fixtures and other work (T3). This is evidence that the facade/finish can differ across photo eras; neither page provides architectural drawings, exact face-level scope, or a finish schedule. The current chapter homepage uses an image named `PurdueTriangleHouse-PostSRI-Screen-623537-crop_edited.jpg`, but neither EXIF nor the page gives its capture date. The page also says new living-room furniture was installed in summer 2026; that does not establish that the homepage exterior image records any summer-2026 exterior work. Treat P1 as the latest first-party published exterior view, not a dated post-renovation survey.

Purdue GIS FID 777 identifies “Triangle” / `SOTRI` at that address and returns a closed 20-coordinate WGS84 ring. In the repository’s approximate campus-local conversion (X east, Y north), the envelope is about **36.88 m east–west by 31.52 m north–south**, plan area about **719.34 m²**, with bounds X −301.56 to −264.68 m and Y −235.57 to −204.05 m. There are 19 unique vertices. This polygon is an approximate map footprint, not surveyed wall geometry (G1–G2). An independent 2016–2020 Indiana LiDAR-derived building-footprint feature, tagged to 2018, has an approximate converted area of **758.91 m²**. Its centroid is **1.42 m** from the Purdue ring; intersection-over-union is **0.856**, symmetric difference **114.61 m²**, and boundary Hausdorff distance **3.31 m**. The independent outline corroborates identity and broad size, while its differences prohibit treating either ring as the exact wall line (G3).

A north-up Indiana 2023 orthophoto crop with both outlines plotted in their source-returned WGS84 extents and **no image fitting** shows the footprint around two visibly different roof fields and a deep stepped/re-entrant plan edge (A1). The raster is only a broad overhead cross-check; trees obscure portions, roof edge/shadow is not the wall line, and the polygons differ by metres in places. The house is one separate chapter building with adjoining wings; the chapter’s private lot/parking and front sign/plantings are site context, not part of the modeled shell. Do not absorb neighboring parking, sidewalk, monument sign, hedge, retaining beds, or street into the building object.

### Corrected street-side inference

The earlier audit guessed a “west/University Street” entry side. That direction is inconsistent with the source geometry and overhead: the FID 777 footprint lies **west of University Street**, whose north–south road is immediately east/right of the house in the north-up orthophoto. The public approach is therefore most plausibly on the **east-facing University Street frontage**, and the chapter address supports that street association. The entry photo lacks GPS/bearing, so the door-to-ring face assignment remains a strong map/photo inference rather than a surveyed registration. Do not carry the prior west-side label forward as fact (G1, A1; prior audit `audit/triangle/evidence.md`).

## Massing, roof, and height evidence

The plan is not a cuboid: P1 and A1 together show two main intersecting/offset wing volumes, a lower entry/terrace zone, a stepped roof outline, and a deep notch/re-entrant. The overhead resolves the positions of roof zones better than the street photo, but trees and grayscale/shadow conceal whether every dark patch is roof finish, shadow, or plant. No inner court should be enclosed or opened based solely on a building name; map the FID perimeter and retain the actual re-entrant edge while wall topology remains unknown.

The local 2018 DSM covers **316** valid 5-ft grid cell centers inside the official GIS outline. Its elevation quantiles are p10 **647.85**, p25 **648.12**, p50 **653.94**, p75 **654.14**, p90 **654.25**, p99 **656.51**, and maximum **657.42 ftUS**. A 6×6 interior grid yielded 25 points inside the same ring; the Indiana 2016–2020 bare-earth ImageServer values were compared with the nearest same-location DSM cell. Those surface-minus-ground samples have p10 **27.72 ft**, p25 **30.86 ft**, median **33.78 ft (10.30 m)**, p90 **35.79 ft**, p95 **36.25 ft**, and maximum **37.15 ft** (L1; full point values/method in `sources/triangle_lidar_height_screen_2018.json`). The lower tail and multi-foot spread are compatible with roof setbacks/edges or mixed surfaces, but the DSM is not classified to building roof, samples are coarse, and the bare-earth surface under the building is interpolated. This is a top-surface envelope, not wall/eave height or a measured parapet.

The previous audit used a visual **10.2 m wall plus 0.45 m parapet** estimate, about 10.65 m overall. The new height screen is broadly close to that overall scale but cannot validate the wall top, exact total, story count or vertical datum. Purdue GIS reports `NUM_LEVELS=1`, `CountOfFLO=1`, `Extrution=12`, and `Elevation=614`; the one-level fields conflict with the visible multiple rows, while all field units and meanings remain undefined (G1). The 2018 DSM does not settle the conflict or identify a penthouse. Do not turn either the one-level attributes or the photo’s apparent three wall rows into an as-built floor schedule.

## Distinctive details, materials, and context

- **Vertical slot/panel rhythm:** P1/P2 show tall dark windows framed by dark verticals and broad light/tan brick piers. Light opaque rectangles interrupt some slots at an intermediate band. Preserve the visible alternation; exact window-vs-shade boundary is not clear enough to dimension.
- **Entry silhouette:** the distinctive central steep triangular canopy has a dark, thick-looking border/roof edge, with the glass/door and triangular symbol set behind it. Its depth creates the strongest local shadow and entry focus. Exact profile and support layout require a frontal/side detail.
- **Terrace/base:** the public entry is raised above grade on a broad deck with dark block-like retaining wall, dark metal railings and straight stair flights. The pale freestanding “TRIANGLE FRATERNITY” monument, lamps, beds, mature trees, hedge, sidewalk and path are outside the building shell. Separate it from the terrace edge; current photo perspective does not prove every masonry wall is attached building fabric.
- **Upper facade appearance:** P1 daytime shows warm tan/buff brick, near-black/dark brown window framing, dark reflective glass and a dark charcoal roof/canopy edge. The brighter pale vertical panels appear opaque and close in color to warm off-white; whether they are painted panel, shade or another material is unknown. P2 dusk confirms illumination through some glass but shifts color strongly. The 2023 campaign references painting without specifying surface or color; do not infer which visible brick/panels were painted from that statement.
- **Lower wall / service elements:** the base reads as near-black/dark charcoal textured masonry or cladding, but material identity is not documented. Window AC units, banner, Triangle signs, planters and lights are condition-specific or replaceable. No verified finish schedule, brick bond, mortar, window alloy, glazing, roof membrane or detailed canopy profile was located.

P3 provides a limited same-corner earlier image labeled “Current2023”; the current home page’s `PostSRI` photo is consistent in broad silhouette and window/entry arrangement, but the source dates and any facade repaint limits are too uncertain for a precise change map. The official About page reports later exterior updates by summer 2026 but does not say what changed. The current source image’s web publication is not a capture date. A builder should use the geometry and observed appearance, but leave latest finish/condition as an explicit unresolved issue until a directly dated walkaround or documented work scope is obtained.

## Evidence-led exterior brief and prior-audit corrections

A future exterior pass should preserve the official irregular 20-coordinate ring and its deep stepped/re-entrant edge; keep the two intersecting/offset brick wing volumes and lower recessed entry/terrace relationship; make the entry a dark triangular gabled canopy with a recessed glazed/wood-toned vestibule; and differentiate the narrow vertical dark window family, warm brick piers, opaque pale panels and dark lower wall. Maintain the multi-level appearance supported by the photographs while withholding exact floor names/heights. Do not model the unrelated monument sign, landscape, hedges, sidewalks or campus parking as fixed shell components. Do not treat window A/C units, chapter banner or glowing Triangle logo as fixed architecture without a current detail source.

This pass corrects the prior audit’s direction error: University Street is east of the mapped building, so the probable street/entry frontage is east-facing, not “west/University Street.” It adds a current homepage image, a separate dusk-light image of the same corner, current 2026 webpage chronology, the 2023 Alumni Organization refresh scope, a no-fit 2023 aerial overlay using both official and independent outlines, and a reproducible, matched-location DSM/DEM height screen. The prior visual 10.2 m + 0.45 m estimate remains an estimate; its broad scale is consistent with the new 2018 top-surface screen but not verified by it. No Blender asset, builder, placement, scene, or model evidence has been edited in this second-pass research.
