## History and reference condition

Purdue Archives identifies the current Armory as a 1918-present facility built on the site of the earlier Armory, which was destroyed by fire on February 24, 1916. The earlier Military Hall/Armory was moved to the location of the present building's front entrance in 1908. The 1918 Armory served the Army, Air Force and Navy ROTC, with classrooms, offices, a large drill deck and rifle range. Purdue's historical record also describes a 1928 fire in the oil store room that damaged the northern wall and roof. These events establish building identity and change history, but they do not establish the current roof surface or measured elevation.

Reference condition is the occupied building observed in Google Street View in April 2026, supplemented by an August 2019 approach panorama and an undated historical Purdue Archives image. The main archive image shows an earlier exterior state; do not silently carry its openings or roof material into the current condition.

### Evidence-led facade and material brief

For the documented historical frontage, preserve the central entry/gable hierarchy: a central arched entrance below a raised stepped brick gable, an arched multi-light upper opening, and repeated multi-pane openings with pale trim to either side. Give the gable and entrance real depth because they define the silhouette. The archive photo suggests a symmetrical frontage, but its small size and perspective do not establish exact bay counts, sash patterns or dimensions.

Keep the current W1 long wall as a different facade family. In the inspected 2026 view it reads as masonry piers and broad recessed brick panels over a continuous pale stone plinth, with only sparse/unclear openings. Do not mirror the historic frontage's repeated windows onto this side. Confirm whether the current blind-panel appearance is intentional wall treatment, infilled historic openings or perspective compression using a perpendicular, full-length current image before any opening schedule is treated as complete.

The inspected palette is red-brown brick with pale stone or cast-stone base, horizontal bands, window trim and entrance surround. The archive source is monochrome and cannot prove historic color; W1's current color is photograph-dependent and uncalibrated. Brick bond, mortar color, stone type, weathering distribution, glazing coating, frame material and metal roof/fascia finish remain unverified.

The earlier audit's R2 repair removed unsupported raised corner blocks, a roof monitor, hipped patch and exposed trusses and added a photo-supported central stepped gable. Its saved report still labels main roof, wall height, gable width and elevations as estimates. The current West-side view adds a major face-specific constraint that audit did not document: the long wall appears mostly blind/recessed. This is an evidence discrepancy to investigate, not authorization to modify the asset. The prior independent R2 check also notes stale manifest object metadata (1,184 listed versus 703 reopened objects); that is a project record issue outside this research pass.
