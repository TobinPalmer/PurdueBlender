## Identity and boundary

Purdue GIS FID 779 names “Lds Institute of Religion,” gives 14 Waldron St., and assigns the student-organization owner code. The Church's official location index and the Lafayette YSA Ward page identify the West Lafayette institute at 14 Waldron; the location index distinguishes it from the separate meetinghouse at 3224 Jasper Road. The county GIS polygon is a four-corner rectangle, and the asset is matched to that polygon in the prior audit. A fresh October 30, 2026 query returns the same four-point ring as the filtered October 26 snapshot. Using the repository's documented approximate local transform (origin 40.4274, −86.9137; X east, Y north), the footprint is about 18.77 m east–west by 32.94 m north–south, 584.14 m². These are transformed map dimensions, not a field survey.

Keep this target separate from the Church meetinghouse at 3224 Jasper Road, the nearby Hillel building at 912 State Street, and other religious-foundation buildings. The official 2019 Purdue–Hillel minor-subdivision staff report places the Hillel Foundation at the northeast corner of State and Waldron and describes its own expansion/access context. It is retained only to separate a nearby building from the Institute; it is not an Institute plan or construction record. Google Street View stations include several one-story brick and institutional buildings, so a visible wall is assigned to FID 779 only where its position relative to the GIS outline and the known 14 Waldron frontage supports that reading.

## Exterior observations and source dates

The strongest current direct source is Google Street View from the opposite side of Waldron Street, at the camera labeled 23 Waldron Street (40.4248225, −86.9179101), captured July 2023. The 1200 × 800 saved image shows a low, elongated red-brown brick building with a deeply recessed glazed entry and a long dark roof edge. A concrete approach with white metal guardrails includes a ramp and steps. Shrubs and mature trees obscure much of the facade and roof junctions. This view is a perspective oblique, not an orthogonal elevation or measured photograph.

Two additional July 2023 Google camera stations improve the perimeter context. From 917 1st Street (40.4251724, −86.9173697), yaw 202° looks toward the mapped footprint from its north/east side across parking. A low red-brown brick building with a shallow dark roof appears along that view line, but adjacent buildings and parking remain in frame; use it as a candidate side view only where FID 779 registration supports the assignment. From 8 Waldron Street (40.4245476, −86.9179131), yaw 45° looks toward the target from the south/west. It shows a low brick wall and dark shallow roof behind large trees, plus a small portion of windows/entry context. The station is distinct from 23 Waldron but still highly oblique. Yaw 0° and 45° images from this same pano are alternate directions from one camera position, not independent viewpoints. The same applies to yaw rotations saved from the 1st Street station.

The 2023 west/front image includes a higher rust-red roof plane or roof-like mass behind the dark front roof edge. The prior model audit interpreted this as a small raised rear roof section, but the photograph does not establish whether it belongs to FID 779 or to an adjacent building, nor whether it is a ridge, parapet, or separate volume. The 2019 Hillel expansion record and the close religious-facility cluster make strict target attribution important. No new elevated image or current roof plan was found.

## Materials, dimensions and phase

Current photographic appearance is dark red/brown brick, dark brown/gray roof edge, dark glazing/frames, pale-painted metal approach rails, gray concrete access surfaces and landscaped shrubs/trees. These are visual descriptions under July daylight, shade and Google image processing, not verified materials or calibrated colors. The higher reddish roof section may be part of the target or a nearby structure; its material and boundary remain unassigned. No construction specification, current maintenance record, facade close-up, permit set or measured section was found.

The prior audit reports a simple four-corner footprint closely matching the Purdue GIS ring, with asset base vertices within about 0.98 m under its comparison method (the independent grade/reopen validation later records a roughly 0.00002 m residual for its projected ring check). The small method/result difference is an audit detail, not evidence of a physical wall survey. The transformed FID ring dimensions above are approximate. ArcGIS service attributes report `Shape__Area=1009.7109375` and `Shape__Length=132.7905216072` with layer units described as metres; the ring transformed through the repository's local approximation yields about 584.14 m² and 100.95 m. Do not use those service summary fields as wall dimensions.

GIS `NUM_LEVELS=1` and `CountOfFLO=1` agree with the low, single-story appearance, but the `Extrution=12` field has no documented units and cannot establish height. The prior asset estimates a 4.15 m wall, 5.45 m primary ridge and 5.90 m secondary ridge. These are model/audit estimates, not measured constraints, and the second ridge may be a neighboring mass. No LiDAR segment, point-cloud datum, roof drawing or height survey was obtained. The archive's September 1974 “new Latter-Day Saints building” entry is a likely historical lead, not address-specific proof that this is the same structure or its construction date.

## History and neighboring-building separation

Purdue Archives' UA 62, Office of Marketing and Media photographs finding aid catalogs four September 1974 contact-sheet/negative items in Box 93, Folder 36. The description includes religious-foundation buildings and a “new Latter-Day Saints [Mormon] building.” This supports a specific archival image request and a possible circa-1974 construction lead. It does not identify 14 Waldron in the catalog text, and the photographs themselves were not obtained or inspected. The prior audit also records a separate UA 62 Series 3 Box 84, Folder 48.4 lead for Institute exterior photographs. Direct access to the source images/finding-aid PDFs was unavailable in the prior audit; no visual claim is taken from the catalog alone.

An official 2019 county staff report describes Hillel's property at the northeast corner of State and Waldron and its own building expansion/access. It confirms that nearby campus religious-facility structures occupy adjacent views and must not be merged with FID 779. The church locator independently distinguishes the 14 Waldron Institute from the 3224 Jasper meetinghouse. No source found in this pass establishes precise construction year, architect, contractor, renovation phases or a facade alteration timeline for the Institute.

## Future-builder constraints and audit comparison

If a later modeling phase is separately authorized, preserve the low one-story elongated rectangular body and its FID 779 plan; keep the Waldron-side recessed glazed entry, brick piers, long roof-edge silhouette, ramp and steps as distinct features; keep the candidate east/north and south/west sides provisional until directly confirmed. Do not adopt a gable, exact window counts, the small raised red roof, materials or secondary mass from the existing asset without stronger evidence. Keep Hillel and the Jasper meetinghouse out of the asset.

The previous asset is a one-object brick mass on the official four-corner plan, with a main gable, rear raised roof, recessed entry, ramp and two irregularly placed windows. Current V1 supports the one-story brick body, recessed entry and accessible frontage. It does not prove the roof-type assignment, raised roof's membership, full opening count/layout, hidden facade treatment or wall/ridge heights. The possible 1974 archive lead is new phase context, not confirmation. No builder, placement, linked asset, master scene or model geometry was touched in this research pass.
