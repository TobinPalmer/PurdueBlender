## Features that determine the exterior

**Northeast front — observed, with hidden wing bays still unresolved.** The central projection is a deep portico with **six round, smooth-shaft columns**, molded bases and leaf-and-scroll capitals. It supports a substantial layered entablature and a triangular pediment with repeated small cornice details, a plain stone field with joints and a crest ornament at the apex. Preserve the roof depth behind the pediment and the shadowed portico recess. Do not replace it with flat facade ornament or generic square supports. The Commons image resolves the capitals and dentil-like cornice repetition; exact carving profiles remain unmeasured.

There are **three central entrance groups** behind the portico, including glazed doors and geometric diamond/cross transom grilles. The center entrance has a shallow curved hood and ornamental panel/garland detail; adjacent door-head treatments differ. The back wall has **five window bays between the six columns** at the two levels above the entry: taller divided glazing at the lower of these levels, shorter paired openings above. Light frames and central vertical/horizontal divisions are visible; trees and column overlap conceal some edges. Treat these as observed central-bay counts, not a completed whole-building window schedule.

The front wings combine red brick with pale vertical stone window surrounds and horizontal bands. Some lower prominent wing windows have small triangular hoods. The high attic strip has shorter windows below the roof edge; the lower main windows are taller. Do not repeat the attic opening proportions on the main floors. Source photos hide many outer bays, so count each wing from clearer evidence before modeling.

The broad central stairs have multiple runs/landings, **two central handrail lines**, outer pale picket railings, stepped cheek walls and lanterns on end plinths. Nearby curved low retaining walls and square piers with incised dome-like caps organize the approach. These are useful scale/entrance references; respect existing building/site ownership rather than adding shared landscape to the building collection without review. Different camera positions establish depth and stair width more reliably than one frontal crop.

**Southeast end and southwest rear — observed in part.** The 2017 and 2019 opposing views show the short end wrapping in the same brick/stone vocabulary. Pale stone vertical framing encloses tall stacked windows; a strong band separates the shorter top row from the main levels. The rear wing is more utilitarian than the formal portico: exposed brick sections, stone-framed tall window groups, smaller intervening openings, a lower at-grade doorway and local paving/grade changes. The 2019 rear view shows three vertical window bays within the prominent stone-framed group near the corner; this does not establish the full rear count. Ground openings/door rhythm differs from the stair-front composition. Preserve this asymmetry rather than mirroring the northeast entrance.

**Northwest end — partially observed.** The 2009 views show the opposite end as a rectangular brick/stone termination, shorter upper windows, pale framing over the taller main window rows and ground-level openings. They provide an opposing oblique and overall massing check, but their low resolution, foliage and age do not justify exact sash divisions or present-day openings. Rear center, the second-story connection and areas behind trees remain unverified at close range.

**Roof — observed overall, details incomplete.** The north-up aerial shows a long northwest–southeast bar, a broad gray central roof surface, reddish sloping tile bands around its perimeter and the central northeast pediment projection. It is not a single generic pitched prism. The elevated published photos cross-check the red roof edge, pediment ridge and separate larger Elliott roof behind. Several small roof penetrations are visible overhead; their precise type, position and current state need dated high-resolution evidence and a segmented cloud check. Do not copy Elliott's rooftop equipment or mistake it for Hovde's roof.

## Material and color matching

Use the Commons 2016 daylight image, Purdue's 2015 fall front and flowers image together with the 2017/2019 street views. The building has **red/brown brick**, **warm pale cream/gray stone**, **red-orange tile roof edges**, pale window/door frames and reflective glazing that varies strongly with sky and shade. The portico stone is lighter in direct sunlight and cooler/darker in shadow; weathering and mortar/joint lines prevent a uniform bright-white finish. Columns are smooth with modeled capital/base relief; stone panels and bands must remain separate from masonry.

No calibrated paint/stone samples or reliable absolute sRGB values have been acquired. Exposure, seasonal foliage reflections, white balance, haze and editorial saturation differ between sources. The low foreground flowers photo has strong depth/occlusion effects; aerial green/snow scenes establish shape, not an exact color standard. The unavailable Wegiel image is deliberately desaturated. Future material tuning should compare matched neutral-daylight renders against several sources, separating brick, stone, frames, glazing, tile, roof membrane and rails; do not bake one photograph's shadows or blue reflections into base color.

The full-resolution spatial query was written as regular LAZ with COPC hierarchy removed; it is a point-cloud source, not a new building model.

The crop contains **40,551 points**, including **6,884 inside the queried footprint**. Classes are 26,760 unclassified (1), 13,770 ground (2), and 21 noise (7). There is no class-6 building classification in this crop. Class 1 must not be interpreted as confirmed roof points.

The retained compound CRS is **NAD83(HARN) / Indiana West (ftUS) + NAVD88 height — Geoid12B (ftUS)**, with horizontal EPSG:2968 and vertical EPSG:6360 identifiers in the WKT. XYZ units are **US survey feet**, conversion factor **0.30480060960121924 m/ftUS**. Ground-class points outside the footprint have median native Z 622.44 ftUS. Within the one-metre-eroded footprint, 5,490 class-1 returns have candidate heights above this local ground median of approximately **17.194 / 18.261 / 19.361 m** at p50 / p90 / p99. These statistics are **not measured eave, ridge or ornament heights**. Trees, other returns, footprint selection and local ground slope can affect them. Airborne data does not establish windows, mullions, portico detail or material colors, and 2018 data cannot prove current conditions.

### Portico, stairs and material brief

The triangular pediment has a layered projecting cornice with small repeated dentil-like elements on its horizontal and sloped edges, a plain jointed stone field and a crest/acanthus-like apex ornament. The entablature beneath is deep and layered, not a thin pale strip. Corner projection, column-capital relief, pediment depth and the back-wall recess matter in oblique views as much as the frontal outline. Exact carving measurements are unavailable.

The 2013 near stair view resolves multiple wide flights/landings, **two central handrail lines**, pale outer picket balustrades, stepped cheek walls and lantern-bearing plinths. The closer stair/column view and farther Commons photograph separate actual stairs from adjacent curved landscape walls and square capped entrance piers. Do not merge those site walls into a single continuous building foundation without an ownership check. The at-grade southwest approach differs from the monumental front stair.

Use Commons daylight and the 2015 front for warm pale stone, smooth shafts with carved capitals, brick/mortar scale, pale metal/sash and red-orange tile. The elevated mall source reinforces the roof-edge color and contrasting gray roof zone. Shade and foliage reflections darken the rear/portico; do not permanently color the portico black or flatten the column/capital relief into a material map. Stone, brick, glazing, frames, roof tile/membrane, rails and lanterns need separate material treatment. No physical color standard or measured roughness was acquired.
