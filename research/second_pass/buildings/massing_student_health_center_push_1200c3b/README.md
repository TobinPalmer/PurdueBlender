## Identity, reference condition, and boundary

Purdue's official 2020 exterior image identifies the red-brick facility as the Student Health Center (P1). The current PUSH contact page and service pages retain the 601 Stadium Mall Drive address (P2–P3). The Purdue historical timeline says the Student Health Center was completed in 1961 and records the first surgery in the new center on May 1, 1961 (H1). This establishes a 1961 completion/use milestone, not the authorship, original appearance, later additions, or which present walls are original. The current exterior reference is bounded by the latest direct evidence retained here: Purdue photograph published 2020, Indiana spring 2023 aerial, and Purdue service-page text retrieved 2026-10-30. No inspected record establishes whether a later exterior alteration changed the facade after the 2020 photograph.

The Purdue GIS feature is an identifiable 13-distinct-vertex polygon (14 coordinates including ring closure), named “Purdue University Student Health Center,” abbreviation PUSH, and addressed to 601 Stadium Mall Drive (G1). The local campus approximation yields a 1,204.480 m² plan area. This is the named facility outline, not a surveyed wall survey or a verified component-by-component expansion plan. The Indiana orthophoto places the elongated red-roofed building between a parking/vehicle access area and pedestrian walks/landscape, with a pale-roofed lower volume at one end (A1). The photo shows a long main block, a low broad entrance volume in front, and a partially tree-hidden continuation of the taller block. The aerial supports a complex stepped/extended outline, but it does not resolve whether all roof zones are joined by continuous occupied floor plates or identify construction joints.

Purdue's current Radiology Services page says parking is at the rear off University Drive and directs visitors to the Extended Care entrance “through the covered ramp”; radiology check-in is on PUSH's ground floor (P3). This is useful evidence for a rear service/entry approach and covered ramp, but the prose does not register the door or ramp to a specific GIS edge, and no exterior view of that entrance was obtained. Do not equate the image-front glass entrance automatically with all current patient/after-hours entrances. No verified adjacent-building overlap or separate owner/building boundary conflict was identified in the named FID; the project-level public campus map places PUSH in F/G5 (P4), but that grid is only a locator.

The base audit and mass-region partition are implementation records only. This research independently uses the official GIS record, direct photographs, the aerial and Purdue service text; it does not accept audit geometry as real-building evidence.

## Roof, massing, materials, and construction brief

The combined evidence supports an elongated multi-part facility with a taller red-brick block and a lower, broad entrance volume on the public side (P1). The 2023 overhead identifies a dominant warm-colored roof perimeter/plane, a long darker central roof strip or valley-like zone punctuated by several rectangular rooftop openings, a small warm-colored roof volume at the entrance end, and a broad pale/gray lower roof or canopy/extension toward the opposite end (A1). These are plan-level color/edge observations; the aerial does not establish whether the warm roof is tile, metal, or another finish, whether the central strip is a valley, flat roof or shadowed roof plane, or the exact pitch/ridge direction. Roof equipment is not reliably classifiable at this resolution. No roof feature's height, eave, ridge, parapet or wall connection is measured.

Photo-visible material families are red-brown brick masonry with light mortar; pale stone-like cap/belt/window trim; reflective clear/dark entrance glazing and light-colored mullions; and a warm red/orange roof finish visible in P1/A1. “Stone-like” and roof color are photographic descriptions, not verified products. P1 presents bright sun on upper brick and deep tree shade across the central facade; its tonal differences must not be interpreted as different brick batches without another lighting view. A1 has lower fidelity and color cast, suitable for plan zoning only. No specification, mortar/bond schedule, calibrated color, glazing/frame specification, or verified post-2020 renovation record was found. Campus paving, lawn, shrubs, trees, bike/traffic furniture and foreground benches are context, not building components.

**Constraints for a future builder:** retain the Purdue FID 274 outline as a map-scale planning constraint while seeking a surveyed/as-built wall line; separate the low foreground entrance volume from the taller block only where direct evidence supports that junction; preserve the clearly asymmetric entrance group and visible caduceus, light masonry cap/belt, and repeated pale-trimmed upper window family on the observed facade; use the 2023 aerial to constrain roof-zone plan relationships, not invented pitch or height; account for a rear Extended Care approach by covered ramp without fabricating its wall position/shape; leave hidden-side bay counts, wall/wing boundaries, level count and vertical dimensions unresolved. No geometry or model asset was changed in this research pass.
