## Identity, boundaries, phases, and intended reference

The 2019 Indiana Commission for Higher Education purchase request identifies University Church at 320 North Street as a long-term potential new-building location, but anticipates near-term academic/administrative use (L0). The June 2019 State Budget Committee minutes identify the 31,964-square-foot, multi-story facility and 0.86-acre property (L1). A November 2023 Commission agenda describes the same named 2019 acquisition as a 32,000 GSF building on 0.86 acre and explicitly says University Church remained standing and was used for university classroom space (L2). The named GIS record, address, 2024 Purdue Road School plan titled “University Church” (P1), and 2023 image caption/page (V1) independently converge on the same campus building. Purdue’s current campus map still lists UC in grid I7 (M1).

The target reference is the red-brick portico-and-spire building photographed on December 10, 2023, with latest independently verified use as a Purdue classroom building as of November 2023. The 2023 property record's future-development context is not an announcement that this building has been demolished or redesigned. No dated 2024–26 direct facade evidence was found in this pass, so post-2023 lifecycle continuity remains unverified. The official 2024 Road School plan and continued campus-map listing provide evidence the named facility remained in university circulation but do not prove present physical condition (P1, M1).

**Image identity conflict resolved:** V2, an image placed on the local Visit Lafayette/Home of Purdue University Church listing, shows a twin-towered Gothic church. The retained listing HTML embeds metadata giving the image address as **411 N. 7th St., Lafayette**. That is a different address/city from the target’s 320 North Street, West Lafayette. Exclude V2 from the target’s style, twin-tower count, arches, and opening schedule; do not treat it as a historic phase. This corrects the earlier audit uncertainty that it might represent another face/phase. No independent target-building twin-tower evidence exists.

G1’s footprint spans approximately **50.8 × 42.7 m** in project-local axes, has 20 outline vertices, and is not a simple rectangle. The building plan also contains connected lateral/return wings. This is a mapped footprint, not a surveyed wall outline: the GIS record contains conflicting `Level_ = 4`, `NUM_LEVELS = 1`, `CountOfFLO = 3`, and `Extrution = 39` with undocumented meaning/units. The audit reports the same unresolved source conflict. The June 2019 state document’s “multi-story” wording and 2023 32,000 GSF area establish scale/use, not a measured floor count or height.

### Signature details, materials and roof constraints

V1 establishes the defining hierarchy: a large porticoed main end beneath a classical triangular pediment; tall red-brick nave/hall wall; one attached square tower and steep tapered green spire; arched flank windows; and a lower dark-roofed attached wing. The four columns appear round, freestanding, tall and smooth, with pale shafts and block-like/capped heads under the portico roof; cap profiles, bases, capital order, entasis, shaft diameter and projection are not legible enough to specify. The stair/landing crosses most of the front width. The oculus is a small circular grille/window centered high in the pediment. A narrow pale cornice/entablature separates the pediment/portico roof from its columns; exact molding stack and depth are unknown. These permanently attached features define the silhouette; railings, bicycles, signs, planting and neighboring buildings do not.

V1 supports red/brown brick walling, pale portico/trim that appears stone or painted masonry, green weathered-looking spire cladding, and a dark gray low-wing roof. A single image at dusk cannot establish calibrated colors, exact masonry/paint identity, brick bond or mortar, window frame material, spire metal, roofing product, or facade-to-facade consistency. No finish schedule or current close daylight view was located. Treat hue as photographic only.

The main hall/nave roof is a dominant pitched volume behind the portico; its ridge and side slope are partly visible, but pitch, ridge direction, valleys and junction with tower are unresolved. The tower has a square shaft, visible setback/cornice, narrower spire stage and a high, steeply tapering spire. The adjoining low wing has a separate dark pitched roof. No roof plan, roof equipment inventory, drainage details or elevated view was found. No reliable point cloud, datum-tied elevation, measured drawing or section is retained for this building. The existing audit’s eave (~11.7 m), main ridge (~14.9 m) and spire tip (~28.54 m) are model estimates, not source-verified constraints; do not report them as measured building dimensions. Floor-to-floor elevations and exact occupied-story count remain unknown.
