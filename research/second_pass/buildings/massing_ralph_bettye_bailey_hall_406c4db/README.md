## Identity, chronology, and scope boundary

The current Purdue Office of Emergency Preparedness page identifies BALY at 359 Northwestern Avenue and shows a current-condition exterior photograph and first-floor emergency plan (P1–P3). Purdue Archives says the hall was completed in 2014, dedicated October 10, and opened to the public October 11, 2014; it houses Purdue Musical Organizations (H1). The Purdue GIS attributes separately encode `BUILT=2013-12-01` and `OCCUPIED=2014-06-09` after timestamp conversion (G2). Purdue reported a July 2013 Grant Street closure to connect utilities from Knoy Hall to the Bailey Hall site across the street (H2), evidence that the site was under construction then. The GIS “built” date is not defined as groundbreaking, substantial completion, or opening, so these records are retained as distinct milestones rather than treated as a date conflict that can be collapsed. No later mass addition or exterior replacement was identified in the inspected sources.

The latest direct exterior photograph is on Purdue’s OEP page, whose revision date is **2026-04-03**. Its JPEG EXIF records a Samsung SM-S901U camera and capture time **2026-04-02 15:05:11**; EXIF has no GPS position or bearing, and the time zone is not encoded (P1). The second full exterior image was published with a Purdue Archives article dated 2016-12-01; its actual capture date and photographer are unknown (P2). A current reference should therefore favor P1 and the 2023 aerial for condition, using P2 to expose another corner and detail. The Emergency Plan’s current page is not an architectural as-built set.

FID 411 is a 22-distinct-vertex closed footprint under the named BALY feature (G2–G4). The 2023 orthophoto/GIS overlay places the hall as an independent, roughly north–south elongated mass with a small lobby/entry projection and a southeast stepped return; the street and campus space separate it from adjacent buildings (A1–A2). The OEP plan identifies assembly space across the street on Dudley Hall’s terrace (P1), which corroborates the site separation. This assigned asset should describe Bailey Hall’s exterior only; the surrounding road, terrace/planter wall, street furniture, lamp standards, landscape, and neighboring Dudley Hall are context. No shared wall, bridge, or elevated connection appears in the inspected overhead or photos.

## Footprint and dimensional constraints

The live Purdue FeatureServer layer reports native spatial reference WKID 102100 / latest WKID 3857, and its FID 411 query was saved both in WGS84 and native Web Mercator (G1–G3). Transforming the WGS84 ring to the project’s approximate campus plane using `x=(longitude+86.9137)×111412.84×cos(40.4274°)`, `y=(latitude−40.4274)×111040` gives an **east–west envelope of 26.323 m**, **north–south envelope of 35.642 m**, **plan area 842.124 m²**, and **perimeter 129.526 m** (G4). The footprint is map-scale geometry, not a measured wall survey. The live feature’s `Shape__Area=1455.691` and `Shape__Length=170.342` match the geometry when measured in its native Web Mercator plane; they are not ground-scale m²/metres. A previous audit copied `Shape__Area` as the local plan area; this pass corrects that interpretation. Shape transformation, formulas, raw values, and result are preserved in G4.

The U.S. Commission for Higher Education’s 2020 agenda identifies Bailey Hall as an **18,000 gross-square-foot** comparator (H3). Two times the transformed footprint is about 18,128 ft². That close arithmetic agreement supports a two-floor working hypothesis for most of the plate, but gross area and a generalized GIS polygon are not a floor-count survey. It aligns with the two repeated above-grade window bands visible in both exterior images (P2/P4); it does not reconcile Purdue GIS `NUM_LEVELS=5` and `CountOfFLO=5` (G2). The service also reports `Level_=4`, `F02=16.7`, `R=33.4`, `Extrution=28`, and `Elevation=614.2`, but exposes no field-level units or definitions. `Elevation=614.2` is numerically close to the exterior ground-return median of 614.54 ft in the LAS, and `R=33.4` is near the roughly 32.8 ft median roof-surface height above that local ground baseline. These coincidences suggest possible unit/field relationships only; they do not establish the schema semantics or datum. Keep all values as reported, not surveyed levels or elevations.

### 2018 LiDAR surface evidence

The Purdue Integrated Digital Forestry initiative exposes Indiana’s 2016–2020 3DEP tiles. The selected tile `in2018_30001885_12.las` intersects the official FID 411 outline (L1–L3). The 9,753,241-point LAS 1.4 / point-format-6 tile was downloaded and hashed; only a direct five-metre footprint crop is retained here. Its WKT records horizontal **NAD83(HARN) / Indiana West, EPSG:2968, U.S. survey feet** and vertical **NAVD88 height, Geoid12B, U.S. survey feet**. The LAS global encoding marks adjusted standard GPS time; the cropped returns’ GPS times convert to **2018-04-20 22:01:52–22:01:53 UTC**. LAS header creation is 2018-10-19, which is file-production metadata, not the acquisition date (L2–L4).

The FID ring was transformed into EPSG:2968; all points inside the footprint plus a 5 m exterior buffer were retained, without class filtering. The crop has 3,458 points: 2,426 class 1 (unclassified), 1,031 class 2 (ground), and one class 7 (low vegetation). Within the footprint there are 1,858 class-1 returns, 11 class-2 returns, and one class-7 return; there are **no class-6 building-class returns**. As a local statistical baseline, the 1,020 exterior-buffer class-2 ground points have a median of **614.54 ft NAVD88/Geoid12B**. The dominant interior above-grade return band is about **10.00 m** above that baseline (median); its 25th/75th percentiles are about **8.20/10.06 m**, and its 95th/99th percentiles about **10.49/10.54 m**. In the aerial overlay, the large north/left roof zone is dominated by the higher return band and the southeast return is lower. These are 2018 top-surface returns, not wall-eave heights or a certified vertical survey; class 1 is explicitly unclassified and may include rooftop equipment, parapets, or edge vegetation. The state product’s statewide QL2 accuracy statistics are not an area-specific accuracy assessment (L3–L5). The crop, source hash, time conversion, point-selection method, units, vertical datum, and calculated distributions are recorded in L4–L5.

This evidence makes the previous audit’s estimated 7.5 m main parapet questionable: the large roof zone has numerous unclassified returns near 10 m above nearby ground, while the southeast zone includes lower returns. The old 10.18 m entry-tower estimate cannot be confirmed as the high point either. The photo/aerial geometry and LiDAR constrain roof levels substantially better than the earlier door-scaled estimate, but no return should be relabeled as a measured eave without a section or classified roof/wall survey.

## Signature detail, roof, and facade materials

P1/P2 show a tall lobby/entry volume with a narrow pale vertical stone-like panel, dark-to-clear full-height glazing, attached raised building-name sign, and a shallow light canopy over the recessed entrance. The lobby glazing rises higher than the two window rows in the adjoining brick wall and creates the principal public-corner silhouette (P2). Both images show two principal horizontal rows of rectangular dark-framed windows on red-brown brick. Pale stone-like horizontal bands cross the facade and wrap the tower/returns; the upper cap reads as a flat parapet with light coping. These are depth-bearing boundaries, not merely color bands. Exact thickness, setback, masonry profile and mullion section are unmeasured. The first-floor plan shows separate lobby/vestibule and service vestibules; its door arcs do not establish the current exterior door leaf configuration.

The 2023 aerial is consistent with a large flat/low-slope light-colored roof zone and a distinct lower southeast roof/return, while the photo silhouettes show a parapet rather than a pitched roof (A1/P1/P2). Do not call the white surface a specific membrane or infer roof drains/equipment from its color. In the LiDAR height overlay, the dominant roof-top surface is about 10 m above local ground; a southeast zone is lower (L4–L5). P1/P2 show the entrance tower as a local raised form, but point-cloud returns do not prove its roof is the building’s maximum. Need a dimensioned roof plan/section to resolve parapet versus tower and any smaller roof equipment.

Photo-visible material/color families are red-brown brick with light mortar; light buff/cream stone-like horizontal bands, window trim and tower panel; dark metal-looking window frames; reflective/dark entrance glazing; and a pale roof surface in A1. P1 is diffuse/overcast with leafless branches and broad shade; P2 has strong sun and saturated sky reflections. Apparent brick-tone differences are exposure/reflection, not evidence for multiple brick batches. No finish schedule, brick bond/material specification, stone product, mortar profile, glass coating, window-frame alloy, roof membrane or calibrated color was found. Exact material identities remain photographic descriptions.

## Evidence-led exterior brief and audit discrepancies

The independent sources support a compact, approximately 26×36 m irregular plan; a major low-rise brick block arranged in long room wings around an interior corridor, an indented/smaller southeast service return, and a taller glazed public lobby/entrance tower. The current facade has two repeated window rows, continuous pale level bands, flat parapet/coping, and a distinctly recessed glazed public entrance. The project first-floor plan indicates one main central vestibule and at least one separate service/storage-end vestibule. The overhead constrains roof plan and confirms the no-bridge/no-shared-wall site boundary. Most wall heights remain unsupported by a dimensioned elevation.

The existing asset/audit evidence is not real-building ground truth. The earlier `evidence.json` reports the same official ring, but labels its `1455.691` native `Shape__Area` value as local plan square metres; G4 corrects the local transformed area to **842.124 m²**. The asset uses a **7.5 m main parapet** and **10.18 m entry-tower apex** estimate; neither is surveyed. The 2018 LiDAR returns put the extensive high roof surface near **10.0 m** above a local point-cloud ground baseline, with the southeast zone lower. This is a material challenge to the prior height assignment, not authority to change model geometry during research. `NUM_LEVELS=5` and `CountOfFLO=5` remain undefined and discordant with the two visible window rows, the first-floor-only plan, and the rough 18,000-GSF/two-footprint-area cross-check. The best working story interpretation is two floors over most of the plate plus a taller lobby volume, but the official GIS level fields and as-built floor heights must be resolved before construction.

**Builder constraints for a future pass:** preserve the official irregular plan including the southeast notch and lobby projection; keep the public entry asymmetrical and recessed beneath the taller glazed tower; continue the two window rows and horizontal pale bands only on image/plan-supported runs; distinguish the lower southeast roof from the dominant taller roof; do not substitute the mapped GIS floor count for observed facade rows; and do not use the old 7.5 m/10.18 m audit estimates as measured heights. Keep the two unphotographed full elevations, exact plan-to-image side mapping, roof/equipment detail, and all unsupported opening totals unknown. No asset, geometry, builder, placement, or master scene was changed during this research pass.
