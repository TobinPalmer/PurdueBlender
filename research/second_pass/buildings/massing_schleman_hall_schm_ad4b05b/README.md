## Identity correction and intended condition

The queued asset is the **former Recitation Building**, renamed **Helen B. Schleman Hall** in the Dec. 2022 Purdue trustee renaming cycle and referred to as Schleman Hall by Purdue in Jan. 2023. Purdue Archives dates the Recitation Building to 1923 and describes it as a three-story dark-brick-and-stone building with terracotta ornament and a tile roof. Purdue's historical timeline records completion in 1923. The current GIS record identifies FID 224 as Helen B. Schleman Hall, `SCHM`, address **656 Oval Drive**. The intended reference condition is the occupied building under the Helen B. Schleman Hall name; the photograph of its inscription still says “RECITATION BUILDING.”

**A consequential inherited-audit identity error is resolved here.** The old Helen B. Schleman Hall of Student Services at **475 Stadium Mall Drive** is a different physical building, now Hall of Data Science and AI, represented by GIS FID 196. The inherited `audit/helen_b_schleman_hall/` records assigned that former building's Student Services Events photograph, 2021 renovation plans, basement interpretation, and associated interior claims to FID 224. They are not evidence for this dossier. Likewise, the inherited `audit/dsai/` MSKTD article, DSAI project photographs, green-roof photographs, and renovation record concern FID 196 and are excluded from FID 224 observations. The old building's large, phased 1920s/1980s DSAI renovation story must not be transferred to this smaller 1923 Recitation Building. The Purdue 2021 Schleman/Stewart renovation plan set is therefore not a plan for the target FID 224.

The appropriate current-name identity chain is supported by Purdue's [Student Support Services page](https://purdue.edu/vpsl/news/featured-stories/2022_archive/student-support-services-starting-point-for-students.php) (explicitly “Helen B. Schleman Hall (formerly known as the Recitation Building)”), the Purdue Senate's [January 2023 minutes](https://www.purdue.edu/senate/documents/meetings/Jan-2023-Minutes.pdf), and the current Purdue Student Legal Services page/address at [656 Oval Drive](https://www.purdue.edu/odos/sls/). The official campus GIS record and this historical identity chain take precedence over name-only matches. Do not associate FID 224 with 475 Stadium Mall Drive, the former Schleman/FID196, or the Hall of Data Science and AI.

## Footprint, orientation and site relationships

`GIS-224-LIVE` is a live ArcGIS query retrieved 2026-10-30 and compared with the repository-preserved Purdue building snapshot: the single FID 224 geometry is byte-for-byte equivalent at the geometry-object level. It contains 16 distinct boundary vertices plus ring closure. This verifies source continuity, not a surveyed wall line. Its source is EPSG:4326. Reprojection to **EPSG:26916 (NAD83 / UTM zone 16N, metres)** with pyproj and a Shapely polygon calculation gives approximately **1,212.25 m² area** and **168.90 m perimeter**. The minimum rotated rectangle is **53.75 m × 25.16 m**; its long axis is approximately north–south (1.33° from north). These are GIS-derived plan constraints, not field dimensions, and no source positional tolerance is published.

The live fields also report `Level_=4`, `NUM_LEVELS=4`, `CountOfFLO=4`, `Elevation=623.3`, `F01=13.4`, `F02=26.8`, `R=39.9`, and `Extrution=48`. Units/definitions for these numeric fields are absent in the supplied layer metadata. Historical Archives says three stories; do not label a basement, fourth storey, or any height as resolved from the GIS values. A probable feet interpretation for some height-like fields is only a hypothesis.

The source's `Shape__Area=2095.8398` and `Shape__Length=222.2059` conflict with the independently computed EPSG:26916 geometry area/perimeter. Their units and calculation basis are undocumented; do not repeat them as metric ground measurements. The prior audit's 53.77 × 25.15 m envelope happened to match the projected minimum rectangle, but its “official polygon area 2095.84 m²” is not supported by the projected ring and should be treated as a field-unit/basis conflict.

`AERIAL-2023` is north-up Indiana GIO World Imagery with a recorded **2023-02-11** source date, 7.62 cm source resolution, and 0.15 m stated accuracy in Esri's identify response. The retained `aerial_FID224_outline_overlay.png` places the exact GIS ring over the source raster. It aligns closely with the long, notched roof mass and confirms north/south orientation, not wall face details. Acquisition is before the 2022–23 name change but after the building's known construction date; imagery age does not establish subsequent facade alteration.

Nearest neighboring Purdue GIS polygon edge distances in the same EPSG:26916 calculation are University Hall FID 213: **10.86 m**; Class of 1950 Lecture Hall FID 230: **14.27 m**; Mathematical Sciences FID 227: **22.38 m**; Beering Hall FID 251: **22.64 m**. Distances are polygon-to-polygon gaps, not entrance/walking distances or evidence of a physical connection. The aerial shows intervening paths/site space. No reviewed FID 224 source documents a shared bridge or a boundary crossing; retain neighboring assets as separate.

## Signature details and facade/material map

- **Principal entry:** `SCHM-CURRENT-PORTICO` shows a projecting, pale stone portal set into dark red-brown brick. The composition has a triangular pediment with layered cornice/moulding and dentil-like small repeated blocks; frieze/inscription panel with round end medallions; two substantial round or engaged columns/pilasters with ornate capitals and bases; a deep rectangular inner surround; upper glass/transom panel; and paired modern glazed doors. The pale stone elements project enough to cast distinct shadows. Current glass lettering is a changeable sign; “RECITATION BVILDING” carved in the stone is permanent architecture. Exact moulding profiles, column dimensions and portal projection are not measured. Plantings, pots, accessible sign and other site furniture are movable/site elements, not facade geometry.
- **Historic entrance corroboration:** `SCHM-ARCH-1182/1183/1184/1185` show the same recognizable portal family and confirm the stone inscription predates the new name. The old portal had multi-light glazing/wood or light-colored frames; do not transfer those old frame details to the current dark-framed doors/windows without contemporary proof. Ivy in `1183/1184` is vegetation, not masonry texture.
- **Masonry:** Purdue Archives' Recitation record describes dark brick and stone; the 1929 colored view shows warm red brick with pale continuous horizontal stone courses and light stone around the portals. Current portal photo shows red/maroon brick, pale cream limestone or similar stone, and a pale continuous belt visible at floor line. Exact quarry, brick bond/mortar, stone type, repairs, color values and exposure-calibrated colors are unknown. These photos support color families, not measured RGB or material specifications.
- **Roof:** Archive photos describe/depict tile roof. The broad 1929 color view and 2023 overhead image indicate a low-pitched, broadly hipped tiled roof with long central ridge and return/hip geometry over the notched footprint; several intersecting hips or roof breaks are likely. The aerial overlays the footprint but does not prove slopes, roof construction, ridges, valleys, dormers, drainage, or equipment. Roof color in the 1929 print is historical and uncalibrated; current roof finish/color needs a current elevated photo.
- **Glazing/frames:** The current photo shows dark bronze/brown metal-looking frames, reflective clear glazing, and at least one tall narrow window with horizontal divisions. Historic images show lighter multi-light sash and should only inform historic phases. The breadth of current window replacement is not documented by available source.

## Roof and dimensional evidence

`LIDAR-2018` was clipped from Indiana COPC tile `in2018_29951885_12`, acquisition **2018-04-20**. The saved clip contains 17,435 points (class 1: 9,127; class 2: 8,301; class 7: 7), with 5,496 inside the GIS polygon. CRS is the compound **NAD83(HARN) / Indiana West (ftUS) + NAVD88 height - Geoid12B (ftUS)**; horizontal and vertical units are US survey feet. Median class-2 ground outside the footprint is 623.16 ftUS. Eroding the polygon by one foot and taking class-1 returns produced candidate relative p50/p90/p99 heights 16.87/18.39/18.89 m. Class 1 includes trees and other objects, and the high values are consistent with tree contamination; these percentiles are **not building eave/ridge heights**. The dataset cannot reveal openings or facade profiles. The crop's method, retained WKT, source URI, clip hash and caveats are in `sources/lidar_crop_summary.json`.

The aerial roof silhouette, 1929 historic view and LiDAR tile are independent kinds of constraint, but none supplies a datum-tied section. No verified as-built roof plan, elevations, section, floor-to-floor heights, parapet/eave height, ridge height, basement exposure, skylight/dormer layout or current rooftop equipment schedule was found. Do not use model height, a top-return percentile, GIS `R`/`F01` fields, or the unrelated FID 196 DSAI plans as measured values.

## Builder brief and inherited asset discrepancies

For a future, separately authorized model iteration, preserve the single elongated, slightly irregular N–S plan ring from FID224; set three above-grade storeys as the strongest historic evidence while leaving basement and GIS fourth-level interpretation explicit; use a low tile-clad hipped roof as a source-backed form family, but await roof plans/current elevated images before fixing intersections or pitches. The building's defining silhouette is the long low roof over three horizontal brick/stone levels. Give the formal stone entrance real depth: broad surround, pediment/frieze, inscription, round supports and recessed glazed double doors. The facade material system is warm/dark red brick against pale continuous stone banding, portal masonry, dark modern glazing/frames, and a historically supported tile roof. Use repeated window rhythm as a qualitative rule only until all faces can be counted and current frame patterns checked.

The old asset audit must not be taken as truth for these details. Its source context conflated a FID 196 former-Schleman building with FID224. The imported FID 224 live/cache ring itself is valid; the audit's exterior photo, 2021 room plans, interior schematic, four-level/basement rationale, entry-side assignment and any photo-based facade/roof assertions founded on those sources are not validated by this pass. The previous roof iteration is described as intersecting gable wings; the target's historical photographs support a low hipped/tile roof silhouette instead. This is a discrepancy to resolve with an actual current overhead/roof drawing before any geometry change. Existing entry position/dimensions and three-storey wall/roof height remain estimates. No `.blend` files were opened or edited for this research pass.
