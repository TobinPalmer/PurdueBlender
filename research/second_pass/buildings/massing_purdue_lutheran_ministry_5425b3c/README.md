## Identity, reference date, boundary and chronology

The intended building is **Purdue Lutheran Ministry (PLM), 330 W Fowler Ave**, not University Lutheran Church at 460 Northwestern Avenue. Our Saviour Lutheran Church’s current PLM location page gives the street address and coordinates (I1); Purdue’s Fall 2022 resource guide independently lists PLM at 330 W Fowler at the Fowler/Northwestern intersection (I2). The March 2026 street images show the small yellow-sided house with a Purdue Lutheran Ministry sign at this corner (V1–V3). A larger neighboring multi-level house with brick lower walls and a porch is separately visible in V1/V3; it is not part of the target footprint.

OpenStreetMap way 860901812 is explicitly named “Purdue Lutheran Ministry” and tagged `building=church` (G1). Its four unique corners describe an approximately **8.37 m east-west × 12.16 m north-south** rectangle and about **101.7 m²** under the project’s local equirectangular transform. These are generalized mapped polygon metrics, not survey measurements or verified wall lines. A current search of Purdue BuildingShapes over the location returned zero features (G2), consistent with this independent ministry property being absent from that campus layer; it supplies no official Purdue building outline. The project location/anchor already follows the named OSM outline rather than a positive Purdue GIS feature.

Tippecanoe County’s official assessor GIS Year Built layer returns **1910** for the 330 W Fowler parcel (G3). This is a nominal tax attribute, not an archival construction record. A commercial property-record aggregator displays 1925 for the address (X1); that unverified summary conflicts with the county field, and neither source establishes the present exterior’s construction or alteration dates. PLM/Our Saviour’s official page identifies the location today, but no permit, original plan, deed history or date-stamped construction photograph for this small house was located. The official PLM image path includes `/2017/06/`; its Wayback capture is dated 2024-02-12. Treat it as a 2017-era close facade source, not proof of the full 2017 building condition or exact image capture date (V4).

The March 2026 panoramas are the best available current-condition evidence. The stored Esri overhead tile has no acquisition date and is only a low-resolution plan/roof-context check (A1). County assessor GIS itself says its map data are a reference snapshot, may be unsuitable for some purposes, and is not a legal survey (G4). The county’s year-built parcel geometry is not the house footprint and is intentionally not used for dimensions.

## Massing, roof, entry, and materials

The current evidence supports a compact two-story converted house on a simple rectangular main footprint, with a broad, shallow front canopy/porch roof visible in V4 and a raised front entry. In March 2026, V1 resolves the overall roof as **low-pitched hipped rather than the north-south gable inferred in the older asset audit**. The street view shows a gray, shingle-like covering, broad eaves, a small roof vent and one masonry chimney near the image-left/west portion. The low-resolution overhead image is consistent with a broad hipped roof but cannot establish slopes, ridge/hip junctions or covering. The older audit’s simple gable roof was an inference and should not be carried forward as verified geometry.

V1 shows two above-grade facade levels and a gray exposed base; it does not resolve whether that base contains a usable basement or determine floor elevations. V4 shows the front canopy’s shingle-like surface and a horizontal gutter/fascia edge, but no full section, post/brace profile or exact tie-in to the main wall. In current views the front approach is raised above sidewalk grade; the approach, stair, landing, rail and canopy need closer measurements before dimensioning. The OSM polygon is a main plan constraint; porch/canopy, stair and roof projections can extend beyond it.

The facade-specific visual material map is: golden-yellow horizontal siding over the main walls; white/light window and entry frames; dark navy/blue shutters; gray exposed base; dark/gray shingle-like roof; and a red/brown masonry chimney. V1 is an uncalibrated sunlit color view, while V4 is close and highly saturated; color values are estimates, not specifications. Siding profile/course height, wood versus vinyl, roof product, chimney brick/mortar, foundation finish, sash material, shutter operation and canopy framing are unverified. The 1910 county year-built entry cannot establish that any visible material is original.
