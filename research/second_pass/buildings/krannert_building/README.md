# Krannert Building — second-pass exterior research

**Queue rank:** 45 · **Stable ID:** `krannert_building` · **Model owner:** Rossen  
**Identity:** Krannert Building (KRAN), Purdue Campus GIS BuildingShapesZip4 FID 54, near State Street and Grant Street in West Lafayette.  
**Review date:** 2026-10-30 · **Status:** Exterior brief prepared with gaps. The overall outline, principal long-face language, and broad stepped-roof profile are supported by independent imagery and elevation data; several face-by-face schedules, current inner/re-entrant faces, entrance geometry, exact story/roof datums, and material specifications remain unresolved. Provisional research package; not a construction-ready survey.

## Identity, orientation, chronology, and boundaries

Purdue Campus GIS identifies the KRAN footprint as one named FID 54 feature. Transforming its 42-coordinate closed WGS84 ring to the project’s approximate campus-local plane yields about **76.0 × 47.5 m**, **2,086.65 m²**, and **289.53 m perimeter**. There are 41 unique vertices. The shape has deep re-entrant offsets and attached orthogonal wings; it is not a single rectangular slab. These are screening measurements from generalized GIS geometry, not surveyed wall lines. The statewide Indiana 2023 north-up orthophoto and direct FID 54 overlay show the outline and re-entrant roof form in its campus setting without fitting the ring to the image (G1–G3, A1). The previous asset wire uses the same GIS source, so its near-zero vertex difference is conversion integrity, not independent building accuracy. The orthophoto is the independent visual cross-check and only supports broad placement/orientation and roof perimeter.

The **1963** Purdue Libraries construction aerial is especially useful for mass composition (H1): a taller, narrow-slot block stands at the street edge, with long lower/orthogonal masses extending behind it, a central junction and multiple roof levels. The 2023 roof outline is consistent with this multi-wing footprint. The 1963 image documents the construction-era arrangement, not every present-day alteration. Purdue’s historical timeline says the Krannert Graduate School of Management Building was completed in 1963; the Purdue Archives catalog separately identifies a **1965 dedication** folder, while the October 1965 *Indiana Architect* photo presents the completed facade (H2–H4). These are distinct completion, dedication, and publication records, not evidence that the modern exterior is unchanged since 1965. The recent Purdue Events image is current-condition evidence, but the capture date is not supplied (P1).

This dossier covers the Krannert Building shell only. Streets, sidewalks, lawns, the 1965 reflecting pool, adjacent Rawls Hall and campus context are separate; the pool appears only in the archival architectural photograph and is not asserted as current. The removed, unsupported site paving slab from an earlier asset pass is not real-building evidence. No bridge or shared wall is established by the inspected footprint/aerial. Where the orthophoto indicates close neighboring context, parcel/asset boundaries remain those of the official building footprint (G1, A1).

## Massing, roof, and dimensional constraints

The GIS screen dimensions are approximately 76 m east–west by 47.5 m north–south, with a 2,086.65 m² polygon and about 289.53 m perimeter in the project’s approximate local conversion. The high oblique construction aerial and current overhead jointly support a compact, deep, multi-wing office complex with one more prominent street-edge block, attached long perpendicular runs, a lower connector/wing, a deep re-entrant and multiple roof levels. The aerial is the best evidence for the mass relationship; the orthophoto is the best independent source for today’s plan footprint. Neither provides a measured wall/eave line or cross-section.

Indiana’s 2018 DSM is identified as the 5-ft-cell tile `in2018_30001885_12`, acquisition date 2018-04-20, NAD83(HARN) / Indiana West in U.S. survey feet. A 5-ft surface-minus-terrain screen over the raw FID 54 footprint used 895 valid DSM cell centers and 22 bare-earth DEM samples. The DEM elevations have 613.98–615.49 ftUS range and 615.255 ftUS median; the DSM is 721.71 ftUS median. The median surface difference is **106.46 ft (32.45 m)**; p25 is **91.61 ft (27.92 m)**, p90 is **106.69 ft (32.52 m)** and maximum is **107.65 ft (32.81 m)**. The spread is compatible with a stepped roof population, but the low tail includes lower surfaces/edges/voids and the high value is not a certified parapet. The 5-ft DSM does not independently show or confirm the model’s 117-ft penthouse or 120.7-ft overrun; those remain unresolved rather than proven absent (L1).

The GIS layer reports `Elevation=612.4`, `G=12.7`, `F01=25.4` through `F07=104.3`, `R=117`, and `Extrution=108`; it also has `NUM_LEVELS=10` and `CountOfFLO=10`. The returned layer schema provides no units or definitions. Values are internally consistent with feet and a ground-relative floor sequence only under an assumed common datum; this is not independent evidence because earlier asset work used these values. `R` and `Extrution` could map to different height/roof concepts; the DSM screen supports broad roof-level plausibility near the main modeled deck/parapet, not floor names or an overrun. Preserve this semantic ambiguity (G1–G2, L1).

The historic facade photo shows a flat roof edge and a narrow screen-like strip; the construction aerial reveals multiple raised/stepped rectangular roof forms. The 2023 overhead shows distinct roof planes and re-entrant form, but current rooftop mechanical equipment, parapet screen, elevator overrun and exact lower-roof connections are too small or shadowed to identify. No roof plan, section, wall section or datum-tied elevation is included in this package.

## Signature details and materials

The identifying feature is the tall vertical rhythm: slender dark glazing set well within broad, pale piers. In the 1965 image each glazed strip has fine horizontal subdivisions, and the piers remain visually continuous through the upper levels. The lower storey is darker and more open, with an entry canopy/colonnade treatment that reads as a recessed base rather than another row of upper slots. The roof edge is horizontal, with a historically visible screened strip. These are photo-visible boundaries and depth changes; exact pier projection, reveal depth, mullion section, canopy thickness/support spacing and roof-screen profile have not been measured (P1–P2).

P1 in color reads as warm light gray/buff upper piers or cladding, dark bronze/black-looking frames and glazing, and a red-brown ground-level wall/band. H1 and P2 provide alternate light conditions/media but cannot calibrate color: P2 is a halftone print and H1 is a historic aerial. Treat these as photographic appearance only. No verified specification was found for limestone versus precast/concrete, masonry substrate, cladding panels, frame alloy/finish, glass tint, lower wall material or roof membrane. Reflections account for some changing glass brightness; the dark glazing is not a solid black panel. Do not use the model’s material names as real-building evidence.

## Evidence-led exterior brief and prior-audit discrepancies

A future exterior builder should preserve the irregular official footprint and attached orthogonal wings, the prominent street-edge block, and the deep inner/re-entrant opening; separate the taller primary block from lower connector/wing roofs; and express the supported long-face contrast of broad light vertical piers, narrow dark vertical slots with fine horizontal divisions, a dark/open ground story and a flat parapet silhouette. Keep the entry/canopy as its own recessed zone rather than repeating upper-level fenestration through it. A roof screen is supported historically in P2 but requires a current view before being asserted on the current asset. Do not add the archival reflecting pool as building geometry. Do not extend any measured-looking opening counts onto faces that the source does not show.

Prior asset reviews report a 41-vertex roof boundary derived from the same FID 54 GIS ring, giving a footprint conversion check rather than an as-built shell survey. They describe the saved footprint’s approximate 0.2 m exterior-wall bounds beyond the wire and broad agreement with the independent 2023 roof outline (G3, A1). The old flat site-paving rectangle was removed because the official footprint did not substantiate its extent; roads, sidewalks, site grade and planting belong to context, not this shell. Existing facade dimensions and bay widths were image-derived estimates. The existing asset/audit is not source truth and its modeled 117-ft penthouse, 120.7-ft elevator overrun, floor datums and material assignments remain unsupported or ambiguous. This dossier documents conflicts and does not authorize or make any model edit.
