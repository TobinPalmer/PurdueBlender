## Identity, boundary, and intended reference condition

Purdue BuildingShapes feature FID 799 names Pi Kappa Phi and gives 330 N Grant Street. The chapter's own history and Purdue BoilerLink independently identify the Omega chapter at that address. The county BZA packet identifies the site as Lots 6–8 of Roberts Addition and describes Grant Street as the west frontage; the west/front direction in this brief follows that record (G1, G2, H1, H2). The intended reference is the rebuilt house represented by the 2019 and undated chapter photographs, not the replacement design submitted with BZA-2225.

The existing GIS polygon is a stepped, nearly rectangular plan, rather than the idealized single rectangle. The audit's documented project conversion gives an approximate plan envelope of **16.49 × 23.21 m**, coordinate-derived area **341.97 m²**, and perimeter **78.8 m**; these are approximate campus-transform measurements, not a boundary survey (G1). The BZA staff report says the *proposed* replacement would shift toward Grant Street and stretch the width of the lot, distinguishing it from the existing structure, which it describes as a relatively small footprint centered between front and rear property lines (B1, pp. 8–9). Do not transfer the proposed plan footprint, setbacks, or covered parking onto the extant-house reference.

The existing record has a chronology that should not be flattened into one construction date. The 2026 BZA staff report says the fraternity house was built for the chapter in 1928 and calls the current structure Tudor-inspired; the chapter history says the house was rebuilt on the same lot after a June 11, 1987 fire (B1, pp. 8–9; H1). These statements can describe an original house followed by a rebuild, but do not establish which walls or details survived, the rebuild date, or how closely the post-fire house followed the earlier design. Current image evidence—not the pre-fire date alone—controls visible exterior form here.

In May 2026 the BZA approved six variances for a proposed teardown and replacement, including a proposed height of 72 ft 5 in. The approved variances are zoning relief only. They are not a demolition/building permit or evidence that the proposed building was built. The official minutes and later chapter posts document activity/events at 330 N Grant in August 2026; the chapter homepage/archive image was published in October 2026 but its filename says “2021-copy” and the capture date is not verified (B1–B2, H3–H4, V2). The latest exterior appearance is therefore not securely dated to October 2026. Model the documented rebuilt house condition unless a later verified project/status source resolves the replacement lifecycle.

## Materials, colors, and signature details

The photographed exterior is predominantly pale gray-to-warm gray ashlar-like masonry with irregular rectangular block joints, darker recessed joints, and selective larger blocks around openings and the entrance. The stone face appears to wrap gables, central raised block and projecting bays. V1 and V2 show this surface in different exposure; V3 shows winter light and snow reflection. The images support a masonry-like stone finish but do not establish natural stone versus manufactured veneer, quarry, finish, joint depth, bond specification, or calibrated color. Use only photographic color language; avoid reading the more golden V4 illumination as a distinct material.

Roof surfaces read as dark gray shingles; window and door frames read as dark brown/black. The paired projecting bay windows have dark frames and broad dark fascia beneath shallow hipped shingle caps. Main upper windows are rectangular and mostly single dark-framed units or paired units; exact frame/mullion configurations vary and should be checked against V2 crop-level detail before construction. The three central dormers have broad, nearly flat or segmental-looking tops rather than triangular mini-gables. Their exact section/profile is unresolved at current resolution.

Distinctive fixed features are: (1) two front gable pavilions, (2) paired shallow projecting bays, (3) three central dormers, (4) raised/corniced central entry block with a centered chapter crest, (5) the three-arch stone porch/arcade with deep shadowed entry, and (6) narrow ornamental dark grille panels at the arcade sides. The large freestanding Pi Kappa Phi monument in the foreground is site signage, not part of the building envelope. Landscaping and low retaining walls need a site-scope decision; no basis here for incorporating them into the building mass.

The coordinate ring supports approximate plan positioning and the outline metrics above. The official feature's `NUM_LEVELS=1` and `CountOfFLO=1` conflict with the two clearly visible occupied façade levels plus attic/gable openings; its other vertical-related fields do not define reliable eave or ridge heights. Existing audit analysis correctly avoids using those fields as height/area measurements (G1; `../../../../audit/pi_kappa_phi/build_evidence.json`).

The county staff report's existing-house description does not give height or level dimensions. Its 72 ft 5 in value applies only to the proposed building in the BZA case. No measured existing-house wall, eave, ridge, dormer, floor-to-floor, basement exposure, or total-height value was found. Aerial and photo perspective could constrain a future estimate, but they cannot supply a datum-tied elevation without a scale/control reference. ## Evidence-led exterior brief and discrepancy checklist

A future exterior pass should preserve the stepped GIS plan; establish a two-level stone-clad house with attic/dormer features; keep the west/front composition legible as two steep end gables framing a recessed/raised central block; retain the three central dormers, two shallow bay projections, centered crest, and **three** deep round arches; and articulate the strong front-to-flank setbacks visible in V1/V4. The current photo evidence does not support a complete all-face shell or precise dimensions.

The first-pass asset and audit evidence should be revisited against these findings before any separately authorized model task:

* Audit/grade imagery says the front asset is flatter and more regular than the photographed house; V4 makes depth articulation a source-backed constraint, not merely a stylistic preference.
* V2 shows a three-arch arcade; the earlier audit describes/model preview emphasizes paired entrance arches. Resolve this count in the next model review, retaining three arches if the photo evidence remains the chosen facade source.
* Four principal upper façade windows in the central raised block and two separate end pavilions are visible in V2; do not treat the number of dormers (three) as the number of central wall windows.
* The two projecting bays, narrow recesses, front wall plane changes, raised center cap, and individual gable transitions need depth, not painted lines on one flat plane.
* Existing GIS vertical/level fields cannot validate the candidate's estimated 9.4/14.0 m heights. Do not convert the proposed replacement's 72 ft 5 in variance into a dimension for this house.
