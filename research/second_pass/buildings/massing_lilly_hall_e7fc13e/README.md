# Lilly Hall of Life Sciences — exterior research pass (rank 59)

**Status: Prepared with major gaps; provisional. Research only.** This brief is for the extant Purdue GIS feature FID581 / LILY at 915 Mitch Daniels Blvd. It describes evidence available through 2026-10-30; the latest inspected outdoor street imagery is a single March 2026 camera station. No modeling, builder, placement, or master asset was changed.

## Identity, date, wings, and boundaries

Purdue GIS identifies FID581 as Lilly Hall of Life Sciences, LILY, Purdue-owned, 915 Mitch Daniels Blvd, and returns a closed 34-distinct-vertex exterior ring. A fresh 2026-10-30 EPSG:4326 query agrees with the 2026-10-26 EPSG:3857 snapshot vertex-for-vertex after Web Mercator inverse projection: 35 coordinates including closure and maximum corresponding-vertex residual about 0.000000011 m (floating-point transformation residual). The source polygon has no interior rings, which does not establish the absence of roofed courts, recesses, or light wells. Transformed to NAD83 / UTM zone 16N (EPSG:26916), its approximate plan area is 7,120.6 m² and bounds are about 137.9 m east–west by 110.1 m north–south. These are footprint constraints, not surveyed wall dimensions.

The Purdue Historic Facilities and Buildings record 122 gives a phased history: west wing construction began in 1951 and Agronomy moved in during 1955; the main entrance began in 1957; the 1958 South Wing and Small Animal Building were completed; Biology and Animal Sciences moved into the east wing in 1959; the name changed to Lilly Hall of Life Sciences in 1960; and a basement was constructed with remodeling in 1967. It lists Scholer Corporation as architect. This sequence supports distinct long-wing and entrance-block families, but it does not map every present polygon segment to a named phase or prove which portions were altered after 1967.

The archive separately identifies the Life Science Animal Building (LSA), formerly the Small Animal Building, at 190 S Russell Street (record 66; fresh GIS FID231). It lies along Lilly’s south side in the current footprints. The two GIS polygons share a near-contact and only about 0.43 m² of computed overlap after transformation to EPSG:26916; that small overlap is a GIS boundary artifact until field evidence shows otherwise. Do not merge the LSA into FID581. The Life Science Ranges and Life Science Plant and Soils Laboratory are also separately named related facilities in the archive, but their precise facade/connection relationship to Lilly was not mapped in this pass.

The historical image set is undated and cannot be silently treated as the current facade. The current exterior reference date is therefore **March 2026 imagery**, with chronology/current-change intervals unresolved. The 1967 basement/remodel is a documented change; no later exterior alteration history was located. The separate “3D Animal Phenotyping Facility” sign in the current panorama may identify the LSA or another neighbor, not FID581.

## Massing, roof, vertical dimensions, and material evidence

The historic record and views support a tall entrance block joined to elongated lower laboratory/classroom wings, with the South Wing completed in 1958 and a separately recorded Life Science Animal Building nearby. AR-787 shows the entrance block stepping above its wing; AR-791/792 show low, flat-looking roof edges behind parapet/cornice-like caps. These are two-dimensional silhouette observations only. No rooftop plan, current roof image from above, drainage/roof equipment view, measured parapet, roof slope, or evidence of courtyard voids was obtained. The single-ring GIS footprint has no hole, but hidden roofed recesses are unresolved.

Fresh GIS FID581 attributes report `CountOfFLO=6`, `Elevation=622.5`, `G=12`, `M=24`, `F01=36`, `F02=48`, `F03=60`, `R=82.2`, and `Extrution=28`; the layer does not publish units or the vertical datum for these fields. The historic narrative independently confirms a later basement. Existing audit evidence interprets `R=82.2` as feet (25.05 m) based on apparent 12-unit level increments; that remains a hypothesis, not measured height. `Elevation=622.5` and floor strings are not safe vertical controls until their field units and datum are confirmed. Do not infer six evenly spaced visible floors from `CountOfFLO=6`.

Materials are conflicting/incomplete. The archive database lists limestone but does not say which element. Its historic photos show dark, regular coursed masonry on the long walls and pale vertical panels/frames on the entrance block; grayscale prevents material/color certainty. The current panorama shows reddish masonry on some nearby wings and glass with brick piers in another direction, but target attribution is uncertain. The companion LSA record calls its material brick, not Lilly’s. No calibrated color, mortar bond, window-frame metal, glazing tint, roof membrane, soffit, or current stone/brick boundary is established.

## Evidence-led builder constraints and audit comparison

- Preserve the current FID581 outline as a plan-boundary constraint: the fresh ring matches the existing audit source after projection and the queue’s FID/location remains consistent. It is a GIS building boundary, not a measured facade survey.
- Keep the entrance block and at least two long wing families distinct in the research interpretation. The 1951 west-wing / 1957 entrance / 1958 south-wing / 1959 east-wing sequence is a construction chronology, not a complete mapping of current envelope phases.
- Historic photos support three-band ribbon-window walls, separate from the taller entrance block’s four vertical bay frames and segmented glazing. Do not use those images to assign compass directions, exact current counts, materials, or a current entry point.
- Keep the LSA (FID231) and uncertain phenotyping sign separate pending field confirmation; the tiny GIS polygon overlap is not proof of a shared shell.
- Existing audit asset is a 25.05456 m one-height shell with schematic window bays, a schematic entrance, and canopy. The prior audit notes the window rhythm and north-facing portal are schematic. The new evidence does not confirm that compass placement, present entrance, one uniform height, complete single-wing profile, or window modules. These are discrepancy items, not instructions to edit the asset during this research phase.
- GIS gives six floors and opaque level/roof fields; the 1967 basement history suggests the floor count/level schema needs reconciliation. Do not use 25.05 m or `Extrution=28` as a measured wall/roof height.
