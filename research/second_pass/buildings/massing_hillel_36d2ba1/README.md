## Identity, history, site, and footprint

Purdue GIS BuildingShapesZip4 layer 0 identifies FID 803 as **Hillel Foundation**, `SOHIL`, at **912 W. State St.** (current address: 912 Mitch Daniels Boulevard). The Hillel Spring 2023 cover says “same building, new address” and “Building Opened 1951.” The Indiana Historical Society collection guide independently says Purdue Hillel moved into a new building at 912 State Street in 1951; the 2019 county variance minutes also report that the building was built in 1951. These sources support the building’s institutional continuity and approximate opening year, not an exact completion date or an unchanged facade since 1951. [GIS-803] [HL-23] [IHS-ARCH] [BZA-19]

The 2019 Tippecanoe County sketch plan places the Hillel property at the **northeast corner of W. State Street and Waldron Street**. The variance minutes say its front faces Waldron Street. Treat this as the written front orientation; the newsletters’ exterior photographs are not compass-registered. The county drawing is sheet V101, a minor-subdivision sketch/replat at 1 inch = 20 feet with an aerial underlay. It is **not** an architectural floor plan, elevation, roof plan, or as-built survey. Its aerial is overprinted with parcel, setback, and utility data, so roof-edge extraction from it is approximate. [PLAN-19] [BZA-19]

The October 30, 2026 GIS query returned the same named feature and address, with one 12-distinct-vertex outer ring. Applying the project’s documented local equirectangular conversion to its WGS84 coordinates gives a polygon area of **610.95 m² (6,576 ft²)** and centroid **(-322.446 m, -330.527 m)** in project XY (X east, Y north). Its bounding rectangle is about **31.40 × 25.58 m**; the polygon is stepped rather than rectangular. The area is close to the county hearing’s approximate **6,500 ft²** existing-building figure, supporting the identity and scale match. This is a GIS planning outline, not a surveyed exterior wall line. The service’s `Shape__Area` is in its Web Mercator geometry and is not ground area. [GIS-803]

GIS `NUM_LEVELS=1` and `CountOfFLO=1` agree with the low single-storey appearance. `Elevation=622`, `Extrution=12`, and the elevation values on the 2019 monument table lack a common documented vertical datum/units; do not turn them into wall, eave, or ridge heights. GIS has no `BUILT` date, and its floorplan field is an inaccessible internal `SOHIL.html` path. [GIS-803] [PLAN-19]

### The 2019 addition remains unresolved

The county’s June and July 2019 records describe an **845 ft² proposed addition** to the existing building, primarily for kosher-kitchen storage and maintenance, expanding toward Waldron Street rather than State Street. The board approved all five requested variances. The sketch plan’s main purpose is subdivision/replat and site constraints; it does not provide architectural addition elevations or a completed footprint. The candidate FID 803 polygon’s area aligns with the hearing’s approximate existing area, but the GIS layer has no source date or as-built designation. The 2022 and 2023 Hillel images do not conclusively establish whether or how the proposal was built. Keep the addition’s construction and current boundary status open pending permits, a completion record, or measured current views. [BZA-19] [PLAN-19] [GIS-803] [HL-22] [HL-23]

## Signature details, massing, roof, and materials

**Massing.** Treat the building as a low, single-storey, stepped/winged plan within the FID 803 polygon. The official Spring 2022 photograph shows two connected perpendicular-looking facades around a corner and the 2019 aerial supports a non-simple plan, but the available exterior images do not separate every wing or identify a rear/service addition. The written county description makes the west/Waldron facade the “front”; the newsletter angle remains unlocated.

**West/Waldron face.** SV-17 and SV-23 show red-brown brick, a very broad dark horizontal fascia/eave band with shallow horizontal courses, isolated small vertical windows, and a multi-panel glazed entrance. A pale rectangular building plaque is attached to the wall near the entrance. A concrete accessible approach rises to the door; metal handrails flank the ramp, with a short stair run beside it. Planting beds and freestanding traffic/parking signs are site objects, not building geometry. The wall’s masonry bond, sill/head sections, brick size, fascia construction, and ramp dimensions are not measurable from these views.

**Newsletter-visible facade family.** HL-22 shows pale buff, block-like masonry (horizontal coursing is visible), tall glazing grouped between pale vertical piers, and a broad light-colored projecting eave. The underside exposes a repeated bracket/rafter rhythm. HL-23-E adds a recessed entrance with a pale block surround/side wall and small round soffit lights. These images may show different faces from the red-brown brick wall, or an unrecorded change in finish. Because the photo bearing and exposure date are unknown, neither explanation is established. Do not apply either observed finish uniformly to all sides.

**Roof.** Overall profile is low and nearly horizontal from street level, with a broad fascia/eave; the 2022 photograph exposes a pale roof edge and shallow visible roof plane. The 2017/2023 wall views show a dark layered fascia and a reddish roof surface behind it in places. Roof covering, slope direction, drainage, plan, hidden intersections, HVAC/vents, and the rectangular block-like projection visible in HL-22 remain unidentified. No overhead roof source or measured roof elevations were found. The exposed triangular-looking elements under the 2022 eave should be recorded as a repeated soffit detail, not assumed to be structural trusses.

**Material/color map (photographic, uncalibrated).**

- SV-17/SV-23 west wall: red-brown brick; dark brown/charcoal horizontal fascia; dark glazed entrance and small framed windows; pale entry plaque. The summer images are partly shaded and cannot establish exact color or brick/finish specification.
- HL-22 photo-relative walls: warm pale buff masonry/block with visible horizontal joints; light-colored projecting window piers/frames; reflective glazing; pale fascia/soffit; a dark freestanding Hillel sign. Direct sun and a printed-newsletter reproduction influence color.
- HL-23-E entrance: pale buff block/stone wall and light soffit in shade; small recessed downlights.
- Roof surface: pale/gray in HL-22 and red-brown behind the fascia in SV-23. It may vary by visible roof plane or be affected by orientation/light; no roofing material identification is verified.

## Comparison with the existing audit asset

The prior audit’s r2 asset and `source_record.json` are history about the model, not evidence for the real building. The asset uses a single light-buff facade scheme, a repeated multi-pane glazing rhythm, a broad shallow roof edge, and an illustrative 3.6576 m height. New Street View evidence adds a geographically identified west/Waldron wall with red-brown brick, long blank spans, sparse isolated windows, a distinct glazed entry, and an accessible approach. The mismatch can be explained by different facade families, different image dates, or an unverified alteration; source bearings do not let the 2022 photo assign its larger window wall to a compass side. The model’s uniform finish and any repeated opening layout around unseen faces remain unsupported. The 3.6576 m height, 0.7 m eave, roof pitch, and opening dimensions were explicitly placeholders/visual approximations in the prior audit and remain unverified.

## Evidence-led future-builder brief

Preserve the official stepped 12-vertex footprint as a planning constraint, not a surveyed wall line. Build a low one-storey volume with distinct facade families: the west/Waldron brick entrance wall has sparse openings and a deep dark fascia; a separate photo-relative wall has tall grouped glazing between pale piers, a masonry identity panel, and a broad light soffit. Keep the main entry recessed, retain its ramp/rail relationship and attached plaque/signage, and do not make the photographed window rhythm wrap every corner. The eave/bracket line affects the silhouette and shadow and needs geometry when its section is measured. Resolve the 2019 addition before splitting wings or choosing the current shell boundary. Do not invent the hidden wall openings, roof plan, rooftop equipment, or vertical dimensions.
