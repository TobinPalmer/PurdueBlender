## Identity and exterior condition

The target is the congregation’s current 460 Northwestern Avenue building, not the Gothic-looking church that appears in historical search results. ULu’s history says it moved to South Chauncey in January 1951 and returned to Northwestern Avenue on 2017-10-29; the old Chauncey building is a separate historic site. The current contact page places the sanctuary on the third floor and fellowship hall in the basement, with the main office accessible through Fuël Coffee and from the parking lot (U3–U4). Purdue Archives’ indexed Purdue Exponent Building record says ULu purchased the remainder of the Northwestern Avenue Exponent building in 2017 and describes the third-floor sanctuary and basement fellowship conversion (U6; direct catalog access presented a Cloudflare challenge during this pass). The architect’s page calls the current church a three-story former office building adapted by Keystone Architecture and notes offices, fellowship space and a public coffee shop (U7). These identity and use records agree on adaptive reuse; they do not supply dimensioned elevations.

The 2017 Area Plan Commission minutes identify Resolution PD 17-05 / 460 Northwestern PD Z-2675 and say approved preliminary plans allowed conversion of the existing Exponent building to church, office and limited commercial-retail uses. The minutes state that final detailed construction plans and plat were approved, creating a specific public-record lead for the drawings, but the inspected minutes themselves contain no facade elevations (U5). ULu’s history page includes an image of the current building; its image link was captured separately as U1. The historic South Chauncey chapel image and interior stained-glass gallery image were inspected and excluded from current exterior evidence (U4, U9).

## Materials and current-vs-historic evidence

U1 supports a current exterior dominated by dark red/brown brick, dark-framed reflective glazing, pale horizontal masonry or concrete-like bands, and dark shallow awnings. U2 supports a pale flat soffit inside a recessed brick entry and dark entry glazing. These are photographic material/color estimates without calibrated color or product specifications. Do not label the pale bands as limestone or cast stone without a specification. The lettering, welcome banner, Fuël branding, bikes, cars, people, poles and snow are not permanent wall materials or building geometry. U9 was inspected and is interior stained glass, so it provides no facade evidence.

The current building is an adapted office building, not the congregation’s former Gothic chapel. Historical chapel roof, stonework, tower and pointed arches do not belong to the present target. The current structure’s pre-2017 Exponent facade and exact adaptation changes remain unverified: the 2017 approval record documents conversion but not which openings, materials or signs changed. No source inspected here gives a post-renovation finish schedule.
