## Identity, parcel, and current reference phase

The stable asset is the **Union Club Hotel**, represented in Purdue GIS as **Purdue Memorial Union Club (PMUC), FID 663**, at 201 N Grant Street. It is the hotel wing/addition adjoining the separate Purdue Memorial Union (PMU), not the main PMU building (GIS FID 644). The official 2025 campus map labels PMU and PMUC separately. A retained official Events listing names “Purdue Memorial Union Club”; Marriott and Circle Design Group photographs place the “Union Club Hotel” sign on the same brick-and-stone building. The identity crosswalk is recorded in `sources/reviewed_identity.json`. A candidate-name search also surfaced Purdue Softball Clubhouse at the same similarity score; that candidate is rejected by the address, PMUC abbreviation, campus-map distinction, and hotel signage evidence.

The intended visible condition is the completed **2020 renovation**. Purdue News records reopening on August 5, 2020 after a yearlong $35 million renovation and describes preservation of original Purdue architectural character. The Marriott and Circle Design Group views are completed-project photography, not design renderings. The hotel originated as a 1929 addition to PMU. Purdue’s 2020 release describes a 1939 second wing and a five-story, 130-room north wing in 1955. A Purdue Events page gives a broader “1929–1953” phase range, while a 2024 Purdue Stories transcript says additions in 1939 and 1955. Preserve this date conflict rather than treating 1953 as a reconciled construction date. The hotel has later mid-1980s and turn-of-century renovations; exact exterior scope by phase is not documented here.

### Facade and opening schedule: what can be stated now

**Primary photographed frontage (Grant Street / likely east side; exact camera azimuth unreported).** Use the main entrance canopy as the start feature and move away along the wing visible to the right in MARR-EXT-01. The visible lower frontage is partly hidden by canopy, sign fascia, railings, raised planting and perspective. The principal entry is a deep, broad, gabled porte-cochère with a pale trim edge and dark underside. The photograph visibly shows brick-clad supports/piers and the recessed glazed door zone behind; exact support count, plan dimensions, door leaf arrangement, ramp/step configuration and canopy depth are not recoverable from the one oblique.

Immediately beside/behind the canopy is a taller red-brick entry block with a light-colored stone band and an upper rectangular-to-curved/angled bay. The photographed upper bay has paired tall multi-light openings, with arched heads on the prominent bay; a lower projecting pale-stone bay has vertically stacked rectangular windows. The source is too oblique to enumerate unseen returns or assign the visible bay unambiguously to one cardinal face. The long adjoining wing has repeated rectangular multi-light sash windows arranged in horizontal floor bands; brick piers separate openings, and stone lintels/sills or belt courses appear at selected levels. Gabled roof ends and projecting dormers interrupt the window rhythm. Tree cover, changing wing angles, the photo crop and low view angle prevent a full reproducible bay count or exact floor-by-floor schedule. Record this facade as a **partial visible-run schedule**, not a total.

### Signature details, materials, and massing

The recognizable sequence is: long red-brick hotel wing; repeated steep gabled roof sections with pale stone coping/edge trim; a taller, lighter stone-clad bay at the entrance; and a projecting gabled porte-cochère with hotel signage. The entry bay is the strongest silhouette break. In the photos the entry tower reads as a faceted or shallow-bay projection, with a brick upper mass, narrow pale horizontal courses, arched upper window heads, and a lower pale stone-clad projecting portion with rectangular windows. Exact plan polygon, stone profile, arch depth, coping projection, cornice and parapet profiles require a square-on close view or elevation. Do not add flags, freestanding lamps, bollards, traffic, seasonal lighting, or landscape to the architectural shell.

Photographs support **red/orange brick**, **light buff/gray stone trim and entry facing**, **dark gray slate-like roof tiles**, and **light-painted multi-light sash** as visual descriptions only. No specification, mortar/bond survey, stone identification, roof product, window material, or calibrated color source was found. Warm dusk lighting and sky reflections change apparent color. Compare MARR-EXT-01, MARR-EXT-02, and CDG-EXT-01 for material boundaries, not RGB values. The 2019 Purdue Board renovation document contains level plans but no verified exterior elevations or roof plan in the reviewed materials; it is not used to infer opening counts or roof geometry.

The 2018/2020 Purdue news releases and 2024 Purdue Stories transcript establish historical context, not exterior shape. In particular, broad Gothic Revival descriptions of PMU must not be transferred wholesale to the Union Club Hotel addition. The hotel's visible exterior is a composite of phased hotel wings and the renovated 2020 entry. Source chronology does not establish which visible gables or stone details belong to which original phase.

### LiDAR / height evidence

Collector retained the 2018-04-20 Indiana QL2 tile `in2018_30001885_12`, a clipped COPC extract and metadata under `sources/` and `data/lidar/`. This is a broad surface/vegetation/equipment mixture, not a measured roof point set or a building height. The tile acquisition predates the completed 2020 renovation; no roof-surface segmentation, eave/ridge extraction, or post-renovation height comparison is available. ## Evidence-led brief for a future exterior pass

1. Preserve the GIS shell's north–south elongated, stepped footprint, but verify wall/perimeter interpretation against a georegistered current orthophoto or survey.
2. Build from connected but visibly differentiated hotel wings with multiple pitched roof masses, not a single bar. Keep PMU as a separate adjacent asset and establish the actual join/shared-wall boundary before joining or overlapping assets.
3. Give the photographed principal entry a strong gabled porte-cochère, readable hotel sign fascia, brick supports, and a distinct taller masonry entry bay with light stone surfaces, paired arched upper openings and lower rectangular windows. Photo perspective is insufficient for dimensions or full bay counts.
4. The long photographed run has multi-light rectangular windows in repeated horizontal bands, brick piers, pale masonry courses, gables and dormers. Do not extend this schedule to the hidden/opposite faces by symmetry.
5. Treat brick, stone, sash and roof color/material as source-based but uncalibrated. Separate attached architecture from flags, lighting, planting and streetscape.
6. Keep floor heights, exact overall height, roof pitch/ridge positions, roof services, courtyard wall layout, service doors, and window counts unresolved until measured sources are secured.
