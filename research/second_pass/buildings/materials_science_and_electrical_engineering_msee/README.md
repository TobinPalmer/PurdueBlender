## Identity, limits and current condition

The target is Purdue GIS FID646, building code MSEE, address 501 Northwestern Avenue. Use the existing asset `modular/buildings/materials_science_and_electrical_engineering_msee.blend` and preserve its origin. The cached Purdue footprint has four corner vertices and a rotated rectangular outline. A local tangent conversion of the WGS84 ring at its mean latitude gives approximate edge lengths of 34.7 m and 75.0 m (area about 2,600 m²; perimeter about 219 m); these are GIS-derived horizontal constraints, not a field survey. The same feature reports `Shape__Area=4,494.1` and `Shape__Length=288.4` with `geometryProperties.units=esriMeters` despite its EPSG:4326 ring. Those service fields do not match the coordinates and remain unresolved; prefer the ring position/orientation, not the conflicting stored metrics. Other published GIS fields include `NUM_LEVELS=4`, `Extrution=36`, `Elevation=624.8` and `R=50.1`, with no documented vertical units or datum. Do not read the four-level count or undocumented GIS extrusion as measured height.

Purdue source dates conflict: the ECN history and Purdue Archives building record say MSEE opened/built in 1988, while Purdue's 2018 College of Engineering article says it was originally completed in 1989. Retain both claims pending construction records. The official archive record describes a two-story glass atrium and two pedestrian skyways connecting toward Electrical Engineering and Physics. The saved 2007 frontal image shows three visible above-grade facade bands below a shallow red roof; an official 2013 emergency plan describes sub-basement, basement, first, second and third floors plus attic equipment. These counts refer to different conventions and should remain separate until elevations or sections reconcile them. Purdue describes a broader student-centered renovation beginning in 2017, with the atrium work reopened in 2019; sources located so far do not establish exterior alterations.

FID646 is the building-shell reference, not a proof that the connecting skyways are MSEE-owned. Identify both attachments and their facade penetrations before any future model work. Keep approach paving, bollards, plantings and the mall outside the asset boundary.

## Materials and dimensional constraints

M01/M05 show a red/brown brick field with contrasting pale horizontal bands, dark metal-framed glazing, and a red tile-like roof. This is a photographic color/material description, not a calibrated palette or confirmed product specification. The Mall facade receives the strongest coverage; the other faces have no usable material comparison yet. M05's oblique composition supports the repeated upper rhythm and long shallow roof but provides no defensible metric facade dimensions.

The official GIS ring is a horizontal location, rotation and approximate dimension constraint only. Its cached elevation/extrusion/roof fields have undocumented meaning and must not be converted to building heights. Its stored area/perimeter are inconsistent with a local calculation from the ring. No current orthophoto was registered, and no LiDAR point cloud, roof plane, eave or ridge height was acquired in this checkpoint.

## Evidence-led construction brief for any future authorized pass

Preserve the low broad rectangular mass, shallow long red roof, brick exterior with strong pale horizontal courses, small repeated upper openings, and the central two-level glazed arch/entry on the Engineering Mall face. Keep the separate hidden-wing opening schedules unresolved. Represent skyways only after confirming their attachment and ownership. Do not use the 2014 “EE–MSEE Study” as MSEE evidence: the report's own scope states it is an interior renovation within the older Electrical Engineering building; the existing copy is indexed in the neighboring Electrical Engineering dossier.
