## 1. Identity, phases, plan and orientation

The live Purdue GIS feature queried 2026-10-30 identifies FID 197 as John W. Hicks Undergraduate Library. Its 20 unique perimeter vertices exactly match the retained 2026-10-22 Purdue GIS snapshot after reprojecting both rings to NAD83 / UTM zone 16N (EPSG:26916): IoU **1.0**, Hausdorff distance **0 m**. This establishes a data match, not survey accuracy. The outline is approximately **31.25 m east–west × 18.88 m north–south**, area **475.89 m²**, perimeter **120.88 m** in EPSG:26916. These are calculations from GIS plan geometry, not measured wall faces or gross floor area. `sources/geometry_summary.json` records the calculation, nearest mapped primary footprints, and comparisons.

The raw feature reports `Shape__Area=822.72` and `Shape__Length=158.88`, materially different from the projected polygon calculations. The layer says its geometry is in EPSG:4326 but does not explain these stored aggregate fields; use the independently reprojected plan measures as approximate constraints and retain the raw mismatch as a data-quality gap. The nearest other saved primary footprint is Stewart Center, about **8.84 m** edge-to-edge. A published campus-history account describes Hicks as an extension of Stewart Center, but this primary-footprint set does not capture any below-grade connector; no connector outline or section was found. The GIS polygons do not establish the extent of the underground library.

**Construction and naming phases.** Purdue Archives and campus records date completion/opening to **1982**; the archives authority record says the Hicks name was used from **1989**, while a secondary account gives **1990**. The live GIS `BUILT` and `OCCUPIED` milliseconds both decode to 1980-03-01 UTC, consistent with the construction-era archive records but not a documented opening date. Field definitions are absent, so those raw dates are not used as completion/name dates. Purdue documented a ground-floor renovation completed in **2013**. In 2025 the Board approved a **$2.1 million** exterior masonry project: mortar and sealant work plus metal coverings on four sloped masonry walls. The Board schedule anticipated construction May–August 2026; the state project document lists September 2026 as the occupancy/end date. These are planned dates, not proof of completion. The latest inspected exterior photographs predate this work, so the intended post-project appearance remains unverified.

The official GIS reports `NUM_LEVELS=3`, `CountOfFLO=3`, `Elevation=621.7`, `Extrution=8`, `B=-31`, `G=-17.4`, and `R=24`. It supplies no unit/field dictionary. A Purdue Libraries 2017 notice calls the basement one level below the ground/“underground” library; it does not define the complete current vertical arrangement. Do not interpret the GIS counts or values as three above-grade stories or calibrated elevations. Existing model basement extents and floor topology are not evidence of the real structure.

## 4. Facade families, signature details and materials

**Upper enclosure.** Both exterior images show a low red-to-brown brick upper volume. The west entrance photo shows dark glazing recessed into the brick wall. Brick bond, brick dimensions, joint color, frame material, frame divisions, glazing type and finish are not specified. Photo color is uncalibrated and changes with shade, foliage and exposure.

**Sloped walls.** The 2023 oblique and 2017 entrance image show broad sloping apron faces; the state project text calls the four faces sloped masonry walls and specifies mortar/sealant repair plus metal coverings. Their visible historic finish reads red-brown and brick-like in the photographs. No source provides a wall section, base/head detail, slope angle, or final 2026 cover color/profile. Do not depict proposed metal as installed without post-project verification.

**Entrance and openings.** The west entrance image supports a large shadowed glazing/door recess. Tree cover and deep shadow conceal the exact frame grid and number of door leaves. No separate source verifies a canopy, piers, bronze color, solar glass, lintels, sills or the entrance slab geometry. The asset’s detailed glazing/pier interpretation remains a model choice pending elevation evidence.

**Site and roof.** Campus imagery shows a planted/paved plaza around the footprint. A pale rectangular feature appears inside the GIS boundary in the overhead export, but its function is unknown. Aerial tone cannot identify a roof product. Treat walkways, trees, lawn and paving as site context; no source confirms the model’s gravel-ballasted roof, three pyramidal skylights, or the dimensions of any lightwell.

## 5. Dimensional, vertical and roof constraints

`geometry_summary.json` provides the approximate footprint dimensions and area in EPSG:26916 and the exact match to the retained official source. The source geometry does not provide exterior wall sections. The GIS has `Elevation=621.7` and `Extrution=8`, but meanings and units are unspecified; existing audit conversion of `Extrution` to a 2.438 m top edge is an inference, not an authoritative elevation.

Indiana 3DEP tile `in2018_30001885_12` (2018-04-20) is in NAD83(HARN) / Indiana West plus NAVD88 height, both in US survey feet. A 26,373-byte LAZ clip has 3,693 returns in its bounding rectangle, 2,950 inside FID 197; classes are 2,854 Class 1 and 839 Class 2. The nearby outside-ring Class 2 ground median is **621.66 ft** (427 returns). Within a one-meter-eroded footprint, Class 1 height deltas relative to that median have p05/p25/p50/p75/p95 of **1.02 / 4.20 / 4.23 / 4.26 / 4.75 m** (n=2,139). Class 1 is unclassified and can include walls, trees, roof and other returns. This pooled distribution is not a surveyed grade, upper wall top, roof elevation or wall slope; no point-class or plane segmentation is sufficient to establish them.

The planned 2026 repair concerns the four sloped masonry faces, not a documented roof replacement. It may change their finish and joints. No post-work image, closeout report or as-built section was found; use the post-project appearance as a specific unresolved condition rather than assuming the 2017/2023 finish remains current.

## 6. Evidence-led construction brief and prior-asset discrepancies

Evidence supports a largely below-grade library with a low brick enclosure and west entrance, surrounded by four sloped masonry faces. The approximate GIS outline constrains its principal plan footprint; photos support the warm brick/masonry family and a deeply recessed glazed entrance. One basement is reported below the ground/underground library. These sources do not support a complete opening array, exact basement topology, roof geometry or measured heights.

The prior asset’s upper perimeter matches the GIS outline closely, and its R2 audit independently reports an estimated lower slope scale and estimated wall rise. Preserve that distinction: an XY match does not validate heights, slope, room levels or roof equipment. The asset currently depicts three pyramidal skylights and a gravel roof deck; neither is confirmed by the inspected real-building views, and the overhead plaza image does not resolve them. Its west entrance glazing/pier interpretation, lower slab extents and room layout likewise need drawing-level evidence. Historical 2013 furniture images are not current layouts. No model, builder, placement or master asset was changed in this research pass.
