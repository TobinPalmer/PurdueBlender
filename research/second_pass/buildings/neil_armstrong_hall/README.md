# Neil Armstrong Hall of Engineering — bounded exterior research pass

**Queue rank:** 18 · **stable ID:** `neil_armstrong_hall` · **owner:** Rossen (unchanged)
**Official map match:** Purdue BuildingShapesZip4, layer 0, FID 642, `ARMS`
**Intended reference condition:** completed exterior, represented by 2012–2023 public imagery and user-supplied views of unknown capture date. This dossier is a source package with significant gaps; it is not a build-readiness decision.

## Finding in brief

Armstrong is a connected, multi-wing engineering hall organized around an atrium/courtyard, with a long Stadium Avenue bar and a conspicuous folded red metal roof/canopy. The north-west entrance is a tall glazed corner under a soaring, triangular prow. Brick and limestone-clad volumes step around the south courtyard; glass upper bands and white/light-metal roof edges mark several wings. The 2018 and 2023 nadir images agree on the overall roof composition, while ground and elevated oblique photographs establish only selected facades. Their visible aperture runs cannot safely be extrapolated to the obscured faces.

The evidence is strong for building identity, the NW entry silhouette, the south-court curtain wall, the west wing’s distinct upper glazing, broad roof plan, and the material families. It remains weak for structural wall boundaries, current east and south-east elevations, complete opening counts, exact floor/wing heights, canopy section/elevations, and the detailed relationship between building and adjacent campus structures. No publicly available dimensioned as-built plan/elevation was found in the reviewed source search. Keep those features unknown or explicitly approximate.

## Identity, map registration, and boundary

The queue’s `Neil Armstrong Hall` is Purdue’s official **Neil Armstrong Hall of Engineering** (`ARMS`, FID 642), 701 W Stadium Avenue. The identity matches the 2007 opening/occupation attributes in the cached GIS, project photographs, and Purdue’s naming/history pages. The Gateway to the Future arch and the Neil Armstrong sculpture are exterior/site features, not wings of the building. Nearby south-court roofs and connected-looking paths in aerial imagery include other buildings and must not be absorbed into the Armstrong shell.

Two retained Purdue GIS extracts were compared after projecting both rings to EPSG:26916: the 2026-10-28 project snapshot at `../../shared/official_purdue_buildings_wgs84_snapshot.json` (FID 642, EPSG:4326) and the 2026-10-24 feature snapshot at `../../../audit/complete_neil_armstrong/sources/gis_feature_642_20261024.json` (FID 642, EPSG:3857). Each has one 118-vertex ring. They are numerically the same boundary: polygon IoU 0.9999999998, centroid separation below 0.001 mm, Hausdorff separation about 0.00001 mm after reprojection. This is source-version agreement, not independent survey validation.

For the projected ring, the generalized mapped polygon is about **5,064 m²**, with **452 m** perimeter and an axis-aligned extent about **162 × 57 m**. This perimeter includes its many small bends and the outline encompasses the multi-part complex. ArcGIS `Shape__Area` (8,757) and `Shape__Length` (593.8) are stored in the source service’s Web Mercator units/geometry and must not be copied as square metres/metres. GIS position is a good identity/location anchor, but the feature is a building-map outline, not a surveyed wall, roof edge, footprint split, floor plan, or proof that every covered volume has a single construction phase.

The project’s local frame uses an approximate equirectangular transformation anchored at `(-109.0, 400.189331)`; the full formula and raster extent are in `audit/neil_armstrong_hall/second_pass/sources/na_ortho_2023_hr.json`. The orthophoto trace coordinates in `audit/neil_armstrong_hall/second_pass/ortho_trace_notes.md` are **approximate asset-local metres and raw elevated roof positions**. Relief, roof cantilevers and registration approximation shift these outlines relative to ground walls. They are not construction dimensions and should not be snapped onto the GIS ring as wall geometry.

The map inventory reports `NUM_LEVELS=6`, `CountOfFLO=6`, `Extrution=60`, `Elevation=628.1`, and floor-related fields. These values have no verified units/definition here and conflict with Purdue OEP’s public four-level directory (lower, grade, two upper levels) and FRP’s “three-story” structural description plus basement/partial sub-basement. Treat the floor-count fields as map inventory attributes, not an established six-storey exterior. The 53 ft / 16.15 m atrium figure below describes atrium height, not total building height.

## Plan, massing, and face-specific exterior schedule

Use **north = Stadium Avenue / primary campus approach** for the broad street facade; south is the planted inner-court side. “West wing” and “SE wing” below are descriptive spatial labels for this dossier, not confirmed construction package names. A georeferenced building outline contains one ring and does not encode these sub-volumes.

### Roof plan and connected masses

The two IndianaMap aerials, especially the 2023 high-resolution crop, show a continuous angular red perimeter canopy/roof with an open/light-colored center roof and projecting pointed ends. Its north and south long bands are nearly east–west; the west end folds to the high NW prow above the glazed arrival corner, while the east end tapers to a long point over the opposite entry/approach. A notch at the NW fold reveals separate red planes and a support/strut; ground photos confirm that this is a deep sculptural soffit, not a flat red roof decal. In the roof view, inner pale roof areas enclose a long bar, with a mechanical/truss zone at the east end. The west wing has a separate pale-edged roof; the SE wing has its own pale roof and mechanical elements.

The previous manual trace gives candidate local extents: red band roughly 5–7 m wide; north edge near y=20–25 m, from x≈−82 to +62 m; east point near (+89,−9) m; SW wing around x=−77…−37 m and y=−32…+8 m; SE wing around x=+34…+73 m, y≈−10…−31 m. These are rough image trace coordinates in the project frame. The 2018/2023 images disagree in apparent edge lean by about ±4 m, and aerial relief shifts the elevated canopy relative to the footprint. Treat these values as search/registration guides with multi-metre uncertainty, **not surveyed dimensions**.

The building reads as (1) long main bar beneath/within the canopy, (2) west wing with rounded upper-floor corner, (3) south courtyard/glazed atrium zone between wings, and (4) east / south-east volume. A fully closed walk-around has not been achieved. Do not infer a symmetric pair of wings from the canopy alone.

### North / Stadium Avenue elevation and NW arrival corner

**Evidence:** `JH-2016-01`, `JH-2016-03`, `JH-2016-04/05`, `DD-2012-22`, `SA-2018`, user `UP-DRONE`; aerial roof alignment from `IND-2018/2023`.

At the west end the red canopy descends to a sharply folded triangular prow and turns around an inward/re-entrant arrival corner. A multi-storey glazed atrium bay wraps this corner: one glazed plane faces the Stadium approach and the adjoining glass plane returns into the west wing; the entrance doors occupy the base under the red soffit. The steel/metal soffit has visible seams/panel joints and at least one central diagonal support/strut at the notch. The statue on its plinth stands outside the doors; treat it and its plinth as a separate campus object, not facade geometry. A broad brick wall immediately east of the glass carries the name “NEIL ARMSTRONG HALL OF ENGINEERING” at pedestrian/first-floor height and narrow pale horizontal bands. Above the brick is a light-framed horizontal window band set behind a projecting pale roof edge. Farther along the long street facade is a wide curtain-wall zone beneath the red eyebrow, transitioning to red brick with repeated, relatively narrow punched openings and pale lintels/bands. Tree crowns obscure much of the base and lower openings in the long views.

**Opening schedule:** entry corner: full-height glazed multi-bay corner, repeated vertical mullions and horizontal transoms across several levels; exact grid spacing/count is not measured. Door leaves at grade are visible but exact leaf count/width and sidelights are unresolved from available perspective. Brick/name panel: largely solid wall; inscription is one horizontal band of letters, exact letter relief/projection not documented. Upper window band: repeated vertical divisions, but total run count cannot be read consistently. Long north/street run: major curtain-wall bay near arrival then punched brick window run; source-era project description calls curtain-wall/window-wall systems, not an aperture schedule. No complete face-frontal photo exists in the retained, pose-registered set, so bay totals, floor-by-floor assignments and the transition station remain unknown.

### West wing: north face, west return, and southwest corner

**Evidence:** NW images as above; `DD-2012-24`; `JH-2016-02`; `IND-2018/2023`; user `UP-DRONE`.

At the north/arrival side, brick meets the glazed entrance corner; lettering appears on a broad brick panel. The west wing is a substantial rectangular lower brick volume. The SW view shows tall openings with pale limestone surrounds/reveals and a lower entrance reached by exterior steps. The upper storey is a visually separate rounded/curved glazed band wrapping the wing’s outer corner, capped by a broad pale overhang/parapet; the curved portion is clearest in `DD-2012-24` and high-angle user imagery. This upper band must not be flattened into a simple rectangular window strip without checking the corner curve. The south/east junction opens toward the planted court and glazed atrium; `JH-2016-02` shows the pale roof edge over upper glazing and the courtyard return.

**Opening schedule:** the SW-oblique photo establishes multiple tall, narrow limestone-framed openings on the lower brick wall and at least one stair/door entry; trees and foreshortening hide the full sequence. An upper glazed band contains repeated vertical frames beneath a projecting light roof edge; exact pane count is not safely countable from this angle. North-side name wall is a broad brick field with lettering and one pale horizontal band; the adjacent entrance glass has the multi-level grid described above. No face-frontal west photo or complete lower-level count is retained. Keep the unshown west-facing runs and any openings behind trees unresolved.

### South / inner courtyard and atrium elevation

**Evidence:** `JH-2016-02`; user `UP-REAR-1/2`; `IND-2018/2023`; source report of 53 ft atrium and curtain-wall/sunshade products.

The court is a real void between opposing occupied wings, with planting beds, trees, walks and raised edges. Do not fill it with the nearby red-tile roofs visible south of the mapped building. A tall glazed curtain wall bounds one side of the court and rises through several levels beneath the red roof/eyebrow. Repeated vertical mullions and multiple horizontal transom levels are visible; exterior horizontal sunshade blades project outward in repeated rows, attached at regular intervals to the facade. The user rear-2 view confirms blades and grade-level entrance doors at the curtain-wall base. The user rear-1 shows a distinct, full-height glass entrance centered along a brick-faced main-bar return, with a sloped red eyebrow overhead and a series of tall, individual openings in adjacent brick; pale horizontal masonry/limestone bands run between levels. These images establish that the rear is not uniformly glazed and that the entry is distinct from the curtain wall.

**Opening schedule:** curtain-wall run: continuous multi-storey glazed grid, at least three visible horizontal frame/transom lines plus roof-level head; multiple vertical bays, exact count and spacing unresolved. Sunshade system: horizontal blades in repeated tiers, projection and count hidden/foreshortened in the views; do not substitute arbitrary fin count. Rear entry: full-height glass group with grade door assembly; exact leaf and mullion count unknown. Brick return: repeated tall/narrow punched openings in a horizontal run, with pale lintels/bands; some portions are obscured by angle, winter/summer foliage and adjacent wings. There are no calibrated elevations or fully frontal photographs to establish aperture sizes, sill/head levels or total counts.

### East end and SE wing

**Evidence:** manual coverage notes from user-inspected Street View and ECN 2009 imagery plus roof-only `IND-2018/2023`; no currently retained frontal source suitable for aperture counts.

Prior audits report a smaller inward glazed entry/notch beneath the eastern red canopy point, an adjacent diagonal brick face with horizontal pale bands, and a south-east wing with brick lower walls, individual windows, a glazed upper band and light overhang. This is only a **provisional description**: direct view files and camera poses are not present in the current research source bundle; the two aerials only corroborate the roof plan, and the canopy can obscure the walls. The user-inspected Street View images were not retained due to Street View terms. The ECN archive is historical and unregistered. No reliable window count, entry width, elevation layout, service-door schedule or current-condition judgment is supportable here. Do not mirror west-wing details onto this volume.

## Signature details, materials, and color

The 2008-era Design & Build with Metal / Arconic-Reynobond project profile identifies RATIO Architects (design lead Thomas Cheesman), Shaffner Heaney as fabricator and Smoot Construction as GC. It describes the sculptural high cantilevered roof and eyebrow as **Mayflower Red Reynobond ACM** (about 40,000 sq ft), with canopy/vestibule ACM in **Sea Wolf** (about 20,000 sq ft), plus campus-matching brick and limestone. It reports a 53 ft atrium, a 200,000 sq ft building, $53M project cost and October 2007 completion. These are project-profile quantities/specifications, not dimensions for each exterior face. FRP identifies a cantilevered roof truss and a structural-steel penthouse; the exterior shape/position of those elements is not dimensioned on the public page.

Kawneer’s project page identifies 1600UT curtain wall and Versoleil sunshade/outrigger context. These product names establish a system family, not installed member depth, spacing, finish, or the precise exterior grid. Photographs support:

- **Red/copper-red folded ACM:** canopy and eyebrow surfaces, including a deep dark soffit that changes apparent hue with shadow. Preserve sharp tapered tips, folded planes, seams and the open notch; the low images show a dark underside while sun-facing panels appear brighter red.
- **Light gray / warm-white metal panels:** vestibule and parapet/roof edges, window-band caps, and separate west-wing overhang. `JH-2016-01`, `DD-2012-24`, `UP-DRONE` show these as distinct from the red panels.
- **Red-brown brick:** main bar and wing walls. `JH-2016-01/03`, `DD-2012-24`, `UP-REAR-1/2` show different apparent hue by sunlight and shade; sampled pixels in `color_samples.json` are photographic references only, not material calibration. Brick bond/joint scale and mortar color need closer source inspection before texturing.
- **Pale limestone / stone trim:** bands, opening surrounds, projecting sills/lintels and some base/entry edges, with visible reveal depth in `DD-2012-24` and rear courtyard views. Exact stone type and profile unverified.
- **Glazing and metal frames:** green/blue/gray reflections change by sky and angle. Mullions and transoms are visible; do not treat reflected sky color as glazing tint. The NW atrium has prominent vertical and horizontal framing; projecting horizontal sunshades form the south court’s defining depth pattern.

Entrance inscriptions, statue, plinth, courtyard paths, planters, trees, gateway arch, temporary 2019 banner, and furnishings are separate from the building shell. The 2019 banner is documented in 2018/2019-source context and should not be interpreted as a permanent wall opening or panel.

## Floors, structure, and height constraints

Purdue Office of Emergency Preparedness lists four accessible levels: lower, grade, and two upper floors. FRP’s structural description calls the building a three-story cast-in-place frame with basement and partial sub-basement, structural-steel mechanical penthouse, cantilevered roof truss, two atrium bridge levels, a raised tiered lecture room and three concrete stairs. Together these support a lower level and three occupied structural stories as a broad organization, but do not map each facade’s floor lines or resolve the GIS “six levels” value. FRP gives no coordinates or dimensions for bridges, stairs or penthouse; do not place these externally from that description alone.

The old Purdue forestry/3DEP NDHM file is 2017–2019 QL2 LiDAR-derived height-above-ground data, EPSG:2968, 5 ft native cells, US survey feet converted to metres, sampled at a 0.75 m local grid. It is coarse for narrow parapets, canopy edges and eaves. `audit/complete_neil_armstrong/roof_height_reconciliation_r8.json` reports only two cells above 22 m within the GIS outline, 23.009 and 23.131 m at local x≈−13.53 m, y≈5.75–7.27 m. These are resampled/localized surface returns, not a roof elevation or evidence for a large end penthouse. A prior review’s “21.6 m ceiling” refers to another coarse summary and does not reconcile with these two higher sampled cells; datum, raster/source window, grid registration and resampling must be made consistent before adopting a peak. Therefore **no total height, eave level, canopy point height, parapet or floor-to-floor dimension is established**. Prior model peak ~21.5 m is an inference, not a survey.

The Arconic 53 ft / 16.15 m atrium height is a published interior-volume constraint. It should not be converted directly into a four-level exterior height because parapets, floor/ground offsets, roof truss and raised spaces change the vertical relation. The 2018/2023 aerials constrain roof plan only. The official GIS `Elevation=628.1` is not an eave/roof height and has no confirmed vertical datum/meaning in this dossier.

## Discrepancy register against prior project evidence

1. **GIS outline vs real shell/roof:** current and older GIS rings are identical, yet both are generalized. The high-resolution orthophoto shows a continuous canopy extending beyond some lower roof masses; elevated roof edges and shadows cannot be wall boundaries. Do not extrude one ring as an all-level solid.
2. **One polygon vs connected volumes:** aerial and ground photos show bar, west and SE wing masses arranged around a court/atrium. The one-ring feature and exterior photographs do not identify all shared walls or construction separations.
3. **Roof position:** 2018 and 2023 imagery exhibit a several-metre apparent lean discrepancy. Relief is expected because the roof is elevated; the source transform is approximate. Previous trace positions cannot be treated as exact ground alignment.
4. **Height / penthouse:** red canopy has a high central/edge profile in low-angle photos; imagery does not calibrate it. Coarse NDHM samples conflict with a secondary “21.6 m ceiling” summary and localize two >22 m cells rather than broad end boxes. FRP confirms a penthouse exists but not its footprint. Exact height/location is unresolved.
5. **Level count:** GIS reports six; OEP gives four access levels and FRP describes three stories plus below-grade space. Use the latter as stronger public architectural/operational descriptions, while keeping floor-by-face assignments unresolved.
6. **Facade counts:** older audit notes and model review contain approximate counts/positions. They are hypotheses unless directly visible in the cited photograph; no photo provides a full, calibrated walk-around. The source images show exceptions (glazed court, broad upper bands, tall framed openings) that should not be collapsed into repetitive generic brick faces.
7. **Current condition:** public photos run through 2023, with user images supplied 2026 but undated. The 2019 Purdue banner is temporary; otherwise current changes have not been positively checked. Do not claim the facade is unchanged in 2026.

Earlier R12.9 model review and R1 audit are retained under `audit/neil_armstrong_hall_refine/` and `audit/neil_armstrong_hall/second_pass/`. They are useful for finding which questions were previously considered, but model geometry/renders are not physical evidence. In particular, the R12.9 reviewer passed that model narrowly at 8.0/10 while noting schematic wall geometry, estimated openings/roof, stale inventory count, and no current-master check. This new dossier makes no change to that score, asset, owner or placement.

## Builder-facing exterior brief (evidence bounds)

Preserve the striking long red folded canopy with projecting, tapered end points and deep visible soffit; a glass entrance/atrium corner sits under the high NW prow. Keep its adjacent brick name wall and upper light-framed glazing. Separate the west wing as a lower brick mass with a rounded upper glazed band and pale overhang. On the courtyard side, preserve the tall multi-storey curtain wall with repeated horizontal sunshade blades, its distinct full-height glass door group, and neighboring brick runs with pale horizontal bands and individually framed tall windows. Keep a distinct SE/east mass and its light roof edge only as a provisional massing cue until a direct elevation is available. Show the planted courtyard void and do not include neighboring red-roof buildings in the Armstrong shell.

Reliable constraints: mapped location and orientation; one complex outline as coarse plan anchor; canopy/wings/court arrangement from aerials; visual surface/material families and NW entry profile; four public access levels / three-story structural description; 53 ft atrium report. Weak/unsupported constraints: exact building wall line by wing; shell footprints and joints; all face opening counts and sizes; east/SE facade layout; level-to-opening correspondence; roof profile/heights, penthouse extent, staircase/bridge positions, and 2026 condition. Preserve those distinctions in any later modeling task.
