## Identity, era and limits

CL50 is the 1990 Class of 1950 Lecture Hall at 648 Oval Drive; its name honors the alumni class and does not date the building to 1950. Purdue’s 1989 Purdue News construction notice says the new hall would replace the front of Peirce Hall (the former Stanley Coulter Annex), connect to the newer rear Peirce portion and Psychological Sciences Building, and be complete by fall 1990. The notice page header reads March 5, 1989, while its URL filename and body’s “Friday (5/5)” date marker imply May 5; the publication date is unresolved. The notice describes a columned brick exterior with window walls looking toward Memorial Mall. Purdue Archives dates the cornerstone laying to Gala week in 1990 and the formal dedication to October 20, 1990. These sources establish the intended current-era identity and the south-facing Mall frontage; the early concept rendering shown in the 2025 Exponent story is explicitly not the completed design.

The historic database says CL50 is beside Stanley Coulter Hall and connected to Peirce and Psychological Sciences. The current Purdue GIS records separate footprints: CL50 FID 230 and Stanley Coulter FID 56 are about 9.4 m apart; Peirce FID 216 touches or shares a boundary with the CL50 outline; Psychological Sciences FID 239 is about 25.4 m away. Those polygon relationships do not identify enclosed links, bridges, shared walls or asset ownership. Do not merge neighboring halls into CL50. The old Physics Building/Peirce history is context for the site, not a surviving CL50 facade phase.

## Footprint, levels and dimensional evidence

The live ArcGIS query retrieved October 30, 2026 returns FID 230, abbreviation CL50, 29 ring positions including closure, source CRS EPSG:3857, `NUM_LEVELS=3`, `CountOfFLO=3`, `Extrution=36`, `Shape__Area=2642.6953125`, and `Shape__Length=200.36405166936964`. The ring is effectively identical to the repository’s cached Purdue footprint (UTM IoU 0.9999999997; maximum boundary deviation 0.00000001 m). Reprojected to EPSG:26916, the approximate ground-plan result is **1,528.545 m²**, **152.416 m perimeter**, and **43.287 × 37.292 m envelope**. These are calculations from the GIS polygon, not a measured wall survey. The larger stored shape area and perimeter are EPSG:3857 planar summaries and should not be read as ground-area/perimeter values at this latitude.

`Extrution=36` has no documented units or definition; `Elevation=623` is an attribute, not a verified building datum. The GIS says three levels, while archived descriptions call the glazed wall the second floor and place the commemorative statue on the second floor; the recent Exponent article calls the statue “top floor.” The exact correspondence among those labels, levels above grade, basement and roof remains unresolved. Purdue’s 1989 construction article lists a planned 474-seat room; the Archives page later describes 500 seats and the current Admissions page lists CL50 as the preferred 470-seat room. These are capacity records, not facade dimensions.

Indiana Statewide 3DEP LiDAR coverage is indexed to acquisition date **2018-04-20**. The retained point crop contains 6,604 returns in the official footprint bounding rectangle (6,203 within the polygon): classes 1, 2 and 7 counts are 6,259, 344 and 1. The compound CRS is NAD83(HARN) / Indiana West (US survey feet) plus NAVD88 height, geoid12B (US survey feet). Among 189 nearby class-2 ground returns outside the footprint, median elevation is 623.09 ftUS. Within a 1 m eroded footprint, class-1 unclassified returns have a 10.17 m median height difference above that baseline (p25–p75: 10.13–10.24 m). This dense, nearly level top-return cluster is consistent with a broad low roof surface, but class 1 can include walls, trees and other returns; it does not measure eave, parapet or ridge height. The 2018 cloud cannot establish later rooftop changes or facade openings.

The existing audit reports an 18.9 m saved mesh height. That value is unsupported by the available measured-elevation record and materially exceeds the LiDAR top-return indicator. Treat the height conflict as open until the 1990 as-built elevations or a vertical survey resolves the intended datum and roof edge. Do not copy either value into a new model as settled truth.

## Inspected exterior views and source-backed observations

Source IDs and provenance are in [`sources/second_pass_source_register.json`](sources/second_pass_source_register.json). The saved north-up Esri World Imagery crop is 1200 × 1200 px. A point query of Esri’s imagery-citation layer identifies the source at the site as Indiana GIO imagery dated **2023-02-11**, 0.0762 m source resolution and 0.15 m stated source accuracy. The returned image is sampled at about 0.244 m/px over its broad context extent. Tree shadows, the orthographic view and a white low-slope roof limit facade interpretation.

The strongest current street-level evidence is the south-front oblique published in *The Purdue Exponent* on June 12, 2025 (photographer credit: Quint Holguin; photo capture date and camera location are not provided). It shows the curved Memorial Mall frontage from a low sidewalk view: red-brown brick piers rise from pale square plinths to pale bearing/capital blocks; a continuous pale curved soffit/spandrel band runs behind them; a low curved brick parapet crowns a tall reflective glazed wall. The upper wall is divided by dark mullions into repeated large vertical panels and has a continuous opaque lower glazing/spandrel band. Beneath it is a darker brick ground-level wall with openings set back behind the piers. A glazed double-door recess is visible near one end of the photographed run, but the source does not register that end to a cardinal corner. Six piers are visible in the frame, one partly clipped; the full facade count is not established. Foliage clips the upper edge and daylight/reflections affect color.

Two additional, older exterior frames appear on slide 1 of the Slideshare student presentation “Beyond the Class of 1950 Lecture Hall” (uploaded by Vandita Churiwal; capture dates and camera positions not stated). They provide distinct but unregistered low obliques of the same general curved frontage. One is a wider, darker view with glazed upper wall, brick piers, pale capitals/bases and shadowed ground-level openings; the other more clearly shows the curved brick parapet, glazing, projecting pale band and repeated round brick columns. They support the depth sequence—plinth, shaft, capital/soffit, upper glazing and parapet—but are not independent north/east/west elevations. The Purdue Events Calendar image and the Purdue Libraries “Buildings and Campus Scenes” photo already retained in the earlier local audit are duplicates/near-duplicates of one oblique and do not count as extra viewpoints; neither has a supplied capture date.

An official Purdue e-Archives close photograph (PPBUC2501, original date 2007-09-12; source metadata retained, image available at the cited IIIF URL) resolves one signature detail at the southwest column: a pale cut-stone/plaque inset into the brick shaft/base assembly with “1992” carved in raised numerals. That visible date conflicts with the Archives’ 1990 cornerstone/dedication chronology. The image is a detail only and does not establish its exact height or overall facade placement beyond the “southwest column” description. Verify whether 1992 dates a later plaque or reflects a different construction milestone before depicting it as the building’s cornerstone date. Brick bond and mortar color are visible close-up; no specification or calibrated color target is available.

The north-up 2023 aerial shows a broad light-colored, mostly flat roof behind the curved south edge, several raised rectangular roof zones/screens, and adjacent roof/building masses along the north side. It confirms outline direction and general roof topology, but the image does not distinguish small equipment from skylight/monitor/roof-screen features or assign the north-side connectors. The 2024 Purdue Road School one-page room plan places auditorium CL50 224 amid rooms 102, 108, 113, 115, 121, 125 and 129 and shows stairs/door symbols; it has no scale, north arrow or facade detail, so it is not registered to the footprint and does not supply an exterior opening schedule.

## Materials and defining profiles

- **Brick:** red to dark red-brown brick is confirmed in the official 1989 description, 2025 south view and 2007 close detail. The 2025 lower wall reads darker than the exposed round piers because it is shaded; that is not evidence of a separate brick type. Bond, brick dimensions, joint profile and facade-to-facade variation remain unverified.
- **Piers:** the curved frontage uses large round brick shafts with pale stone/concrete-looking plinths and pronounced pale capitals or bearing blocks. The shafts support a continuous curving upper band; the columns are permanent architecture, not plaza furniture. Actual diameter, base profile, shaft taper, capital depth and spacing cannot be measured from the uncalibrated photos.
- **Window wall:** large reflective glass panels with dark metal mullions, a solid horizontal spandrel band and deep-looking reveals behind the pier line. Reflections dominate parts of the 2025 photo. Frame material, finish, glass tint and exact vertical divisions are unknown.
- **Curved crown/soffit:** low curved brick parapet above a pale continuous horizontal band/soffit at the top of the window wall. The photo shows projection and layered depth but not a measured section, roof coping or drainage edge.
- **Southwest marker:** the 2007 source shows the pale inset stone slab and “1992” numeral relief framed by brick courses. Preserve its documented location and date inscription, while leaving the chronology unresolved.
- **Roof:** the 2023 overhead image shows a large light, flat/low-slope roof with several raised rectangular zones; LiDAR supports a dominant approximately level top-return cluster. Roof membrane, parapet height, equipment, drainage, skylights and stepped roof levels are not verified.

## Builder-facing constraints and existing-evidence discrepancies

Use FID 230 as the current plan boundary and keep its southerly curved Mall frontage. Preserve the contrast between a heavy brick colonnade and tall upper window wall; keep the round piers, pale bases/capitals, continuous curved band and low parapet as real depth-bearing elements. The south photo is insufficient to extend its bay rhythm around the east/west returns or onto the north connector faces. Keep neighboring Peirce, Psychological Sciences and Stanley Coulter identities separate unless a dimensioned source resolves shared fabric.

Existing asset/audit facts to review against the real-building evidence (not use as source truth): the saved mesh is reported at 18.9 m despite the approximately 10.17 m LiDAR roof-return indicator; the prior asset audit treats the current GIS shell and roof bounds as unchanged but did not establish surveyed height; and a prior refinement counted seven image-visible shafts from a single reference and enlarged them by an estimated 20%. The new evidence confirms the pier-and-band design but does not verify seven as the full arc count or support the prior dimensional adjustment. Do not change the model during this research-only pass.

## Gaps and conflicts to carry forward

1. **East, west and north elevations; all floors:** no registered face-on exterior coverage or complete walkaround. Searches found the Purdue Libraries undated facade image, two unregistered student-deck views, a south-front 2025 Exponent view, an official 2007 close detail and a 2021 Purdue COVID-stories photo lead. The COVID submission’s page/image now returns 410 Gone; no image could be retained or inspected. Obtain current public street-level views or the 1990 as-built elevations. Missing faces prevent reliable wall, opening, connector and material schedules.
2. **South opening and support count:** photos establish the repeated colonnade and upper glazing, but endpoints, full pier count, mullion count, lower doors/windows, stair/accessible approach and floor alignment are hidden or perspective-compressed. A 2024 room plan is unscaled and unregistered. Resolve with a measured/registered elevation and current multi-position sidewalk views; these details control the building’s principal silhouette and bay rhythm.
3. **North connectors and shared boundaries:** archives/1989 notice name Peirce and Psychological Sciences connections; GIS shows Peirce touching CL50, but the Psych GIS polygon is approximately 25.4 m away. Search of public pages and footprint context did not reveal as-built link drawings. Obtain connector plans/sections and exterior photographs to determine whether enclosed bridges, lobbies or circulation are owned by CL50 or adjacent assets.
4. **Heights, floor datums and roof form:** GIS `NUM_LEVELS=3`, `CountOfFLO=3`, `Extrution=36` are not a vertical survey; historic/current sources use conflicting “second” and “top” floor descriptions; saved asset height is 18.9 m; 2018 class-1 returns show a strong 10.13–10.24 m middle spread above nearby ground, but class and vegetation limits prevent assigning that band as a surveyed parapet/eave. Obtain the 1990 as-built section/elevations or a current vertical survey to resolve total height, basement/level labels, roof screens, parapets and equipment.
5. **1990 vs. 1992 marker chronology:** 1990 cornerstone and dedication dates conflict with the “1992” inscription in the documented southwest-column photo. The open UA 117 folder is described as containing a dedication invitation and building information booklet, and UA 62 Series 3 Box 87 Folder 25 lists CL50 photos/interiors and alternate-design renderings from 1985–1991; neither was retrievable as a digitized source in this pass. Consult those records or the building file before assigning a built phase or plaque-installation date.
6. **Materials and current condition:** close/oblique photos do not reveal masonry bond, window-frame finish, exact stone/concrete products, current repairs or roof materials. Sources have varying daylight and reflections and no calibrated color. Obtain specification sheets/material schedule and neutral-light close views for each face.
