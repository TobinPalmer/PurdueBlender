## Identity, construction periods and current condition

This is a connected group of different wings, not one block with one repeated window grid. Purdue's older [department history](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ME/AboutUs/History) identifies the 1929 laboratory/central portion, 1932 main wing, 1942 southeast portion and 1948 connection. It records Gatewood completion in May 2011; the [2025 retrospective](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ME/News/2025/purdues-mechanical-engineering-building-represents-more-than-a-century-of-history) calls its opening 2012. Purdue's 2026 building information uses other construction-phase dates. Preserve these as differing completion/construction/opening descriptions, rather than forcing one date for every connected wing.

The 2025 retrospective reports extensive 2023–2025 renovation, including infrastructure and accessible entrances, while retaining the historic exterior. It is the relevant current-condition warning: old plans and photographs establish earlier geometry, not a 2025 as-built entrance layout. Keep the demolished nineteenth-century Heavilon tower out of this building; it is historical context, not an existing roof feature.

## Defining visual details

The close contemporary old-wing entrance in M01 is a priority detail: a broad pale-stone semicircular surround, carved floral/scroll bands at its spring points and upper segments, a central upper keystone field, a square relief crest above, inset gridded half-round glazing and engraved two-line name panel above rectangular glazed doors. The arch has genuine depth and layered profiles. A plain white half-ring would omit much of its visual identity.

The modern Gatewood portal in M02/M03 is different: simpler pale voussoir-like segments, a pronounced projecting keystone, horizontal spring-level cap/molding, squared piers and a deeply recessed door plane. A nearby stone panel reads 2011. Do not copy the ornate old entrance onto this newer portal, or transfer the old entrance's fanlight arrangement into the brick-filled upper recess of the modern arch.

Across M02's modern frontage, lower windows are narrow and vertically tall, main upper windows are larger multi-light rectangles and the top row is shorter. Each has its own stone/brick surround and sill treatment. The connecting old wing visible left has different bay spacing, mullion patterns and corner articulation. Brick pilaster-like vertical strips, patterned spandrels, pale belt courses and a dark projecting eave make the rhythm legible at campus scale. Count each wing and story independently from the original images; no complete bay schedule has yet been verified.

Older north/east references M05–M07 show a brick corner, patterned spandrels, a smaller entrance with rails, and an east glass-block opening above a stone-framed door. Treat these as dated features needing recent verification. The 2015 ground plan also distinguishes several connected wings; it is not evidence that proposed changes were built. [Existing ground plan, PDF page 43 / printed 42](sources/study_2015_pdf_page_43.png), [source provenance](sources/study_2015_provenance.json).

The overhead source shows multiple red/brown pitched perimeter roofs, darker flat/inset roof areas, a bright roof zone near the central junction, equipment and changes in roof level. Trace each region separately. Avoid a uniform roof extrusion, duplicated rooftop equipment or closing a visible lightwell without checking the point data.

For materials, compare daylight M01/M02/M03: variegated red-brown brick with lighter mortar, warm pale stone bands/portals, dark window frames, blue-gray reflective glazing, dark eave/gutter and red-brown roof sections. M02's shaded facade and M04's warm evening light differ strongly. No calibrated target or manufacturer specification was obtained; fixed RGB colors would be estimates. Match stone, mortar, old/new brick and metal separately, using several sun/shade positions and preserving geometric relief.

## Coverage gaps before a future model pass

The dossier has detailed old/new portals, historical north/east close views, several mall-side distances, two elevated sources and overhead/point-cloud evidence. It still needs **recent post-renovation north/east/south full elevations, rear/service and courtyard photographs, current roof mechanical views, and a complete per-wing opening schedule**. The older sources cannot resolve those changes. No measured camera poses, facade survey or certified eave/ridge dimensions were found.

Next research should compare each 2015 entrance against 2025–2026 images, trace wing-specific roof planes and floor offsets, then photograph near/upward corners and recesses from multiple positions. Keep the old wing, Gatewood, attached service volumes and the separate Railway asset distinct. Evidence gaps remain explicit; this dossier is not a complete second pass or an independent visual acceptance.
