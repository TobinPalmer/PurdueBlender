# Exterior research depth required before building

User clarification, October 29, 2026: research must be as thorough as the exterior agent would perform for its own accurate building model. A collection of links, broad descriptions, a photo quota, or a LiDAR download alone does not satisfy that requirement. Research remains the current phase; do not create geometry to fill evidence gaps.

## The deliverable is a usable building brief

Each building's dossier must explain how its actual exterior is organized, with source-linked observations specific enough for a future builder to lay out the model. Retain the original photographs, plans, imagery and point data with their provenance. Write what was learned from them rather than leaving the next builder to repeat the investigation. Scale effort to the building's architectural complexity and uncertainties; there is no fixed minimum image count or page count that establishes adequate research.

1. **Identity, reference date and boundaries.** Register the named building, footprint, orientation, wings, additions and connections. Distinguish neighboring assets and shared stairs, bridges, terraces or services. Identify the intended exterior condition and reconcile renovations, proposed designs, historical images and actual completed work. State unresolved ownership/identity conflicts explicitly.
2. **A real walk around the exterior through available evidence.** Seek every distinct outer and inner elevation, opposing obliques, all corners, entrances, recesses and service faces. Move along long facades to expose hidden bays. Seek near architectural details, medium views of bay relationships, distant silhouette views, low/upward angles, elevated obliques and overhead roof evidence. Vary positions, heights, elevations and distances where sources allow. Log actual camera positions/directions and dates when available; otherwise record unknowns. Multiple crops, rotations, search thumbnails or inaccessible image links do not establish new inspected viewpoints. Record tree, shadow and perspective limitations.
3. **An opening schedule for each face, wing and floor.** Identify a reproducible starting corner and direction. Record bay sequence/counts; windows, doors, arches, blank or infilled bays, louvers and vents; relative widths and spacing; sill/head relationships; sash and mullion divisions; recessed/protruding frames and reveals; and changes at corners or wing junctions. Link each observed run to its supporting image/plan location. Annotated copies or diagrams may help, but retain unaltered originals. Mark obscured runs unknown rather than mirroring a visible facade or guessing totals. A prose instruction to count windows later is unfinished research.
4. **Signature details with depth and profiles.** Investigate entrances, steps/ramps, columns, capitals, arches, pilasters, ornament, inscriptions, reliefs, cornices, eave brackets, dormers, finials, grilles, rainwater goods and other features affecting recognition and shadows. Record their placement, repetition, proportions, projection and visible cross-section/profile. Seek additional sources for details too small or hidden in the first photos. Separate permanently attached architecture from movable objects and campus landscaping.
5. **Roof, massing and dimensional constraints.** Explain wing heights, setbacks, floor changes, parapets, slopes, ridges, hips, valleys, dormers, courtyards/lightwells, lower roofs, equipment and connections. Cross-check plans, aerials and available elevation data. Where useful, inspect/segment actual point distributions and roof planes; a cloud crop or return percentile is not a measured eave/ridge height. Retain CRS, units, vertical datum, dates, point selection and conversion/calculation methods. Give measured or estimated dimensions only with their basis and uncertainty. Document missing constraints without fabricating precision.
6. **A facade-specific material and color map.** Identify brick/stone/precast/metal/roof/glass types, joints, bond/panel patterns, texture scale, trim boundaries, weathering and frame differences. Compare multiple real views in differing daylight and shade; distinguish material color from exposure, illumination and reflections. Record which source regions support each material. Seek specifications or product information when useful. Label photographic color estimates and never present uncalibrated RGB, roughness or material identities as measurements. Do not let historical sash, study renderings or sky reflections silently determine current materials.
7. **An evidence-led construction brief.** Summarize the building's major masses and level relationships, the distinct facade families and exceptions, the details that need geometry for depth/silhouette, and the source-backed constraints a builder must preserve. Include a discrepancy checklist against existing asset/audit evidence when available, without treating the base model as a source of real-building truth. No builders, generated geometry or model changes during this research-only phase.
8. **A specific gap and conflict register.** For every material unresolved feature, identify the affected face/level, why current evidence is inadequate, sources/approaches already tried, what would resolve it, and its effect on an accurate model. Seek alternatives such as Purdue facilities/department archives, architects, public project drawings, independent photographers, aerials and elevation datasets. Unavailable, blocked, old or proposed sources remain labeled. Search result snippets are leads, not inspected visual proof.

## Evidence traceability

Use stable source/view IDs or equally unambiguous image/page/panorama links in the schedules and feature descriptions. Record provider/photographer, original URL, capture date versus publication/retrieval date, source format/resolution and relevant limitations. Retain full camera metadata when supplied, the raw source and its hash. Describe dimensions as observed, measured, estimated, conflicting or unknown. No invented symmetry, hidden window counts, camera heights or roof equipment. Historic imagery remains useful when its limitations and changes are explicit.

## Research readiness and earlier dossiers

**Prepared with gaps is a provisional source-package status, not a declaration that the research is sufficient to build or achieve 9.9.** The existing dossiers need the same depth review as new work. Preserve their research history, then address their substantive gaps; do not advance solely because they already contain many files or a lengthy narrative.

Before declaring research sufficient for a model pass, inspect the saved sources and audit whether every major mass and facade has a source-backed layout, an opening schedule, defining detail coverage, material comparison and defensible dimensional constraints. List any remaining minor uncertainties separately from missing elevations or major architectural details. Material missing coverage, unresolved current-vs-historic conditions and unsupported opening layouts keep readiness unresolved. If a source cannot be obtained, document the concrete limitation and the further evidence needed; do not silently waive it or call a known gap verified. Research readiness does not itself authorize modeling, replace independent visual review, or alter the 9.9 threshold.
